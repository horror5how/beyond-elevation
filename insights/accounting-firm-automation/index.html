Accounting Firm Automation: the 9 Systems US Firms Connect First

Hayat Amin · Updated 2026-10-22

Accounting firm automation means practice management, the client portal, ledgers, bill pay, tax software and billing pass data to each other without retyping. The nine systems a US firm connects first, with every price read on the vendor's own page on 22 October 2026.

Accounting firm automation means the systems a firm already runs pass client data, documents, deadlines and bills to each other without a staff accountant retyping them. For a US firm the nine to connect first are practice management, the client portal, document capture, the client ledgers, bill pay, tax software, engagement and billing, the month end close, and a live screen for the partners. Each one below names the products, the published price and the handoff that usually breaks.

Hayat Amin has spent twenty years in technology and sold three companies as chief financial officer, so he reads an accounting firm the way a CFO client does: how fast the books close, what is unbilled and how many times the same client gets typed in. He now builds these connections inside companies himself, as a forward deployed engineer. Every price below was read on the vendor's own page on 22 October 2026, in US dollars, and where a vendor didn't show a price we say so.

which accounting firms get the most from this

Three symptoms qualify a firm. Someone copies a client's details from the CRM into the tax software, then into the billing tool, then into the portal. Staff spend February chasing the same missing 1099s by email, one client at a time. And the partners can't say how much work in progress is unbilled, or which preparer is at capacity, until someone builds a spreadsheet on Monday morning.

The people doing that retyping are expensive and scarce. The Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the median wage for accountants and auditors at $83,680 a year, or $40.23 an hour, in May 2025, across 1,595,200 jobs. It projects about 115,300 openings a year over the decade to 2035. A firm in New York or Chicago that can't hire its way out of tax season has to get hours back from the work it already does.

how we picked the nine

A system made the list if a firm of 5 to 50 people runs it every week, a named product does the job today, and the handoff around it is one we see done by hand. Prices come from each vendor's own page, read on 22 October 2026. If a page contradicts itself, we say so. Ignition showed no prices on its pricing page, TaxDome returned an error, and Dext's US pricing page didn't load for us, so none of the three has a figure here. Nobody paid to appear. Beyond Elevation appears once, near the end, with the affiliation stated.

1. practice management, the hub everything else plugs into

This is the system that holds the client list, the jobs, the deadlines and who owns each one. Karbon lists Team at $59 per user a month billed annually or $79 monthly, and Business at $89 annually or $99 monthly, with 4 or more users needed for monthly billing. Business adds automatic client reminders and task automation. Canopy lists Standard at $74 per user a month, Plus at $109 and Premium at $149, all billed annually, with Premium marked coming soon. Financial Cents lists Solo at $19 per user a month, Team at $49 annually or $69 monthly and Scale at $69 or $89, with a 5 user minimum on monthly billing.

Pick one and make it the record. The test is simple: when a new client signs, does their name, entity type and year end get typed once, into this system, and flow out to the rest? If it gets typed four times, that's the first join to build.

2. the client portal and document requests

Most of a preparer's February is chasing paper. Liscio lists File Intelligence at $19 per user a month and the Liscio Platform at $49, billed annually, with organizers and deliveries at $7 each, or $5 each in a bundle of up to 499. The Platform tier adds Outlook and Gmail integration, client requests with due dates, document collection and e-signature. Karbon, Canopy and Financial Cents all carry their own portal too, so check what you already pay for before adding another login for clients.

Here is the arithmetic, with our assumption stated. Say you send 300 organizers a season and staff spend 15 minutes chasing each one. That's 75 hours, about $3,017 at the $40.23 BLS median hourly wage. Automated reminders that stop when the document arrives remove most of that chasing. The join that matters is between the portal and practice management: when the last document lands, the job should move to "ready to prepare" without anyone touching it.

3. receipts and source documents into the ledger

For bookkeeping clients, someone has to get bills and receipts out of an inbox and into the books. Hubdoc lists $12 a month after a 30 day free trial and syncs to Xero and QuickBooks Online. Dext does the same job and is common in firms, but we couldn't read a US price on its own page this run, so ask for a quote. Wrong for you if your clients' spend already runs through a card or bill pay tool that codes it at source.

4. the client ledgers, and a deadline Intuit has set

Your bookkeeping clients live in QuickBooks Online or Xero. Intuit's accountant page says QuickBooks Online Accountant retires on 31 December 2026 and points firms to Intuit Accountant Suite, which it says needs no data migration. Intuit describes the Core version as having no recurring fee. Xero lists its US plans at $27 a month for Early, $59 for Growing and $97 for Established, with auto reconciliation included from Growing and a $5 add-on on Early.

If your firm runs on QuickBooks, the next ten weeks are a good moment to map every connection into it, because any integration that relies on the old accountant product needs checking before the switch. That's a two day job now and a lost January if nobody does it.

5. bills and payments for client accounts

BILL lists an accountant console at $49 a month, and its consumer plans at $49, $65 and $89 per user a month for Essentials, Team and Corporate. The detail that matters for automation is on the same page: Essentials syncs to QuickBooks and Xero by manual CSV import and export, while Team and Corporate get automatic two way sync. A firm running client bill pay on Essentials is doing a file transfer by hand that the next tier removes.

6. tax software

US firms mostly prepare in Drake, UltraTax CS, CCH Axcess, Lacerte or ProConnect. Drake is the one that published clear prices when we looked: Drake Tax Pro with unlimited returns at $3,145 until 30 November and $3,295 from 1 December, or Pay Per Return at $379.99 with 10 individual returns included and $49.99 per extra individual return. We didn't read prices for the other four this run.

The join to build runs two ways. Client details and prior year data should come into the tax software from practice management, not be retyped. And the return's status, filed, accepted or rejected, should go back to the job so the partner sees it without opening the tax program.

7. engagement letters and billing

Ignition sells proposals, engagement letters, payments and WIP billing, with a 14 day free trial, but its pricing page showed no figures when we read it. Karbon's Team plan and Canopy's Standard plan include billing and payments, so many firms can do this inside the hub. The link that breaks is between the signed engagement and the work: the letter says monthly bookkeeping plus a 1120S, and someone has to create those jobs and the recurring invoice by hand. Connected, the signature creates both.

8. the month end close across every client

A bookkeeping firm closes dozens of client files a month, each with its own checklist. Canopy lists Close Automation at $10 per connected client a month, billed annually, with the first 5 connections included. Financial Cents sells a Month End Close add-on, and its page gives two prices for it, $5 per client a month in one place and $4 in another, so confirm the figure on your quote. Either way, the work is the same: pull uncategorised transactions and unreconciled accounts from each client's ledger into one list, so staff work the exceptions instead of opening 40 files.

9. the partners' screen, live

Everything above feeds one view: unbilled work in progress by client, realisation on fixed fee work, jobs waiting on the client, returns filed this week against plan, capacity by preparer for the next 14 days, and cash collected against invoices out. In most firms that view is a spreadsheet someone builds at month end, which tells the partners in May that the April deadline was never staffed.

Practice management shows part of it. It can't show realisation against hours in the tax software, or cash from the payments tool, because that data sits in other systems. The work is in the joins, getting each one to agree on what a client, a job and an invoice are, so a managing partner can move a preparer or chase a debtor this week.

Frequently asked questions

What is automation in accounting?

It's software doing the repeatable steps between systems: documents into the ledger, bank lines matched to transactions, client details into the tax return, reminders sent until a document arrives. In a firm, the biggest wins are usually the handoffs between practice management, the portal and tax software, not the bookkeeping itself.

What does CPA firm automation cost?

The software is the smaller part. A practice management seat runs from $19 to $149 per user a month on the plans above, a portal from $19 to $49, and Hubdoc $12 a month. The cost that decides it is the build: who connects the systems and keeps them connected. At Beyond Elevation smaller firms start with a fixed $3,000 audit.

How is AI automation for accounting firms different?

Rules move data when the format is fixed. AI handles the messy step: reading a client's email, naming and filing a scanned document, drafting a reply to a request. Liscio's File Intelligence tier is sold on reading, classifying and naming documents. Keep a person reviewing anything that touches a filed return.

Will accounting be automated?

Parts of it already are, and the BLS still projects 5 percent growth for accountants and auditors from 2025 to 2035, with about 115,300 openings a year. The jobs that go are the retyping and the chasing. The judgement on a return, and the client conversation, stay with the accountant.

What is a good CPA firm tech stack?

One practice management hub as the record, a client portal, document capture into QuickBooks Online or Xero, your tax software, and a billing tool, all reading the same client list. Five to eight products is normal. The number matters less than whether a new client is typed once.

What happens to QuickBooks Online Accountant?

Intuit says it retires on 31 December 2026, and points firms to Intuit Accountant Suite, which it says needs no data migration and whose Core version has no recurring fee. Check every integration that touches your client files before then.
