AI Consulting for Small Businesses: What It Costs and When to Skip It

Hayat Amin · Updated 2026-09-19

AI consulting for small businesses means an outsider picks the processes worth automating and builds them inside your own systems. The published rates, the do it yourself floor, and the seven questions that separate a builder from a slide deck.

AI consulting for a small business is an outsider picking which of your processes are worth automating, then building them inside the systems you already run. Published rates sit between 100 and 500 dollars an hour, and a small fixed scope project between 5,000 and 25,000 dollars. Before you pay any of that, spend 20 dollars a month on tools and find out what actually breaks.

Hayat Amin has spent twenty years as a technology chief financial officer and sold three companies. He now builds this work inside small and mid sized companies himself instead of writing a report about it. That's why the sections below on price and handover are blunter than a consultancy's own page. Every number here was read on the publisher's own page on 19 September 2026, and where nobody publishes a number, we say so.

the four shapes it comes in, and what each one leaves you holding

"AI consulting" covers four different products at four different prices, and the confusion between them is where most of the money goes missing.

Advisory is a diagnosis. Somebody interviews your people for two weeks and hands back a roadmap. You own a document. Nothing in your company has changed, and the build is quoted separately.

Implementation is somebody building the thing. They get logins to your accounting package and your customer record, write the integration, and leave it running. You own working software.

Managed automation is implementation plus a monthly fee to keep it alive. It's the right shape when the process touches a system whose interface keeps changing. It's the wrong shape once the build is finished and stable. You'll pay rent on something that no longer needs a landlord.

Fractional is a person for one or two days a week, inside your business, on your side of the table. It costs the most per day and the least per outcome. That suits a company doing four or five automations rather than one.

Ask which of the four you're being sold before you discuss price. A firm that quotes an implementation number and delivers a roadmap has done nothing wrong contractually, and you've still spent 15,000 dollars on a PDF.

what it costs, and why nobody publishes a real rate card

We went looking for a survey of what small companies pay for this. There isn't one. What exists is a set of pricing guides published by agencies that also sell the service. Ask the fishmonger what fish costs. Here's what two of them publish, named so you can weigh them.

Groovy Web's rate guide, published 2 April 2026 and updated on 10 September, puts solo experts at 80 to 200 dollars an hour, boutique consultancies at 150 to 300, Big Four and enterprise firms at 300 to 600, and offshore agencies at 22 to 50. Its project bands run 20,000 to 50,000 dollars for a proof of concept, 50,000 to 100,000 for a production system. The firm says the numbers come from its own 200 plus client engagements. That is not a survey, and Groovy Web sells consulting.

Leanware's guide, updated 9 September 2026, splits by seniority instead: 100 to 150 dollars an hour for a junior, 150 to 300 for mid level, 300 to 500 for senior. Small fixed scope assessments at 5,000 to 25,000 dollars. Retainers from 2,000 to 5,000 dollars a month for 5 to 10 hours, and 5,000 to 15,000 for 10 to 25 hours. Same caveat: Leanware sells consulting too.

They agree on one band. A competent mid level builder costs 150 to 300 dollars an hour, and that's the only figure we'd treat as close to a market rate. Everything above 500 is a brand premium. Everything at 30 is somebody who won't be there in March.

the floor any quote gets measured against

You can't judge a 12,000 dollar quote without knowing what the same job costs if you do it yourself. So here's the floor, read on the vendors' own pricing pages this week.

Zapier's Free plan gives you 100 tasks a month and 400 agent activities. Professional starts at 19.99 dollars a month on annual billing for 750 tasks, or 29.99 paid monthly. Team starts at 69.00 dollars a month annually. n8n's Community Edition is free and open source if you host it yourself, and its Cloud Starter plan is 20 euros a month billed annually for 2,500 executions, with Pro at 50 euros. The model calls underneath are cheaper than people expect: Anthropic publishes Haiku 4.5 at 1 dollar per million input tokens and 5 dollars per million output, Sonnet 5 at 2 and 10.

So the honest floor for a first automation is about 20 dollars a month plus your own weekend. A 12,000 dollar project is 50 years of Zapier Professional at the annual price, which is our arithmetic off their published number, not theirs. That doesn't make 12,000 dollars wrong. It makes it a number you should be able to explain to your bookkeeper. The explanation has to be about the part the subscription can't do.

why companies pay for it anyway

Two pieces of evidence, both worth reading before you decide.

The first is MIT Media Lab's Project NANDA report, "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025", published July 2025 off a review of more than 300 publicly disclosed AI initiatives, 52 structured interviews and 153 survey responses from senior leaders. Against 30 to 40 billion dollars of enterprise spending, it found only 5 percent of custom enterprise AI tools reach production. The authors put the failure on adoption rather than on the models. Pilots stall because the tool doesn't retain feedback or adapt to how the work is actually done.

The second is the United States Census Bureau, which asks this question every fortnight. In its Business Trends and Outlook Survey covering 14 December 2025 to 3 May 2026, overall AI use hovered between 17 and 20 percent of firms, reaching 19.8 percent nationally by the May collection. Break it by headcount and the gap opens: fewer than 20 percent of firms with four or fewer employees use AI, against 32 percent of firms with 100 to 249 staff and 37 percent of firms with at least 250. Use rose among firms with 20 or more employees over that period and didn't move significantly below 20. By sector, information sits at 39.7 percent and finance and insurance at 33.9, while retail trade runs near 14.

A 9 person company isn't behind because its owner is slower. It's behind because nobody there has this as their job. That's the gap a consultant sells into. It's a real one.

seven questions that separate a builder from a slide deck

Ask these on the first call. The answers take ten minutes and save the 15,000 dollar version of finding out.

Which of my systems will you write into, and have you written into it before? Reading from a system is easy. Writing a credit note back into your accounting package is the part that goes wrong. Sometimes the honest answer is no.

What does the first deliverable look like, and what date does it run on my data? A good answer is a working thing inside four weeks. A bad answer is a workshop.

Where does the code live? It should be in a repository you control, under your accounts, with your API keys. If it lives in their workspace you've rented a process, not bought one.

What happens to this if I stop paying you? Ask it out loud and watch the face.

Show me the last one you built for a company my size. Not a logo wall. The process, the systems it joined, what it does now.

How will we know in 90 days whether it worked? Make them name the number before the build, because after the build everyone is generous.

What have you told a client not to automate? Anyone who has done ten of these has killed at least two. Somebody who has never said no has never been past the demo.

when to skip the consultant

Skip it when the process sits inside one system and the rule fits on a page. Chasing invoices in Xero, routing a form to the right inbox, drafting a quote from a template. That's a weekend and 20 dollars a month, and we wrote the steps in how to build AI agents for small businesses.

Skip it while you still want advice rather than a build. The Small Business Administration funds mentoring you can have for nothing. SCORE gives area specific advice at no cost by email, phone and video, plus training and webinars. Small Business Development Centres give individualised business advising and technical assistance. Women's Business Centers are free to low cost. None of them will write your integration, and all of them will tell you whether your idea is mad, which is worth having before you spend.

Skip it when the real problem is that a number lives in somebody's head. No model fixes that. The data build comes first. Any firm that agrees to automate around a missing system is taking your money.

what a fair engagement looks like

Fixed price for the first build, not hours. Hours reward slowness and neither side can forecast them.

One process, not a programme. The first engagement should be small enough that being wrong costs you a month, not a year.

Your accounts, your repository, your keys, from day one. Handover documented well enough that the next person can pick it up without ringing the last one.

A named number checked at 90 days, agreed before anybody starts. If it moved, do the next one. If it didn't, stop, and expect the firm to say so first.

On "near me": most of this work is done remotely, and the part that needs a room is the first week, when somebody sits next to the person doing the job and watches what they actually do. Buy the on site week. The rest can be anywhere.

Frequently asked questions

How much does AI consulting cost for a small business?

No survey exists, so the honest answer is a range from the guides that agencies publish themselves. Groovy Web's April 2026 guide puts solo experts at 80 to 200 dollars an hour and boutique firms at 150 to 300. Leanware's guide puts mid level consultants at 150 to 300 and small fixed scope projects at 5,000 to 25,000 dollars. Both firms sell consulting, so treat the numbers as asking prices. The band they agree on, 150 to 300 dollars an hour, is the closest thing to a market rate we could find.

How much do AI automation agencies charge?

Retainers are where agencies cluster. Leanware publishes 2,000 to 5,000 dollars a month for 5 to 10 hours of work and 5,000 to 15,000 for 10 to 25 hours. Before you sign one, check what the retainer covers once the build is finished, because maintenance on a stable automation is a few hours a quarter, not a few hours a week.

Is an AI automation agency worth it?

It's worth it when the job crosses systems and somebody has to write into one of them. It isn't when the process sits in a single tool you already pay for. MIT's Project NANDA found only 5 percent of custom enterprise AI tools reach production, off 30 to 40 billion dollars of spending, and the reason it gives is adoption rather than technology. Buy the agency that changes how the work is done, not the one that adds a tool beside it.

How much does AI cost?

The models are the cheap part. Anthropic publishes Haiku 4.5 at 1 dollar per million input tokens and 5 per million output, and Sonnet 5 at 2 and 10. The plumbing is the rest: Zapier Professional from 19.99 dollars a month on annual billing, n8n Cloud from 20 euros a month or free if you host the Community Edition yourself. A first automation running at low volume costs about 20 dollars a month. The cost you should actually budget for is the person who builds it.

Are there AI consulting companies for small business near me?

Plenty, and location matters less than it looks. The week that has to happen in a room is the first one, when somebody sits with the person doing the job and watches the real process rather than the described one. The build, the testing and the handover run fine remotely. Pay for the on site week, then judge on whether they've written into your kind of system before.

What does an AI implementation consultant do that a tool does not?

A tool waits for you to tell it the rule. An implementation consultant finds the rule, including the exceptions nobody has written down, then makes two systems that were never designed to meet agree on one record. That's the work. If a firm's first deliverable is a roadmap rather than something running on your data, they're selling advisory, whatever the invoice says.

Why do people search for AI consulting for small businesses on Reddit?

Because every page on the first results page is written by somebody selling the service. This one included. Go and read the threads. Then come back and ask the seven questions above. They're the ones the threads keep wishing they'd asked.
