AI for Construction Companies: the 7 Systems to Connect First, and What Each Join Removes

Hayat Amin · Updated 2026-10-13

AI for construction companies pays off at the joins between systems: takeoff to bid, Procore to the ERP, field time to job cost, lien waivers and certificates to the payment run, and a live WIP schedule. Every product and price below was read on the vendor's own page on 13 October 2026.

AI for construction companies pays off first at the joins between seven systems: the takeoff and the bid, the project platform and the accounting file, field time and payroll, subcontractor invoices and lien waivers, insurance certificates and the payment run, the daily log and the project record, and the owner's view of work in progress. Most contractors run every one of those and retype between them each week. Connect them and you see this month's over and under billing while the month is still open.

Hayat Amin has spent twenty years in technology and sold three companies as chief financial officer, so he knows that the work in progress schedule is the first page a buyer's accountant or a surety underwriter opens. He now builds this work inside companies himself, as a forward deployed engineer. Every product detail and price below was read on the vendor's own page on 13 October 2026, and where a vendor publishes no price, we say so.

which construction companies get the most from this

Three symptoms qualify a company. A project manager approves a subcontractor's pay application in the project platform, and someone in accounting keys the same invoice into the ERP. Foremen's hours reach payroll on paper or in a spreadsheet, so job cost for labour is a week behind the field. And the owner can't say which jobs are overbilled today without waiting for the month-end WIP schedule.

The people who'd normally absorb that retyping are the hardest to hire. The Associated General Contractors of America and NCCER surveyed 1,342 people at construction firms in July and August 2025. Among firms hiring for each role, 81 percent said superintendents were hard to find, 77 percent said estimating personnel and 76 percent said project managers. Among firms that employ craft workers directly, 88 percent had craft openings. Forty-five percent reported project delays caused by their own or their subcontractors' worker shortages. The same survey put the average construction wage at $39.69 an hour.

The field side is large and still growing. The Bureau of Labor Statistics counts about 1.7 million construction labourer and helper jobs in 2025, with a median wage of $46,680 in May 2025, and projects 6 percent growth from 2025 to 2035 with about 137,000 openings a year. An office that spends Monday rebuilding last week's numbers is capacity you can win back without hiring, whether you build in New York City, Dallas or Phoenix.

Contractors are warming to this. In the AGC survey, 45 percent of firms expect AI and robotics to have a positive effect on construction jobs by automating manual, error-prone tasks, up from 41 percent in 2024. Only 12 percent expect AI to cost jobs. The same survey has a new hiring problem in it: 47 percent of respondents listed AI specialist positions as hard to fill, up from 30 percent in 2024.

1. the takeoff and the bid

Estimating is where a general contractor or a specialty subcontractor spends hours before a job is even won, and it's the role 77 percent of hiring firms in the AGC survey struggle to fill. Togal.AI runs automated takeoffs on drawings, with chat prompts and symbol search. Its Growth plan is $299 a month per user, billed yearly, with unlimited automated takeoffs. Its Business plan, for teams of 4 or more users, is priced on request. A painting contractor quoted on Togal's own page says small projects now take 30 minutes to an hour and larger ones 2 to 3 hours, which is one customer's account and not a measurement.

Procore says one customer, Crest Industries, took its bidding process from 1 week to 20 minutes. That's Procore's claim about its own customer, and we read it that way. The join that matters comes after the takeoff. The quantities have to become the budget in the project platform without someone retyping them, or the estimate and the job cost report will never agree.

Wrong for you if your bids are mostly design and build negotiations, where the takeoff is a small part of the work.

2. the project platform and the accounting file

This is the join every contractor fights. Procore lists 14 ERP integrations on its support site: Acumatica, CMiC, MRI Platform X, NetSuite, QuickBooks Online, QuickBooks Desktop, Sage 100 Contractor, Sage 300 CRE, Sage Intacct, Xero, Yardi Voyager, Workday, Viewpoint Spectrum and Viewpoint Vista. The list is long. Read the limits before you count on any one of them.

The QuickBooks Online connector is a good example. Procore's own documentation says only QuickBooks Online Plus or Advanced are supported. Prime contracts and their change orders, prime contract invoice payments, budgets, subcontracts, purchase orders, commitment change orders and timecards don't sync at all. Vendors and subcontractor invoices have to be approved by an Accounting Approver, who is the only user allowed to start the export. Job costs come back once a day. If someone deletes a transaction in QuickBooks after it synced, Procore says the integration can't detect the deletion, and the cost stays on the project until Procore Support removes it.

The subcontract you signed lives in Procore, and the bills against it live in QuickBooks. Your committed cost, which is the number that tells you whether a job is about to lose money, sits half in each system. That gap is the build. If your stack is Foundation or Vista, Agave sells middleware that connects Procore and Autodesk Build to Foundation, Vista, Spectrum, Sage 300 CRE, Sage Intacct, CMiC, Acumatica, Deltek ComputerEase and both versions of QuickBooks.

3. field time, job cost and payroll

Labour is the cost that moves fastest on a job and reaches the books slowest. Raken sells daily reports, time cards and production tracking for the field. It lists accounting and payroll integrations with Sage 100 Contractor, Sage 300 CRE, Sage Intacct, Foundation, Deltek ComputerEase, QuickBooks Desktop, QuickBooks Online, Viewpoint Vista, Viewpoint Spectrum, Paychex, Points North and CMiC, and says they share "time data cost codes, projects, employees, and more". Raken publishes no price.

The number to build is labour hours against budget by cost code, daily, not at payroll close. A crew of 8 on 40 hour weeks running 10 percent over on a 6 week task is 192 extra hours, about $7,620 at the AGC average wage of $39.69, and you want to see it in week 1, not week 7. Check one thing first: whether a cost code changed in the ERP flows back to the field app, or whether the foreman keeps picking the old one.

4. subcontractor pay applications, lien waivers and payment

A general contractor's month end is mostly this. Subcontractors submit pay applications, a project manager approves them, someone chases the lien waiver, and accounting won't release the cheque until it's on file. Procore Pay gives subcontractors one place to invoice, tracks each invoice against a checklist of payment requirements, and collects signed unconditional lien waivers that are, in Procore's words, "securely staged and released at the time of payment". Approvals route through Procore Workflows with an audit trail. Procore publishes no price for it on that page.

The join to check is between the approved pay application and the ERP's accounts payable. If the waiver is in Procore Pay and the bill is in Sage 300 CRE, the person releasing payment needs to see both at once, or the chasing comes back.

5. insurance certificates and the payment run

An expired certificate of insurance on a subcontractor is a risk most contractors find out about after the work is done. Billy collects certificates from subcontractors and brokers, checks them against project-specific requirements with AI-assisted review, monitors expiry, and follows up automatically. It lists integrations with Procore, Sage 300, Sage Intacct, Autodesk, CMiC, Docusign, JD Edwards and Viewpoint Vista, and its FAQ says the Procore integration syncs vendors, commitments and project data.

The part that matters here is the payment hold. Billy says it can "flag non-compliant vendors before payment is released" through compliance gates connected to accounting. Billy publishes no price. Its own calculator assumes $600 a month as typical for 100 to 300 subcontractors, and says the real quote comes after a demo.

6. the daily log, RFIs and the project record

A project engineer looking for one answer in the spec book can lose a morning to it. Procore AI is the layer inside Procore that reads daily logs, submittals, drawings, RFIs, photos and financials. Procore names five agents. A Deep Search Agent answers questions across specs, drawings, RFIs and submittals with citations. A Submittal Review Agent checks submittals against specs and contracts. An RFI Agent drafts RFIs for review. A Daily Log Agent turns field photos, emails, video and voice into daily logs. A Contract Review Agent flags conflicts between contracts and drawings.

Procore's customer figures are large: Haskell went from 1 week to 10 minutes on submittal review cycles, and Bernards went from 1 hour to seconds searching a spec book. Those are Procore's claims about Procore's customers, and they're only as good as what goes in. A daily log built from a foreman's voice note is useful when the foreman says which cost code the crew was on.

7. the owner's screen: work in progress, live

Everything above feeds one view: contract value, cost to date, committed cost, estimated cost to complete, percent complete, billed to date, and the over or under billing that falls out of those, by job. Then cash collected against cash billed, and retention held on both sides. That's the WIP schedule, and in most contractors it's an Excel file the controller rebuilds after month end.

Adaptive calls itself "the AI-native project accounting platform for construction", aimed at companies with $5 million to $1 billion in revenue, and it runs beside your existing ERP. It sells agents for accounts payable, accounts receivable, WIP, lien waivers and certificates of insurance, and lists Sage, QuickBooks, Foundation, Acumatica and a two-way Procore integration. Pricing is scoped to your revenue band, month to month, with no published figure. Agave pitches the same thing: financial numbers that update as the field and the back office update, not once a month.

No single vendor sells the screen, because the committed cost is in Procore, the actuals are in the ERP, the labour is in the field app and the cash is at the bank. This is the join we build most. A month-end WIP tells you in November that a job slipped into underbilling in September. A live number tells you on the Tuesday, while you can still push a change order through, bill the stored materials or call the owner about the pay app.

what the contract you sign decides

Procore charges an upfront annual fee by product, based on your annual construction volume, with unlimited users and no published dollar figure. Its QuickBooks Online connector needs QuickBooks Online Plus or Advanced. Adaptive and Billy both price on request. Togal.AI is the only vendor on this list that publishes a per-user price. Budget for the sales calls, and ask each vendor in writing which records move, in which direction, and how often, before you sign. "Integrates with" on a logo page can mean a daily one-way export.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best AI for construction companies?

It depends on which join costs you most. For takeoffs, Togal.AI is $299 a month per user billed yearly. For document work inside the project, Procore AI has five named agents for search, submittals, RFIs, daily logs and contracts. For project accounting beside your ERP, Adaptive sells agents for AP, AR, WIP, lien waivers and certificates. Buy for the number you can't see today.

What AI tools do construction companies use?

Most contractors run Procore or Autodesk Build as the project platform. Around it sit an ERP such as Sage 300 CRE, Sage Intacct, Foundation, Viewpoint Vista, Viewpoint Spectrum or QuickBooks, a field app such as Raken, and tools for takeoff (Togal.AI), insurance certificates (Billy) and payment (Procore Pay). Middleware such as Agave joins the project platform to the ERP when the native connector doesn't cover what you need.

How to use AI in construction estimating?

Start with automated takeoff on your most repeated job type, check the quantities against a job you've already priced by hand, then make sure the output becomes the project budget without retyping. Estimators are hard to hire: 77 percent of firms seeking them in the AGC 2025 survey reported difficulty, so time back on takeoffs goes straight to bids.

How to use AI in construction accounting?

Use it where a person is moving the same record between two systems: subcontractor invoices from the project platform to accounts payable, field hours to job cost, lien waivers and certificates to the payment run, and the monthly WIP schedule. Read each connector's limits first. Procore's QuickBooks Online connector, for example, doesn't sync budgets, commitments or timecards.

Does Procore integrate with QuickBooks?

Yes. Procore lists connectors for both QuickBooks Online and QuickBooks Desktop. The Online connector supports QuickBooks Online Plus or Advanced only, links one QuickBooks company per Procore site, needs an Accounting Approver to start exports, and doesn't sync prime contracts, budgets, commitments or timecards.

Will AI replace construction workers?

No. The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects construction laborer and helper jobs growing 6 percent from 2025 to 2035, with about 137,000 openings a year, and 88 percent of firms that employ craft workers had craft openings in the AGC 2025 survey. Every product named here works on the office side. Only 12 percent of firms in that survey expect AI to cost jobs.
