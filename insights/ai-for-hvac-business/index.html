AI for HVAC Business: the 8 Systems to Connect First, and What Each Join Removes

Hayat Amin · Updated 2026-10-03

AI pays for itself in an HVAC business at the joins between systems: phone to dispatch board, field software to QuickBooks, call tracking to job revenue, and five more. Every named product and price below was read on the vendor's own page on 3 October 2026.

AI pays for itself in an HVAC business at the joins between eight systems, and the phone is the first one. Most owners run a phone line, a field service platform, an accounting file, a call tracker, a membership list, payroll, a parts supplier and their own spreadsheet, and someone in the office retypes between them every day. Connect those eight and the retyping stops. You can also see this week's booking rate, average ticket and unbilled work while the week is still running, instead of after month end.

Hayat Amin has spent twenty years in technology and sold three companies as chief financial officer, so he knows which of those numbers a buyer's accountant checks first. He now builds this work inside companies himself, as a forward deployed engineer. Every price and figure below was read on the publisher's own page on 3 October 2026, and where a vendor publishes no price, we say so.

which HVAC businesses get the most from this

Three symptoms qualify a company. A call that comes in after hours becomes a voicemail, then a note, then a job somebody types into the dispatch board the next morning. Invoices leave the field software and somebody re-keys or batches them into QuickBooks. And you can't tell a technician their average ticket for this week without exporting two reports and joining them in Excel.

Labour makes the case sharper in this trade than in most. The Bureau of Labor Statistics counted 440,900 heating, air conditioning and refrigeration mechanics and installer jobs in 2025, with a median wage of $61,010 a year in May 2025. It projects 11 percent growth from 2025 to 2035 and about 40,600 openings a year. Of those mechanics, 68 percent work for plumbing, heating and air conditioning contractors. Technician hours are already scarce, and an office that spends its morning retyping is the cheapest capacity you have left.

Adoption is still low, so the bar is low too. ServiceTitan published a survey of more than 1,000 contractors on 6 April 2026: about 60 percent said they're familiar with AI and more than 70 percent see it as relevant, yet only about 12 percent have embedded it in their workflows. That's a vendor survey of the vendor's market, and we read it that way. The gap is still large enough to matter in Dallas, Phoenix or on Long Island.

how we picked the eight

A join made the list if an owner needs the number behind it every week, a real named product sits on each side of it today, and connecting the two is where the work is. We name the products on both sides because that is the build. Prices come from each vendor's own page, read this week. Performance claims are attributed to the vendor that makes them, since nearly every figure in this market comes from a vendor about its own customers. Nobody paid to appear here. Beyond Elevation appears once, near the end, and we state the affiliation.

1. the phone line and the dispatch board

This is the join that loses the most money, because a missed call is a job that goes to the next contractor on Google. Four products now answer the phone and write the job straight into the board. Housecall Pro's CSR AI answers calls, chats and texts 24/7 and books jobs against your own schedule, services and pricing, and Housecall Pro says "Pros with CSR AI earn over 2x more revenue on average". It publishes no price for it. Jobber sells a Receptionist under Jobber AI that answers calls and texts, books work, takes requests and escalates urgent calls, with transcripts and recordings kept.

Avoca, which announced a $125 million Series B at a $1 billion valuation on its own site, is built for ServiceTitan shops and publishes a ServiceTitan certified integration. Its case study on Granite Comfort, a private equity backed HVAC and plumbing group with brands in Texas, Pennsylvania, Illinois, Georgia, North Carolina and New York, says nine call centres became one and nine after-hours vendors became zero. Avoca also says the pilot brand, Yost & Campbell, grew revenue 20 percent year over year. Avoca publishes no price either.

Wrong for you if the call doesn't land in the dispatch board as a booked job with the customer and the equipment attached. If someone still has to type it in at 8am, you've only moved the retyping.

2. the field platform and the accounting file

Every invoice a technician closes has to reach the general ledger, and in most shops it gets there in a batch someone posts by hand. ServiceTitan's Touchless Integration exports transactions as journal entries "approximately every two minutes", which removes the manual batching, posting and exporting. It works for QuickBooks Online and Sage Intacct only. Desktop and Xero users still batch.

On the smaller platforms the sync is in the plan, and it matters which plan. Jobber includes QuickBooks Online and Xero sync on all four plans, from Core at $29 a month billed annually. Housecall Pro lists QuickBooks Online sync among its Essentials features, at $149 a month billed annually for 5 users, or $189 billed monthly. FieldEdge includes QuickBooks Online and Desktop on all three of its plans and publishes no price. On the other side, QuickBooks Online Plus lists at $140 a month for 5 users and Advanced at $340 for 25, with 50 percent off for the first 3 months.

Check one thing before you trust any of these syncs: whether a job edited after it was invoiced writes back as a change or as a second invoice. That is where an HVAC general ledger quietly drifts away from the field platform.

3. call tracking and the revenue of the job

Most owners know what they spend on Google, Local Services Ads and direct mail. Few know which channel produced last Tuesday's system replacement. CallRail's ServiceTitan integration puts lead source and keyword detail inside ServiceTitan and lets you "generate true ROI based on the value of each job". CallRail's Lead Tracking plan is $50 a month with 5 numbers and 250 minutes, and Lead Conversion is $150 a month with call summaries and automatic conversion tagging.

The number to build is cost per booked job by channel, then revenue per channel 30 days later. Without the join you're paying for the channel that rings most, which isn't the same as the one that pays.

4. maintenance agreements and the schedule

Agreements are recurring revenue, and they fill the shoulder months in spring and autumn when the phone goes quiet. They are also the easiest number in the business to lose track of, because the agreement sits in one list and the visit sits on a schedule nobody built from it. ServiceTitan lists customizable memberships on its package page. Housecall Pro puts recurring service plans on MAX, its top plan, at $299 a month billed annually for 8 users. FieldEdge offers basic service agreements on Select and advanced ones from Premier.

ServiceTitan says its Atlas assistant, unveiled at Pantheon 2025, can take direct action, like "throttling marketing spend when schedules are full or triggering campaigns when demand is light". That is the decision this join supports. An owner who can see the spring tune-ups due against booked capacity can move a technician or a campaign two weeks earlier than one who finds out from the board.

5. technician time, commission and payroll

Spiffs, commission on sold equipment, overtime in a heatwave and a flat-rate bonus all have to reach payroll. In many shops that is a Monday spreadsheet. ServiceTitan lists payroll management and commission tracking on its package page, with configurable payroll in The Works. Housecall Pro's Essentials plan pays commission at different rates by item, service or job type. FieldEdge puts performance and commission on Premier and Elite, not on Select.

The join to get right is the one between the job's final invoice and the commission line. If a callback cancels a sale after payroll has run, someone corrects it by hand, and technicians remember the month you got it wrong.

6. purchasing, the truck stock and the books

Parts bought at the supply house counter on a company card, parts taken off the truck and parts billed to the customer are three different records. In most HVAC shops they meet once a month, if at all. ServiceTitan sells purchasing and inventory as part of its platform. FieldEdge's warehouse inventory management sits on its Elite plan and, in its own words, "is only available to those who use QB Desktop Enterprise with Advanced Inventory".

That footnote decides the build. If your accounting file is QuickBooks Online, warehouse inventory in FieldEdge isn't on the table, and the join between truck stock and cost of goods is a piece of work somebody has to write. The number it gives you is margin by job, with parts at what you paid for them.

7. the technician in the field and the job history

A technician standing at an old condenser needs the model, the last three visits and the manual, and usually phones the office for them. ServiceTitan's Atlas gives technicians job details, customer history, equipment documents, troubleshooting guidance and parts lookup by plain language query. Atlas is a core product for all ServiceTitan customers, but the equipment and parts features need a Field Pro subscription and the Atlas mobile app needs ServiceTitan Max. ServiceTitan's own help page warns that "Atlas may generate inaccurate, incomplete, or outdated responses".

Take the warning seriously. The useful version answers from your own job history, and the answer is only as good as the notes your technicians wrote on the last visit.

8. the owner's screen

Everything above feeds one view: calls answered and booked today, booking rate by channel, average ticket by technician this week, agreements due against capacity, jobs completed but not invoiced, and cash collected against cash billed. ServiceTitan's reporting covers closing rates and booked jobs inside its own walls. The cash figures sit in QuickBooks, the channel figures in CallRail and the agreement figures in a list, so no single vendor sells you that screen.

This is the join we build most. Month-end reporting tells you in August that June's booking rate fell. A live number tells you on the Tuesday, while you can still move a technician, call back the unbooked leads or pause the ad that isn't converting.

what the plan you're on decides

The integration you can build depends on the plan you pay for, and this catches owners out. Housecall Pro opens Zapier and its open API on MAX only, at $299 a month billed annually, with additional users at $35 a month each. Jobber includes Zapier on every plan, and its Plus plan at $399 a month billed annually adds an expert-led tour of its open API. ServiceTitan prices per technician across three packages, Starter, Essentials and The Works, and publishes no figure. FieldEdge also asks you to call. Budget for the sales calls, and ask each vendor whether the API you need is on the plan you're being quoted.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best AI for an HVAC business?

It depends on which join is costing you most. For missed calls, Avoca is built around ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro has CSR AI and Jobber has its Receptionist. None of the three publishes a price for its AI answering. For the books, ServiceTitan's Touchless Integration posts journal entries to QuickBooks Online or Sage Intacct about every two minutes. For marketing, CallRail starts at $50 a month and ties calls to job revenue inside ServiceTitan. Buy for the number you can't see today.

What AI tools do HVAC businesses use?

The field platform is usually ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber or FieldEdge, and that choice decides most of what else you can connect. Around it sit QuickBooks Online or Sage Intacct for the books, CallRail for call tracking, and an AI answering product such as Avoca, Housecall Pro CSR AI or Jobber's Receptionist. ServiceTitan's Atlas assistant is a core product for its customers, with the field features on a Field Pro subscription.

How do I use AI for my HVAC business?

Start where a person is retyping. The phone into the dispatch board comes first, then invoices into QuickBooks, then commission into payroll. ServiceTitan's April 2026 survey of more than 1,000 contractors found only about 12 percent had embedded AI in their workflows, so fixing one join puts you ahead of most of your market.

Is an AI answering service worth it for an HVAC business?

It is when the call becomes a booked job in your dispatch board without anyone touching it. The vendor figures are large: Housecall Pro says CSR AI users earn over 2x more revenue on average, and Avoca says its Yost & Campbell pilot grew revenue 20 percent year over year. Both are vendors describing their own customers, so treat them as ceilings and run your own month against your missed-call count.

Does ServiceTitan integrate with QuickBooks?

Yes. ServiceTitan integrates with QuickBooks Desktop, QuickBooks Online, Sage Intacct and Xero. Its Touchless Integration, which exports journal entries about every two minutes without manual batching, works with QuickBooks Online and Intacct only.

Will AI replace HVAC technicians?

No. The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects heating, air conditioning and refrigeration mechanic jobs growing 11 percent from 2025 to 2035, with about 40,600 openings a year. Every AI product named here works on the office side: calls, booking, invoicing, payroll and reporting. The technician at the condenser is the scarce resource, and the work above exists to give that person more billable hours.
