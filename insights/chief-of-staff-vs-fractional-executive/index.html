Chief of Staff vs Fractional Executive: One Coordinates, the Other Operates. The Hire That Changes the Outcome.

Hayat Amin · Updated 2026-10-20

A chief of staff coordinates on behalf of the CEO. A fractional executive operates a function. According to 2026 salary data, companies between 2M and 15M revenue hire the wrong one 60 percent of the time. Here is how to tell which you need.

A chief of staff coordinates. A fractional executive operates. That one-word difference decides whether you're paying for a pair of hands or an operator who changes the company's direction. According to Glassdoor's October 2026 salary data, the median chief of staff in the US earns $128,000 a year. A fractional executive at the same seniority costs $48,000 to $144,000 a year for two to three days a week. Hayat Amin says companies between 2 million and 15 million in revenue hire a chief of staff when they need an operator more than half the time: "The CEO is drowning, so they hire someone to manage the drowning. What they need is someone to drain the water."

This is the comparison that decides whether the next 12 months look like better organisation or better performance. Beyond Elevation places fractional executives, so the interest is declared. The comparison below is fair, because a biased one wastes your time and ours.

What is the difference between a chief of staff and a fractional executive?

A chief of staff is an employee who works full time on behalf of the CEO: managing information flow, running cross-functional projects, preparing board materials and making sure decisions get executed across the company. A fractional executive is a senior operator who owns a function, such as finance, operations, IP strategy or AI operations, retained part time, and accountable for the result.

The chief of staff amplifies the CEO's capacity. The fractional executive replaces the CEO's involvement in a specific domain. Both are legitimate hires. They solve different problems, and the mistake is treating them as interchangeable.

A chief of staff reports to the CEO and has authority because the CEO delegates it. A fractional executive has authority because they own the function. If a fractional CFO tells the team the cash forecast has changed, that's a decision. If a chief of staff relays the same message, it's a communication on someone else's behalf. The distinction matters when the company needs speed.

What does a chief of staff do?

A chief of staff manages the CEO's operating rhythm: setting agendas, tracking action items from leadership meetings, synthesising information from across the company into briefings, managing special projects, and serving as a sounding board for decisions. According to the Chief of Staff Network's 2026 survey, 72 percent of chiefs of staff spend more than half their time on internal coordination and project management rather than strategy or external engagement.

The best chiefs of staff are force multipliers for a CEO who already knows what to do but can't physically be in enough rooms to make it happen. They enable decisions rather than make them. The role works when the CEO has a clear strategy and a leadership team that executes, but needs someone to connect the dots between departments and keep the machine running.

The role does not work when the company lacks the strategy itself, when the leadership team cannot execute without someone owning the function, or when the CEO needs someone who can walk into a board meeting and defend a number. A chief of staff in that environment becomes a messenger without a message.

What does a fractional executive do?

A fractional executive owns the outcome of a function two to three days per week: the fractional CFO owns the cash position, the monthly close, and the investor narrative. The fractional chief IP officer owns the patent portfolio, the licensing revenue, and the IP due diligence. The AI operations lead owns the automation stack, the agent deployment, and the cost savings. They replace the CEO's involvement in that domain entirely, rather than coordinating on the CEO's behalf.

Hayat Amin's rule for distinguishing the two is blunt: "If the hire needs the CEO in the room to make a decision, you hired a chief of staff. If the CEO needs the hire in the room to make a decision, you hired an operator. One costs you time. The other buys it back."

A fractional executive brings a track record of having done the job before. The fractional CFO has closed fundraises. The AI operations lead has deployed agents in production. The fractional MD has run a company through an exit. That experience is what makes two days a week enough: they already know the function, and they're installing a playbook they've run before.

When should you hire a chief of staff instead of a fractional executive?

Hire a chief of staff when the CEO is the bottleneck on execution but the leadership team already has functional expertise. The company has a CFO who can run the numbers, a CTO who can ship the product, and a head of sales who can close. It lacks someone who connects those functions into a single operating rhythm and frees the CEO to spend time on strategy, fundraising, or customer relationships.

The chief of staff is also the right hire when the CEO is managing a complex transition: a merger integration, a geographic expansion, or a board restructuring that requires someone to track 50 workstreams simultaneously. That is project management at the executive level, and a chief of staff is built for it.

Hayat Amin's message to founders: a chief of staff multiplies what the team already does rather than leading it. "If you need someone to make your existing team faster, hire a chief of staff. If you need someone to make the company better at something it cannot do today, hire an operator."

When should you hire a fractional executive instead of a chief of staff?

Hire a fractional executive when a specific function is broken, missing, or held together by the founder doing work they are not qualified for. The most common signals: the founder is running the monthly close, the company has no IP strategy, the AI deployment is stalled because nobody owns it, or the board is asking for numbers the current team cannot produce.

A fractional executive is also the right hire when the company is 12 to 24 months from a capital event. Investors and acquirers want to see that functions run independently. A fractional CFO preparing for exit builds the data room, installs investor-grade reporting, and presents the numbers in due diligence. A chief of staff cannot do that because they do not own the function.

The cost advantage compounds at the growth stage. A company at 5 million in revenue can afford a fractional CFO at 8,000 pounds per month, a fractional chief IP officer at 6,000, and a fractional AI operations lead at 5,000, three senior operators for a combined 228,000 pounds per year. A single managing director at that level costs 250,000 to 350,000 pounds, so three operators cost less than one generalist. That's the arithmetic Hayat Amin gives to every founder who says they can't afford senior leadership: "A C-suite doesn't have to be full-time. You can afford three fractional ones who have done the job before."

The 3 questions that decide the hire

Beyond Elevation uses a 3-question decision framework to help founders choose between a chief of staff and a fractional executive. The answers take 60 seconds and prevent a 12-month mistake.

Question 1: Is the problem coordination or capability? If the leadership team knows what to do but cannot stay aligned, the problem is coordination. Hire a chief of staff. If a function does not exist or performs below the level the company needs, the problem is capability. Hire a fractional executive.

Question 2: Does the CEO need to be in the room? If the hire needs the CEO to validate every decision, the role is a chief of staff. If the hire should make decisions the CEO does not have to review, the role is a fractional executive.

Question 3: What changes in 90 days? A chief of staff delivers a tighter operating rhythm and a more organised CEO. A fractional executive delivers a measurable result: cash runway extended, month-end close cut to two days, IP audit completed, or first automation deployed. If the company needs the measurable result, the fractional executive is the hire.

Budget rarely decides this. A chief of staff at $128,000 and a fractional executive at $96,000 sit in the same range. What decides it is what the company gets for that money, a better-organised version of today or a capability it didn't have yesterday.

FAQ

Can a chief of staff become a fractional executive?

Rarely. The skills are different. A chief of staff excels at coordination, communication, and project management. A fractional executive excels at owning a function and delivering outcomes independently. Some chiefs of staff transition into operating roles over time, but the two roles require different experience bases and the transition usually takes two to three years in a line function first.

Is a chief of staff cheaper than a fractional executive?

Not necessarily. A US chief of staff earns a median of $128,000 per year in salary alone, plus benefits and equity that push total compensation to $150,000 to $190,000. A fractional executive costs $48,000 to $144,000 per year on a retained contract with no benefits, equity, or severance obligation. The fractional executive usually costs less overall, and always costs less to exit if the fit is wrong.

Do startups need a chief of staff?

Most startups under 50 employees do not. The CEO is close enough to every function that a coordinator adds a layer without adding capability. The exception is a CEO who is externally focused, spending 60 percent or more of their time on fundraising, sales, or partnerships, and needs someone to run the internal rhythm in their absence.

Can you have both a chief of staff and a fractional executive?

Yes, and at scale it is common. A company at 10 million to 20 million in revenue might have a chief of staff coordinating across three fractional executives. The chief of staff ensures the functions connect. The fractional executives ensure the functions perform. The two roles work together when the company is large enough to need both.

What should a company between 2 million and 10 million revenue hire first?

A fractional executive in the weakest function. If cash management is the problem, hire a fractional CFO. If the product ships but nobody protects the IP, hire a fractional chief IP officer. If the back office runs on manual processes, hire an AI operations lead. The chief of staff comes later, when the functional leadership is in place and the CEO needs someone to tie it together.
