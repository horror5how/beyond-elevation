Ecommerce Automation: the 9 Systems DTC Brands Connect First

Hayat Amin · Updated 2026-10-20

Ecommerce automation means your store, stock, shipping, support, returns, subscriptions and books pass data to each other without retyping. The nine systems a DTC brand connects first, with every price read on the vendor's own page on 20 October 2026.

Ecommerce automation means the systems behind your store pass orders, stock, refunds and cash to each other without someone retyping them. For a DTC brand the nine to connect first are Shopify itself, inventory, Amazon, shipping, the support inbox, returns, subscriptions and email, payouts into the books, and a live screen for the owner. Each one below names the product, the published price and the handoff that usually breaks.

Hayat Amin has spent twenty years in technology and sold three companies as chief financial officer, so he reads a DTC brand the way a buyer does: margin per order, cash sitting in stock and how many days it takes the books to agree with Shopify. He now builds these connections inside companies himself, as a forward deployed engineer. Every price below was read on the vendor's own page on 20 October 2026, in US dollars, and where a vendor hides its price we say so.

which DTC brands get the most from this

Three symptoms qualify a brand. Someone downloads a CSV from Shopify or Amazon every morning and pastes it into another system. Your support team answers "where's my order" by opening the store, the shipping tool and the carrier's site. And you can't say what this week's contribution margin was until the bookkeeper finishes the month, because payouts land as one lump that has to be picked apart.

The market is still growing under you. The Census Bureau's quarterly report, released on 18 August 2026, puts US retail e-commerce at $340.2 billion in the second quarter of 2026, seasonally adjusted. That's up 3.8 percent on the first quarter and 12.2 percent on a year earlier, and it's 17.1 percent of all retail sales. More orders through the same people is exactly where hand-carried data starts to cost you.

how we picked the nine

A system made the list if a DTC brand doing a few hundred to a few thousand orders a week runs it every day, a named product does the job today, and the handoff around it is one we see done by hand. Prices come from each vendor's own page, read on 20 October 2026. Vendor claims are labelled as the vendor's. Klaviyo and ShipBob publish no paid prices, and we say that where they come up. Nobody paid to appear. Beyond Elevation appears once, near the end, with the affiliation stated.

1. Shopify, and Shopify Flow inside it

Start with what you already pay for. Shopify lists Basic at $39 a month, Grow at $105 and Advanced at $399 billed monthly, or $29, $79 and $299 billed yearly, with Plus starting at $2,300 a month. Shopify Flow, its built-in automation, runs on Basic, Grow, Advanced and Plus. Flow works as trigger, condition, action, and Shopify says workflows ran 562 million times over Black Friday Cyber Monday.

Use Flow for the rules that live inside the store: tag a high-risk order and hold it, flag a VIP customer, alert the team when a SKU drops below a set level. It's the wrong tool when the rule needs data Shopify doesn't hold, like your Amazon stock or what the 3PL has received. That's where the joins below come in.

2. inventory across every channel

If you sell on your own site, Amazon and a wholesale account, one stock number has to feed all three, or you oversell on one and sit on dead stock on another. Cin7 Core lists Standard at $349 a month for 5 users, 6,000 sales orders a year and 2 ecommerce or app integrations, Pro at $599 for 10 users, 24,000 orders and 4 integrations, and Advanced at $1,199 for 15 users, 120,000 orders and 6. Omni is quoted on request.

Count your integrations before you pick a tier. A brand on Shopify, Amazon, ShipStation and QuickBooks has used Pro's allowance of 4 before adding returns or a wholesale portal. Wrong for you if you sell on one channel from one location, where Shopify's own inventory is enough.

3. Amazon as a second storefront

Amazon's Professional selling plan is $39.99 a month plus selling fees, and the Individual plan is $0.99 per item sold. For a DTC brand Amazon is usually a second set of orders, a second stock pool if you use FBA and a second payout with its own fees. The automation worth having writes Amazon orders and fees into the same inventory and accounting records as Shopify, so the margin per SKU you see is the blended one and not two numbers you add up by hand.

4. shipping and the warehouse

If you ship yourself, ShipStation lists Starter from $14.99 a month for 50 shipments, Standard from $29.99 for 50 and $449.99 for 10,000, and Premium from $349.99, all monthly, with annual billing advertised at 20 percent off. If you hand fulfilment to a 3PL, ShipBob publishes no prices at all: its page says every quote is customised and lists warehousing, pick and pack, returns and kitting as fees without amounts. It does say the software and its turnkey integrations, Shopify among them, cost nothing.

The join to check is the tracking number. It has to write back to the Shopify order the moment the label prints, so the customer's shipping email fires from the same record. Miss that, and the next system on the list pays for it.

5. the support inbox

"Where is my order" is a ticket every DTC brand gets, and it's the cheapest one to remove. Gorgias lists Basic at $50 a month billed annually for 300 tickets, Pro at $300 for 2,000 and Advanced at $750 for 5,000, with AI Agent interactions at $0.90 each on annual billing and $1.50 above a plan's included allowance. The same page also carries a second set of monthly prices, $77, $471 and $1,227, that don't match the first, so confirm the figure on your quote.

Do the arithmetic before you switch on an AI agent for tracking questions. Say you get 1,500 of them a month and each takes 3 minutes, which is our assumption and not a measured figure. That's 75 hours, about $1,615 at the $21.53 median hourly wage the Bureau of Labor Statistics gives for customer service representatives in May 2025. The AI agent answering those same 1,500 at $0.90 would cost $1,350. A tracking number that writes back to Shopify, and a shipping email that fires from it, removes the reason for the ticket instead of answering it. Fix the join first and point the AI agent at what's left.

6. returns and exchanges

Loop lists Essential from $155 a month and Advanced from $340, with a free Checkout+ option paid for by return shipping. Advanced includes Instant Exchange. The return itself is easy to buy. Three things have to happen after that, and that's where it breaks: the item goes back into sellable stock in the inventory system, the refund or store credit posts to the books, and the support ticket closes. If any of those happens by hand, your stock count and your margin are both wrong until someone catches it.

7. subscriptions and the email list

Recharge lists Starter at $99 a month plus 1.49 percent and 19 cents per transaction, and Plus at $499 a month plus 1.34 percent and 19 cents. Klaviyo's free plan covers up to 250 active profiles and 500 emails a month. Its paid prices weren't shown on the page we read, so check them against your own list size.

The join that pays here is between a failed subscription payment and the email that recovers it, and between a stockout and the subscription that's about to ship it. A brand that skips a subscriber's box because Cin7 knows the SKU is out, and emails them before the charge, keeps the customer and avoids a refund ticket.

8. payouts into the books

This is where the month end goes. Shopify and Amazon pay out in lumps net of fees, refunds and reserves, and someone has to split each payout into sales, fees, refunds and tax before the books agree with the store. A2X sells this as payout reconciliation and lists Shopify plans at $29 a month for 200 orders, $45 for 500, $79 for 2,000 and $115 for 5,000, posting into QuickBooks Online, Xero or NetSuite. A2X's page says prices rise on 14 November 2026, except Mini. QuickBooks Online lists Simple Start at $38 a month, Essentials at $85, Plus at $140 and Advanced at $340 at regular price.

Connect it so every payout lands in the books split by channel within a day of arriving. Then the margin question on the next item is answerable on Tuesday.

9. the owner's screen, live

Everything above feeds one view: orders today by channel, contribution margin this week after shipping, fees and refunds, stock cover in days for the top 20 SKUs, open tracking tickets, returns waiting to be restocked and cash due from Shopify and Amazon payouts. In most DTC brands that view is a month end spreadsheet, which tells you in November that a hero SKU went out of stock in September.

Shopify's own analytics shows the store in real time. It can't show you margin after the 3PL bill, Amazon fees and the refunds Loop processed, because that data sits in five other systems. The work is in the joins, getting each system to agree on what an order, a SKU and a refund are, so the owner can move ad spend or reorder stock before Friday.

Frequently asked questions

What is ecommerce automation?

It's software doing the handoffs between your store and the systems behind it: orders to the warehouse, tracking back to the customer, stock across channels, refunds into inventory and payouts into the books. The test is simple. If someone downloads a file from one system and uploads it to another every week, that step can be automated.

How to automate ecommerce business?

List every job someone does by hand for one week and the minutes each takes. Automate the largest first, usually tracking questions or payout reconciliation. Use Shopify Flow for rules inside the store, an integration tool or a built connection for anything that crosses systems, and keep a person on exceptions until the error rate is known.

What are the best ecommerce automation tools?

It depends on which job costs you most. For store rules, Shopify Flow on Basic and above. For stock across channels, Cin7 Core from $349 a month. For shipping, ShipStation from $14.99. For support, Gorgias from $50 billed annually. For payouts, A2X from $29. Buy for the hours you can measure today.

Does Shopify Flow cost money?

Shopify's Flow page lists no separate price and says Flow is available on the Basic, Grow, Advanced and Shopify Plus plans. Basic is $39 a month billed monthly or $29 billed yearly. The apps Flow triggers can carry their own fees.

What does an ecommerce automation agency do?

A good one maps where your team carries data by hand, connects the systems you already run and hands the connections over documented. Ask who owns the code and accounts at the end, and whether they'll show you the hours saved in your own numbers. At Beyond Elevation the client owns everything built: code, agents, accounts and docs.

Is ecommerce automation worth it?

It is when the hours are real and measured. At the $21.53 BLS median for customer service representatives, 75 hours a month of tracking questions costs about $1,615. If a tracking writeback removes most of them, it pays back in the first month. If you ship 50 orders a week, a spreadsheet and Shopify Flow may be all you need.
