Forward deployed engineer vs implementation consultant: which one a CEO should hire

Hayat Amin · Updated 2026-11-26

An implementation consultant sets up one product you've bought. A forward deployed engineer fixes a problem across every system it touches. Here is what each costs in the US, when each is the right hire, and a 5 step test you can run this week.

Hire an implementation consultant when you've bought one software product and need it set up the way the vendor intended. Hire a forward deployed engineer when the job crosses several systems, needs code written inside your stack, and is judged on a number moving in your business, not on a go-live date. Most AI work is the second kind.

Hayat Amin wrote this. He spent twenty years in technology as a CFO, sold three companies, and now works as a forward deployed engineer, which means he's sat on the buyer's side of both of these hires and now does the second job himself.

What an implementation consultant does

An implementation consultant takes a product you've already bought, like NetSuite, Salesforce, HubSpot or Workday, and configures it for your company. They map your process to the product's settings, load your data, test it before go-live and train your staff. Many work for the vendor or for one of its certified partners, so they know that one product very well.

The pay reflects a configuration role. PayScale puts the average implementation consultant salary in the US at $85,834 in 2026, with a range of $62k to $122k, from 851 salary profiles updated 26 August 2026. PayScale's own description of the job is installing and setting up software or hardware at client sites.

The limit is built into the job. Their brief ends where the product ends. If your invoices live in QuickBooks, your jobs in a field service tool and your customers in HubSpot, an implementation consultant for any one of them will make that one work. Nobody on their contract owns the gap between the three.

What a forward deployed engineer does

A forward deployed engineer sits inside your company, learns how the work gets done, and builds the software that fixes it, in your systems, against your real data. Paraform's October 2026 hiring guide draws the same line: an FDE ships production code inside the customer's environment, where an architect advises and designs.

The brief starts from a problem, not a product. "Our dispatchers retype 40 jobs a day from email into the scheduling tool" is an FDE brief. The answer might be an AI agent, a 200 line integration or a change to the process that needs no code at all. The FDE picks, builds it and stays until it holds up on live data.

Paraform puts FDE base salaries at $150,000 to $217,000, so a full time FDE costs roughly double the average implementation consultant on PayScale's figures. That gap is the price of someone who can write the code and run the room.

When the implementation consultant is the right call

I'd say this plainly, because an FDE would be the wrong hire here. If you've just signed for NetSuite and the job is to get your chart of accounts, approvals and reports set up by quarter end, hire the vendor's partner. Setting up that product is their whole job, they know the product's corners, and they cost less. An FDE on that job is paying senior rates for work that has a known answer.

The same goes for any single product rollout where the vendor's standard process fits your business with modest changes. That's the job implementation consultants are built for.

When you need a forward deployed engineer instead

Watch for these 5 signs:

  • The AI pilot worked in the demo and never reached production. That's covered in why most AI pilots fail.
  • Staff retype the same record into 2 or more systems every day.
  • You can't see a number like margin by job or cash this week until month end.
  • Each vendor says the problem is in the other vendor's system.
  • The board has asked for an AI plan and nobody in the building can write one that names systems, owners and a cost.

Every one of those lives between products. An implementation consultant can't fix it, because nobody pays them to look past their own product.

The fix you can start this week

You can work out which hire you need in about 3 working days, before you speak to anyone.

  1. Write the problem in one sentence with a number in it, like "our 4 coordinators spend 2 hours a day copying orders from email into the ERP". If the sentence names a product you haven't bought yet, you need an implementation consultant.
  2. List every system the problem touches and who holds the admin login for each. One system points to an implementation consultant. Three or more points to an FDE.
  3. Ask each vendor in that list one question in writing: "Will your implementation team build the connection to [the other system] and own it after go-live?" Most will say that's out of scope. Their answer tells you who owns the gap.
  4. Price both routes. A vendor partner will quote a fixed implementation fee. For the FDE route, compare a full time hire at $150,000 to $217,000 base plus a 25% recruiter fee and an 8 to 12 week search (Paraform) with a fractional or project engagement.
  5. Decide what "done" means before you sign. If it's a go-live date, either can be measured on it. If it's hours saved or a number on a dashboard, write that into the contract.

If step 2 gives you one system, hire the implementation consultant and you're done. If it gives you 3 or more, and step 3 tells you nobody owns the connections, the job needs a forward deployed engineer. Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a forward deployed engineer and an implementation consultant?

An implementation consultant configures one product you've bought. A forward deployed engineer starts from a problem in your operations and writes production code across every system it touches, then stays until it works on live data.

Is a forward deployed engineer just an implementation consultant?

No. The implementation consultant's job ends at go-live of one product. An FDE is measured on whether the business number moved, and Paraform describes the role as shipping production code inside the customer's environment.

How much does an implementation consultant make compared with a forward deployed engineer?

PayScale puts the US implementation consultant average at $85,834 in 2026. Paraform's October 2026 guide puts FDE base salaries at $150,000 to $217,000.

What does an implementation consultant do in software?
