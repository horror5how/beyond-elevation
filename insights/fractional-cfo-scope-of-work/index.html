How to Write a Fractional CFO Scope of Work (So Neither Side Wastes the First 90 Days)

Hayat Amin · Updated 2026-11-17

Most fractional CFO engagements fail because nobody wrote a proper scope of work. Here is the four-block framework that defines deliverables, boundaries, and success criteria before the first invoice.

Most fractional CFO engagements collapse before the invoice cycle even stabilises. The cause is never the CFO's ability. It is a scope of work that was never written — or one written so vaguely that neither side knows what success looks like after 90 days.

A fractional CFO scope of work is the document that defines exactly what your part-time finance executive will deliver, how often, at what cost, and against which milestones. According to a 2025 Deloitte CFO Signals survey, 62% of fractional executive engagements that end before six months cite unclear deliverables as the primary cause. Hayat Amin argues the real failure rate is closer to 80% — because most engagements that limp past six months are still running without a written scope. They just have not failed visibly yet.

The fix is not hiring a better CFO. The fix is writing a better scope.

Why Do Most Fractional CFO Engagements Fail Without a Proper Scope?

Most fractional CFO engagements fail without a scope because the CEO and the CFO start with incompatible assumptions about what the role covers — and those assumptions never surface until three months of billable hours have passed. Three patterns repeat across every failed engagement.

Pattern 1: The unlimited mandate. The scope says "provide strategic financial leadership." That means everything and nothing. The CFO touches AP, AR, board packs, fundraising, tax, and hiring — none deeply. The CEO sees breadth without depth and concludes the CFO is spread too thin. They are. The scope spread them.

Pattern 2: The bookkeeper upgrade. The CEO hires a fractional CFO expecting strategic capability but scopes the work as transaction processing — reconciliations, VAT returns, payroll oversight. A CFO doing bookkeeper work charges CFO rates. The CEO sees the invoice and fires the hire.

Pattern 3: The invisible output. The scope includes strategic items — financial modelling, scenario planning, cash flow forecasting — but defines no deliverables, no cadence, and no format. The CFO does the work. The CEO never sees it. Both parties assume the engagement is failing.

Hayat Amin says every failed fractional CFO engagement he has diagnosed across three prior exits and multiple fractional placements falls into one of these three buckets. The scope document is not a formality. It is the diagnostic that prevents all three.

What Should a Fractional CFO Scope of Work Include?

A fractional CFO scope of work should include four distinct blocks that separate the operational from the strategic and define measurable outputs for each. Hayat Amin's Scope Architecture Framework — the four-block model Beyond Elevation uses to structure every fractional CFO placement — eliminates the three failure modes above by forcing specificity before the first invoice.

Block 1: Operating rhythm. Define what the CFO does weekly, monthly, and quarterly. Weekly: cash position review, AP/AR oversight, exception flagging. Monthly: management accounts, variance analysis, board pack preparation. Quarterly: forecast refresh, budget-vs-actual review, tax planning checkpoint. This block answers the question "what happens on Tuesday" and prevents the unlimited mandate trap.

Block 2: Strategic deliverables. List every output by name, format, and deadline. Examples: 13-week rolling cash flow forecast updated weekly and shared as a live spreadsheet. Three-year financial model updated quarterly, with a named assumptions tab. Fundraising data room built by week eight and maintained continuously. Every deliverable has a name, a format, and a date. No "strategic financial leadership" — only named outputs with due dates.

Block 3: Boundaries. State what the fractional CFO does NOT do. This is the most important block and the one most scopes omit entirely. Does the CFO manage the bookkeeper or replace the bookkeeper? Does the CFO attend investor meetings or prepare materials for the CEO to present? Does the CFO handle HR, payroll, or compliance — or flag those as separate hires? Boundaries prevent scope creep, protect the engagement, and set realistic expectations for a part-time role that works 2 to 10 days per month.

Block 4: Success criteria. Define how both parties measure whether the engagement is working at 90 days. Specific, testable criteria only. Examples: month-end close completed within five working days. Cash forecast accuracy within 10% variance. Board pack delivered 48 hours before each board meeting. If the scope does not name what good looks like, neither side can call it.

How Do You Set Deliverables by Company Stage?

The right deliverables depend on company stage because a pre-revenue startup needs different finance outputs from a Series B company scaling toward profitability. A fractional CFO scope of work that ignores stage will over-deliver in some areas and under-deliver in the ones that actually matter.

Pre-revenue to Seed. Cash runway management is the entire job. The scope focuses on burn rate monitoring, 13-week cash forecast, investor update preparation, and cap table management. Beyond Elevation's pre-revenue scopes typically run two to three days per month at £4,000 to £6,000.

Series A (£1M to £5M revenue). The scope expands to management accounts, unit economics tracking, board reporting, and financial modelling for the next raise. The CFO builds the finance infrastructure that investors will scrutinise at the Series B data room stage. Typical scope: four to six days per month at £6,000 to £10,000.

Series B and beyond (£5M+ revenue). Full FP&A function design, departmental budgets, working capital optimisation, and exit-readiness preparation. The fractional CFO at this stage is building the finance function that will eventually need a full-time hire — and their scope should include a transition plan. Typical scope: six to ten days per month at £8,000 to £15,000.

What Should You Leave Out of the Scope?

The most common mistake in a fractional CFO scope of work is including tasks that belong to other roles — bookkeeping, HR administration, office management, legal compliance — because the CFO is the most senior operator the company has. A fractional CFO working four days a month cannot absorb an entire back office. The scope should block this from day one.

Hayat Amin's rule is blunt: if the task would still exist even if the company had zero investors, zero board meetings, and zero growth plans, it belongs to an operations hire — not the CFO. Payroll processing, benefits administration, office lease negotiation, and vendor management all get dumped into fractional CFO scopes because nobody else is doing them. The correct move is to scope those as separate line items and hire accordingly — or flag them as tasks the fractional CFO will document once and delegate permanently.

Leave out anything that requires daily presence. A fractional CFO works two to ten days per month. If a task requires daily attention — AP approvals, bank reconciliations, expense reviews — it belongs in the bookkeeper or finance manager scope, with the CFO reviewing outputs on their operating rhythm.

How Much Does a Fractional CFO Scope Engagement Cost?

A fractional CFO engagement scoped properly in 2026 costs between £4,000 and £15,000 per month, depending on company stage, scope complexity, and the CFO's seniority. According to Ramp's 2026 Fractional Finance Report, the median fractional CFO engagement in the UK runs £7,500 per month for five days of work. US-based engagements average $8,000 to $12,000 per month.

The scope document itself should state the monthly retainer, the included days, the day rate for additional days, and the payment terms. Hayat Amin reminds founders that a fractional CFO who refuses to put pricing in the scope document is a fractional CFO who plans to expand the scope without a conversation. Lock the number on paper before the engagement starts.

FAQ

How long should a fractional CFO scope of work be?

One to two pages. If the scope is longer than two pages, it is either a job description — which is a different document — or it includes operational detail that belongs in a process manual. The scope defines the what and when. The operating manual defines the how.

Should the scope include KPIs for the fractional CFO?

Yes. Every scope should name three to five measurable success criteria tied to the company's current priorities — month-end close speed, cash forecast accuracy, board pack delivery cadence, or fundraising milestone completion. KPIs without deadlines are aspirations. Aspirations do not survive invoice disputes.

How often should a fractional CFO scope be revised?

Quarterly. Company priorities shift, funding rounds close, and team hires change the finance function's shape. A scope written for a pre-revenue company does not serve a post-Series A one. Build a quarterly scope review into the engagement from day one.

Can a fractional CFO write their own scope of work?

They can draft it, but the CEO must own it. A scope written entirely by the CFO will scope in the work the CFO wants to do — not necessarily the work the company needs done. The best scopes are written collaboratively in a single working session, documented by the CFO, and signed off by the CEO.
