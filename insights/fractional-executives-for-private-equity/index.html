Private Equity Spends 18 Months Finding a Full-Time CFO. The Best Funds Fill the Seat in Two Weeks.

Hayat Amin · Updated 2026-09-30

Fractional executives are the fastest, lowest-risk way for private equity firms to plug C-suite gaps in portfolio companies. Here is how the best funds use them to accelerate value creation from day one.

Fractional executives are the fastest, lowest-risk way for private equity firms to plug C-suite gaps in portfolio companies, and the funds that use them consistently outperform the ones that wait to hire permanent staff. According to a 2025 AlixPartners study, PE-backed companies that installed interim or fractional leadership within 30 days of close achieved 23% higher EBITDA growth in the first year than those that waited for permanent hires.

Hayat Amin argues that private equity's biggest operational blind spot is the 6-to-18-month leadership vacuum between closing and filling the C-suite, not the deal thesis. "Every month without a CFO costs you a month of value creation runway," Amin says. "Fractional operators close that gap in weeks, not quarters."

Why do private equity firms use fractional executives?

PE firms use fractional executives because the traditional recruitment timeline doesn't match the value creation timeline. A buyout funded with debt creates immediate operational urgency, the debt service clock starts on day one, and EBITDA improvements need to be visible by the first board meeting. Hiring a permanent CFO, CTO or COO through a retained search takes 4 to 6 months on average, according to Spencer Stuart's 2025 executive hiring survey. A fractional executive can start within two weeks.

The mismatch is structural. PE operating partners know what needs to happen in the first 100 days: clean up the financials, install reporting that lenders trust, identify quick-win cost reductions, and build the operating playbook. They do not need someone who will stay five years. They need someone who has done this exact job at three other portfolio companies and can execute immediately.

Beyond Elevation places fractional executives into PE portfolio companies for exactly this reason. The operator has exited before. The playbook is proven. The clock starts ticking the week they arrive, not six months later when a recruiter finally finds someone.

Which C-suite roles should PE portfolio companies fill fractionally?

The three highest-ROI fractional hires for PE portfolio companies are CFO, Chief IP Officer, and AI Operations lead, in that order. Each one addresses a specific value creation lever that PE operating partners care about in the first 100 days.

Fractional CFO. The most common and most impactful first hire. A fractional CFO rebuilds the financial reporting stack, installs a 13-week cash flow forecast, cleans up the close process, and builds the reporting package lenders expect. Most PE portfolio companies inherit a finance function built for the prior owner's needs, not for a PE sponsor's reporting cadence. The fractional CFO fixes this in 60 to 90 days.

Fractional Chief IP Officer. Relevant when the portfolio company owns patents, trade secrets, or proprietary data that the deal thesis values but nobody is actively managing. A fractional Chief IP Officer audits the IP estate, identifies licensing revenue opportunities, and structures the portfolio for exit. Hayat Amin's Portfolio Company IP Triage, the framework Beyond Elevation runs on every PE engagement, maps every IP asset against three questions: does it generate revenue, does it block a competitor, and will an acquirer price it? Assets that fail all three get pruned. Assets that pass get structured for licensing or valuation uplift.

AI Operations lead. The newest addition to the fractional PE playbook, and the one moving the EBITDA needle most in 2026. An AI operations operator automates the back-office processes that PE firms historically threw headcount at, accounts payable, contract review, customer support triage, management reporting. A single fractional AI operator can eliminate 2 to 4 FTE equivalents in the first 90 days, which drops straight to EBITDA.

What does a fractional executive cost for a PE portfolio company?

Fractional executive rates for PE portfolio companies range from £3,000 to £12,000 per month, depending on the role, the complexity of the business, and the number of days per week. A typical engagement runs 2 to 3 days per week for 6 to 12 months. For comparison, a full-time CFO hire for a PE portfolio company commands a base salary of £150,000, £250,000 plus bonus, equity, and benefits, a total cost of £200,000, £350,000 per year before the recruiter's 25 to 30% fee.

The cost math is simple: one permanent executive's salary pays for three fractional operators working at the same time. For a PE firm managing 8 to 12 portfolio companies, the arbitrage compounds. Hayat Amin reminds operating partners that the real cost is not the fee, it is the 6-month vacancy. "A portfolio company without a CFO for six months after close is not saving £175,000 in salary. It is losing six months of value creation that compounds across the hold period."

How long does a fractional executive stay in a PE portfolio company?

The typical fractional engagement in a PE portfolio company runs 6 to 18 months, long enough to build the function, install the systems, and hire or promote a permanent replacement. The best fractional operators are explicit about this: they are building something that will outlast them.

The exit timeline depends on the role. A fractional CFO typically hands off to a permanent hire after 9 to 12 months, once the reporting stack, close process, and lender relationships are established. A fractional AI operations lead may stay 6 to 9 months until the automation stack is running and a junior operator can maintain it. A fractional Chief IP Officer may stay through exit preparation if the IP estate is a material driver of the deal thesis.

Hayat Amin says the handoff is the most underrated part of the fractional model: "Operating partners worry that fractional means temporary. It does, that's the point. A fractional executive who builds a function and then leaves a permanent team running it has delivered more value than a permanent hire who spends year one learning the business and year two asking for a bigger team."

What should operating partners look for when hiring a fractional executive?

The single most important criterion is PE-specific experience. A fractional CFO who has worked in PE-backed environments understands lender reporting cadences, covenant compliance, add-back calculations, and the operating rhythm that a PE sponsor expects. A fractional executive with only founder-led startup experience will struggle with the reporting precision and governance structure that PE requires.

Beyond that, operating partners should evaluate three things before signing:

Speed to first deliverable. The right fractional executive produces something tangible in the first two weeks, a financial diagnostic, an IP audit summary, a process map of what to automate. If they are still "getting up to speed" at week four, they are not fractional-grade.

A repeatable playbook. The best fractional executives have done this exact engagement 3 to 5 times before. They are not inventing a framework for your portfolio company, they are deploying one they have already tested. Ask for the playbook before you hire.

Clean exit history. Ask how their last three engagements ended. Did they hand off to a permanent hire? Did the function survive their departure? A fractional executive who cannot point to functions still running without them is a contractor, not an operator.

Hayat Amin argues that operating partners should interview fractional candidates the same way they evaluate portfolio company management: "Ask what they built, who runs it now, and what the numbers looked like six months after they left. If they cannot answer all three, they are not an operator."

PE funds are shifting to fractional models in 2026

The shift is accelerating because the economics have changed. Higher interest rates mean higher cost of carry, which means faster value creation is not optional, it is the difference between making fund returns and missing them. According to Bain's 2026 Global Private Equity Report, the median time from close to first EBITDA improvement shrunk from 18 months to 9 months between 2022 and 2025. That timeline demands operators who can start immediately.

The talent market is also working in favour of the fractional model. Senior operators who have exited or left permanent roles increasingly prefer portfolio work, shorter engagements, multiple companies, higher hourly rates. The supply of experienced fractional executives has grown 40% since 2023, according to Staffing Industry Analysts, making it easier for PE firms to find operators who have done the exact job before.

Every operator has prior PE-backed experience and a documented playbook for the first 100 days. The permanent C-suite is a legacy cost that PE can no longer afford to wait for.

FAQ

Can a fractional executive work across multiple portfolio companies simultaneously?

Yes. Most fractional executives work with 2 to 3 clients at once. For PE firms, this is an advantage, an operator working across three portfolio companies in similar industries develops pattern recognition faster than a permanent hire seeing one business. Beyond Elevation structures engagements at 2 to 3 days per week per company to ensure adequate depth without diluting focus.

Do fractional executives conflict with the existing management team?

Not when scoped correctly. The fractional executive fills a gap, a function that either does not exist or is not performing at the level PE requires. They are not competing with existing management. They are building something the company does not have. Clear mandate definition and operating partner alignment before day one eliminates friction.

How do fractional executives handle confidentiality across competing portfolio companies?

Standard fractional engagement agreements include non-compete and confidentiality provisions that prevent an operator from serving direct competitors simultaneously. PE firms should require these provisions and verify that the fractional executive's current client roster does not create conflicts before engagement.

Is a fractional executive right for every portfolio company?

No. Portfolio companies with strong existing management teams that just need board-level guidance are better served by advisory arrangements. The fractional model works best when there is a clear functional gap, no CFO, no IP strategy, no AI capability, and the operating partner needs that function built from scratch within the first 100 days.
