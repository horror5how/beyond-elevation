# The 5-Level AI Operations Maturity Model: Most Companies Claim Level 3. They Are at Level 1.

72% of companies describe their AI maturity as intermediate or higher, according to MIT Sloan's 2026 AI adoption survey. Fewer than 15% have a single AI workflow running in production without daily human intervention. That gap is not a measurement error. It is delusion — and it is where AI budgets disappear.

Hayat Amin argues this is the most expensive blind spot in modern operations: companies spending six figures on AI tools while operating at Level 1 of the AI operations maturity model. Beyond Elevation sees it in every diagnostic — the CEO who says "we use AI" and the ops team that opens ChatGPT when they remember the password.

Here is the framework that tells you exactly where you sit, what each level costs, and what it takes to move up.

## What Is an AI Operations Maturity Model?

An AI operations maturity model is a five-level scoring framework that measures how deeply AI is embedded in your business operations — from individual tool use to fully autonomous agent-driven workflows. It tells you where you sit today, what the next level looks like, and what it costs to get there.

Most frameworks stop at adoption metrics: users, sessions, tools purchased. That measures gym memberships, not fitness. The AI operations maturity model measures outcomes — how many processes run on AI without human initiation, how many decisions are AI-informed, and what percentage of your operations budget flows through automated workflows rather than manual labour.

## Where Does Your Company Sit on the AI Operations Maturity Model?

The five levels are distinct and measurable. Each carries specific cost benchmarks, headcount implications, and clear signals that tell you exactly where your company sits — no ambiguity, no wishful thinking.

**Level 1: Individual Tool Use.** The CEO bought a ChatGPT Enterprise licence. Three people use it regularly. Nobody else knows the login. AI is a personal productivity tool, not an operations function. No workflows are automated. No agents run. Total AI spend: under £5,000 per year. This is where 60% of companies sit when they say "we are using AI." It is not AI operations. It is AI tourism.

**Level 2: Single-Team Pilot.** One department — usually engineering or marketing — runs a proof of concept. A chatbot handles first-line support. An AI tool drafts marketing copy. The pilot works. The rest of the company watches. No production workflows are AI-driven across the business. Total AI spend: £15,000 to £50,000 per year. This is the level where most companies stall because the pilot succeeds but nobody owns the rollout to other functions.

**Level 3: Departmental Deployment.** AI runs specific workflows in production in two or more departments. Finance uses AI reconciliation. Legal uses contract review agents. Customer support runs AI triage. Each department owns its own tools. No cross-functional integration exists. Total AI spend: £80,000 to £200,000 per year. The danger at Level 3 is tool sprawl — each department picks its own vendor, nobody measures total cost per automated task, and the [AI operations budget](/insights/ai-operations-budget-2027/) grows 40% year on year with no central accountability.

**Level 4: Cross-Functional Integration.** AI workflows span departments. An order triggers AI-driven fulfilment, finance reconciliation, and customer communication in a single chain. Someone — a fractional AI operations lead or an internal ops director — owns the AI stack across the company. Processes are measured by cost per task and error rate. Total AI spend: £200,000 to £500,000 per year, but cost per process drops 30–60% versus Level 3 because duplication is eliminated. This is the first level where AI operations generates ROI that survives a board presentation.

**Level 5: AI-Native Operations.** The back office runs on agents. Humans handle exceptions, strategy, and relationship decisions. Monthly reporting closes in [two days](/insights/two-day-close/). Procurement, compliance, and vendor management operate autonomously with human review gates. The AI operations function reports directly to the CEO or COO. Total AI spend: £400,000 to £1M+ per year, but headcount per million in revenue drops 25–40%. Fewer than 3% of companies operate here. The ones that do look nothing like their peers on the P&L — same revenue, 30% lower operating costs, and exit multiples that reflect it.

The gap between Level 2 and Level 3 is not a technology problem. It is an ownership problem. The pilot works because one champion in one team drove it. The rollout stalls because nobody owns AI operations across the company — nobody has the authority, the cross-functional visibility, or the operational skill to move AI from one team to ten.

## How to Score Your AI Operations Maturity in Five Minutes

Score your company right now. One point per yes. Your total is your level.

**1.** Does at least one team run an AI-powered workflow in production — not experimentation? (Level 1 → Level 2)

**2.** Do two or more departments use AI agents for live production tasks? (Level 2 → Level 3)

**3.** Is there one person — internal or fractional — who owns AI operations across the entire company? (Level 3 → Level 4)

**4.** Do AI workflows span multiple departments in a single automated process chain? (Level 3 → Level 4)

**5.** Does AI handle more than 50% of back-office operations, with humans managing exceptions only? (Level 4 → Level 5)
