# Your First External Audit Will Expose Every Finance Shortcut — Here Is the 90-Day Fix

82 percent of startups going through their first external audit get at least one material finding back. Fraud is rarely the cause. The real problem is months of well-meaning shortcuts that hardened into habit.

First external audit preparation is the process of cleaning up your books, documenting your accounting policies, and closing every control gap before an auditor walks through the door. According to a 2025 Deloitte mid-market audit readiness survey, organisations that dedicate at least 90 days to audit preparation reduce adverse findings by 67 percent compared to those that start within the final 30 days. Hayat Amin says the pattern is always the same: "Founders treat the audit like a test they can cram for. It is not a test. It is a financial X-ray. Every shortcut you took shows up on the scan, and the auditor's job is to report every one of them."

## What does an external auditor check first?

An external auditor checks five areas in the first 48 hours of an engagement: bank reconciliations, revenue recognition policies, accrued liabilities, related-party transactions, and equity accounting. These five areas account for over 70 percent of all qualified opinions issued to companies under 50 million in revenue, according to ICAEW guidance on common audit deficiencies.

Bank reconciliations are the foundation. If your bank balances do not agree to your ledger to the penny, every number downstream is suspect. Most startups reconcile monthly, auditors expect weekly at minimum, with documented explanations for every reconciling item above the materiality threshold.

Revenue recognition is where most audit findings concentrate. What matters is whether you recorded the sale in the right period, for the right amount, under the right policy. Startups with multi-element arrangements, annual contracts recognised monthly, or deferred revenue balances get flagged on the first pass if the policy is not documented and consistently applied.

Accrued liabilities, expenses you owe but have not yet been invoiced for, are the most common omission. Payroll accruals, bonus provisions, legal fees, and contractor invoices in transit are the four items auditors find missing most often. Each one understates liabilities and overstates profit, which is exactly the misstatement that produces a qualified opinion.

## How should you prepare for your first external audit?

You should start first external audit preparation exactly 90 days before the engagement date, following a three-phase sequence: cleanup, documentation, and dry run. Starting later compresses the work into a period where every correction creates a new risk of error.

Hayat Amin's Audit Immunity Framework is the five-layer sequence Beyond Elevation runs with every client facing a first audit, and the same framework applies whether the trigger is an investor requirement, a board mandate, or a regulatory threshold.

**Phase 1, Cleanup (Days 1 to 30).** Reconcile every bank account, credit card, and payment processor to the penny. Clear the suspense account. Reclassify any transactions parked in miscellaneous or ask-accountant categories. Post every accrual. Confirm that revenue recognition follows a written policy, if the policy does not exist in writing, create it now and apply it retroactively with opening-balance adjustments where necessary. Close every open period that should be closed.

**Phase 2, Documentation (Days 31 to 60).** Auditors check more than numbers. They check whether a system with controls produced them. Document the following: who authorises payments, who reconciles accounts, who reviews journal entries, how revenue is recognised and over what period, and what happens when an error is found. If the answer to any of these is the founder does everything, that is a control weakness, not a control. Segregation of duties matters. If you are too small for full segregation, document the compensating controls that mitigate the risk.

**Phase 3, Dry Run (Days 61 to 90).** Run a mock audit. Pull the same schedules an auditor will request: trial balance, aged receivables, aged payables, bank reconciliations, loan schedules, equity rollforward, and revenue waterfall. Time how long each schedule takes to produce. If any schedule takes more than two hours, the underlying data is not ready for audit. Fix the data, not the schedule. A fractional CFO who has managed 15 audits knows which items auditors ask for on day one, and ensures they are waiting on the desk before the engagement letter is signed.

## What audit findings kill fundraising rounds?

Five specific audit findings destroy investor confidence faster than a missed quarter: a going-concern paragraph, a revenue recognition restatement, a material weakness in internal controls, undisclosed related-party transactions, and unreconciled equity. Each one tells investors that the numbers they are modelling from cannot be trusted.

A going-concern paragraph does not mean the company is failing, it means the auditor is not confident the company can survive 12 more months without additional funding. For venture-backed companies, this often triggers because management has not prepared a cash-flow forecast that demonstrates sufficient runway. The fix is straightforward: build a [13-week cash-flow forecast](/insights/cash-flow-forecast-13-week-model/) and present it to the auditor with documented assumptions.

Revenue restatements are the most damaging finding for SaaS and subscription companies. If the auditor determines that revenue was recognised in the wrong period, even by 30 days, the restated financials undermine every growth metric the investor has modelled. Hayat Amin argues this is the single most preventable audit failure: "Revenue recognition is not an accounting decision. It is a policy decision that should be locked before the first invoice ships. Every company I work with has a written revenue recognition policy by day 15 of the engagement. It takes two hours to write and saves six figures in audit remediation."

Undisclosed related-party transactions, payments to founder-related entities, loans between directors and the company, contracts with family members, are not illegal, but failing to disclose them is an audit deficiency. The fix is a related-party questionnaire completed by every director before the audit begins.

## When is it too late to start first external audit preparation?

It is too late to start first external audit preparation fewer than 45 days before the engagement date. Below that threshold, you cannot complete the three-phase cleanup, documentation, and dry-run sequence without cutting corners that create new findings.

The ideal start point is 90 days before the engagement. At 60 days, the work is feasible but compressed. At 30 days, a fractional CFO can limit the damage, reconciling critical accounts, documenting the three highest-risk policies, and preparing schedules, but cannot build the full internal controls framework. The company will survive the audit but is likely to receive findings that require remediation before the next one.

## FAQ

### What triggers a requirement for a first external audit?

Common triggers include reaching a revenue or balance-sheet threshold (the UK Companies Act requires an audit above 10.2 million in turnover), investor term-sheet requirements, preparing for a funding round, and board-mandated governance standards. If any of these apply, start preparation 90 days before the engagement date.

### Can a fractional CFO manage the entire first external audit process?

Yes. A fractional CFO with audit experience handles preparation, auditor selection, information requests, query resolution, and post-audit remediation. Most fractional CFOs require two to four additional days per month during the audit period beyond their normal engagement scope.

### What is the difference between a clean opinion and a qualified opinion?

A clean (unqualified) opinion means the auditor found no material misstatements and the financial statements present a true and fair view. A qualified opinion means the auditor found one or more issues that are material but not pervasive. Investors treat a qualified opinion as a serious red flag that delays or kills fundraising conversations.

### How does audit preparation differ from month-end close?

Month-end close produces accurate numbers for a single period. Audit preparation ensures that 12 months of closed periods, plus the policies and controls behind them, meet the standard an independent auditor applies. A company with a strong month-end close process is roughly 80 percent of the way to audit readiness, the remaining 20 percent is documentation, control testing, and schedule preparation.

### What should a founder do if the audit is in 30 days and nothing is prepared?

Hire a fractional CFO immediately for a triage engagement. Prioritise bank reconciliations, revenue recognition documentation, and accrual completeness, these three areas account for the majority of qualified findings. Accept that the audit may produce findings but ensure they are explainable and not systemic. A competent fractional CFO can limit the damage in 30 days even if full audit readiness is not achievable.
