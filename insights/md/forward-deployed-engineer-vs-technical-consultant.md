# Forward Deployed Engineer vs Technical Consultant: What You Are Actually Buying

Hire a technical consultant when you need someone to tell you what to do inside a system that already exists. Hire a forward deployed engineer when you need someone to make the thing run and answer for it afterwards. The difference is not seniority, and it is not skill. It is the deliverable. A technical consultant is paid to hand you a recommendation. A forward deployed engineer is paid to hand you something in production with their name on it.

That sounds like a distinction of degree until you read the two job descriptions next to each other, which is what this piece does. Both are real occupations with real postings live this week in New York City. One of them is enormous and well understood. The other barely exists yet and costs nearly three times as much. Hayat Amin, who takes the forward deployment engagements at Beyond Elevation, spends most first calls establishing which of the two the caller actually needs, and roughly half the time the answer is the consultant.

## What Each One Is Paid To Hand You

The United States government has already written the technical consultant's job description, and it is worth reading before any vendor writes you a better one. The Bureau of Labor Statistics defines management analysts, the census category most consultants fall into, as people who "recommend ways to improve an organization's efficiency" and who "advise managers on how to make organizations more profitable through reduced costs and increased revenues". The mechanism is stated plainly: they "make recommendations to management through presentations or written reports".

The neighbouring category, computer systems analysts, is the closer fit for the technical half of the title. Those are people who "study an organization's current computer systems and design ways to improve efficiency". Study. Design. Recommend. Report.

Neither definition contains the word production. Neither contains deploy, ship, or own. That is not an oversight by the Bureau of Labor Statistics. It is an accurate description of what the occupation sells, and it has been accurate for forty years.

## Read the Two Job Descriptions Side By Side

Now take a live forward deployed engineer posting. Anthropic is running 587 open roles today. Six of them are forward deployed. Not one of the 587 has the word consultant in its title.

The Forward Deployed Engineer role, posted for New York City, San Francisco and Seattle at 280,000 to 320,000 dollars in annual salary before equity, describes the job in its own words. The engineer will "work within customer systems to build production applications with Claude models". They will "deliver technical artifacts for customers like MCP servers, sub-agents, and agent skills that will be used in production workflows". They will "provide white glove deployment support for Anthropic products in enterprise environments", embedding "directly with our most strategic customers" to "ship advanced AI applications that solve real world business problems".

Production appears twice in the first two lines. Ship appears in the third. Customer systems, not a customer document. The verbs are the entire argument, and they are the seller's own verbs, not a critic's characterisation.

Set the two vocabularies against each other and the choice stops being a matter of taste. Recommend, advise, study, design, report. Build, deliver, deploy, embed, ship. Both lists describe useful work. Only one of them ends with something running in your company.

## The Title Has Already Stopped Working

Here is the complication a buyer runs into in 2026. The consultancies have bought the word, and the word now means three different jobs depending on who filed the document.

A full text search of the Securities and Exchange Commission archive returns 87 filings containing the phrase forward deployed engineers, 29 of them filed since the first of January 2026. Read two of those 29 and the problem is obvious.

EPAM Systems, a technology consultancy, put an investor day presentation on file on 12 March 2026. Under a slide headed as the firm's differentiation moat, it lists "forward deployed engineers, full-stack agentic engineers offering full-stack ownership, agentic tooling depth at the team level, and delivery-as-code". Full-stack ownership. Delivery as code. That is the forward deployed definition, sold by a consultancy, and it is a real offer.

SailPoint filed its annual report a week later, on 19 March 2026, and used the identical phrase for something else entirely. Its forward deployed engineers are "overlay resources, including technical specialists and forward deployed engineers who assist in the sales process, ensuring that our solutions are presented with the necessary technical depth and clarity". That is pre-sales. Anthropic itself has a posting titled Pre-Sales Program Lead, Forward Deployed Engineering. Deloitte sells Forward Deployed Engineering as a service line inside its consulting practice.

So stop asking what the person is called. The title has been worth nothing since roughly the middle of last year, and forward deployed engineer job listings grew around 800 percent between January and September 2025 and more than 1,000 percent year on year into 2026, which is exactly the growth rate at which a word gets taken. Ask instead what the person will own, in writing, and what will exist in your systems when they leave.

## Where the Technical Consultant Wins

Often. This is not a piece about the consultant being a lesser animal, and any firm that tells you otherwise is selling.

The technical consultant wins on platform depth. Read what Accenture asks of a Salesforce Technical Consultant: be an "APEX code expert who understands and can implement Apex SOAP and REST web services", handle "Process builder, Flow, Workflows, Approvals, Apex Triggers, Apex Classes, Visual Force Pages", apply "best practices for trigger development and design patterns", "design and implement complex sharing models using declarative and programmatic methods", and "design and implement a test plan that ensures adequate quality". That is a decade inside one vendor's stack. If your problem lives inside Salesforce, a generalist forward deployed engineer will spend three months learning what that person already knows, and you will pay for the education.

It wins on breadth. A migration touching eleven systems and four hundred users is a staffing problem, and consultancies are extremely good at staffing problems. One embedded engineer is the wrong shape for it.

It wins on procurement. There is already a master services agreement, an insurance certificate, a named partner and a route to escalate. For a regulated buyer in the United States that is not bureaucracy, it is the reason the project is allowed to start at all.

And it wins on price, which is the next section.

## What Each One Costs in the United States

The consultant is the cheaper unit and it is not close. The Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the median annual wage for management analysts at 101,860 dollars as of May 2025, across 1,077,100 jobs, with the occupation projected to grow 10 percent between 2025 and 2035. Computer systems analysts sit at 105,850 dollars across 544,400 jobs, growing 8 percent. Those are salaries rather than billing rates, and a firm marks them up, but they set the floor of the market you are buying in.

Against that, Anthropic's forward deployed engineer band starts at 280,000 dollars, before equity, for the same three American cities where the consultants live. Nearly three times the median analyst wage, for a role that exists six times inside a company running 587 open positions.

A buyer should be suspicious of that gap, so here is the honest justification. You are not paying three times for a better engineer. You are paying to delete a handoff. In the consultant model the person who learns how your operation actually works writes it down, and somebody else, later, builds from the writing. Everything true but unwritten is lost in that gap, and everything unwritten is usually the reason the last project failed. The forward deployed engineer is the same person on both sides of the handoff, which is why the model costs what it costs and why it is worthless when there was no handoff problem to begin with.

## The Leaving Test

Hayat Amin's Leaving Test is one question, asked before you sign anything. Picture the last day of the engagement. What exists that did not exist before?

If the honest answer is a document, a roadmap, an architecture, a set of recommendations, a trained team, then you want a technical consultant and you should buy one without embarrassment. Those are real goods. Companies pay for them because knowing what to do is genuinely hard.

If the honest answer is that something has to be running, connected to live data, used by people who did not ask for it, and still working in month six when the person who built it is gone, then a recommendation is not a smaller version of what you need. It is a different product, and buying it will cost you a quarter.

The expensive mistake is not choosing wrong. It is choosing the consultant, receiving exactly what was promised, and mistaking the document for progress. Ninety days later the deck is better, the vocabulary is better, and nothing is in production. That is not a failure of the consultant. It is a failure of the purchase order.

## FAQ

### Forward deployed engineer vs technical consultant: what is the actual difference?

The deliverable. A technical consultant produces a recommendation, a design or a report, which is how the United States Bureau of Labor Statistics defines the occupation: making recommendations to management through presentations or written reports. A forward deployed engineer works inside your production environment and leaves a running system behind. Anthropic's own posting for the role describes building production applications within customer systems. If the engagement ends with a document, you bought a consultant, whatever the invoice says.

### Is a forward deployed engineer just a technical consultant with a new title?

Sometimes it is exactly that, and the filings show it. The phrase now appears in 87 documents at the Securities and Exchange Commission, and it means different jobs in different ones. EPAM Systems uses it for full-stack ownership and delivery as code. SailPoint uses it for pre-sales overlay resources supporting a sales process. Both are honest uses of a word that has stopped carrying information. The test is not the title but what the person commits to your systems and stays accountable for.

### Do consulting firms hire forward deployed engineers?

Yes, and increasingly. Deloitte sells Forward Deployed Engineering as a consulting service line. EPAM Systems named forward deployed engineers as part of its differentiation moat in an investor day presentation filed on 12 March 2026. Twenty nine filings with the Securities and Exchange Commission have used the phrase since January 2026. Buying it from a consultancy is a legitimate option; just confirm you are buying the EPAM meaning of the phrase rather than the pre-sales meaning.

### Forward deployed engineer vs technical account manager: which is which?

A technical account manager owns the relationship and the health of an account you are already paying for, and works for the vendor. A forward deployed engineer owns a build inside your company. The overlap is that both sit close to the customer. The difference is that only one of them writes code you keep.

### How much does a forward deployed engineer cost compared to a technical consultant in the United States?

Roughly three times more at the salary level. The median annual wage for management analysts in the United States is 101,860 dollars as of May 2025, and for computer systems analysts 105,850 dollars. Anthropic's forward deployed engineer role for New York City, San Francisco and Seattle is posted at 280,000 to 320,000 dollars in base salary. Firms price differently again: Beyond Elevation's fractional engagements start from 5,800 dollars a month and projects from 30,000 dollars.

### Should a startup hire a technical consultant or a forward deployed engineer?

A startup that knows what it needs built and needs specialist depth in one platform should hire the consultant, because it is cheaper and the scope is real. A startup where nobody can yet write the specification, and where an AI pilot has already produced a good demo and no production system, is describing the forward deployed problem. The tell is whether your last engagement ended with something running or something written.
