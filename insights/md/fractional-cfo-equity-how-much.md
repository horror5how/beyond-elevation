# How Much Equity Should a Fractional CFO Get? The Numbers Most Founders Never See

0.25% to 1.5%. That is the equity band a fractional CFO should receive — and most founders either overshoot it or structure the grant so badly it becomes a liability, not an incentive. According to the 2026 Carta Equity Benchmarks Report, fractional and part-time executives now receive equity in 38% of seed-stage companies, up from 12% in 2022. The shift is real. The problem is that nobody publishes the deal structures behind the numbers.

Hayat Amin has structured more than 40 fractional CFO engagements across the UK, US, and Gulf. The pattern repeats: founders who get the compensation structure right retain their CFO for 18+ months and exit with a clean cap table. Founders who get it wrong lose the CFO at the worst possible moment — mid-raise, mid-diligence, mid-close — and the equity they granted sits on the cap table doing nothing.

## How Much Equity Should a Fractional CFO Get?

A fractional CFO should receive between 0.25% and 1.5% equity, depending on company stage, cash retainer, and the scope of work. Pre-revenue startups that pay a below-market retainer sit at the top of that range. Series B companies paying full market rate sit at the bottom — or skip equity entirely. Beyond Elevation uses a four-band model tied to stage and cash offset.

**Pre-seed and seed (pre-revenue to £1M ARR):** 0.75% to 1.5% equity alongside a £2,000–4,000/month retainer. The CFO is building a finance function from nothing — chart of accounts, reporting cadence, board pack, data room foundations. The equity compensates for the gap between a £3,000 retainer and the £8,000+ monthly cost of an equivalent full-time hire.

**Series A (£1M–5M ARR):** 0.25% to 0.5% equity alongside a £5,000–8,000/month retainer. The finance stack exists. The CFO is optimising it for the next raise or preparing investor-grade reporting. Lower risk, lower equity.

**Series B and beyond (£5M+ ARR):** 0% to 0.25% equity alongside a £8,000–12,000/month retainer. At this stage the retainer is close to market rate. Equity is a retention sweetener, not a core compensation component.

**Pre-exit engagements:** retainer plus a success fee (typically 0.5%–1.5% of enterprise value uplift attributable to the finance workstream), not equity. The engagement is time-bound and the incentive should be too.

## When Does a Fractional CFO Deserve Equity at All?

A fractional CFO deserves equity when their work creates enterprise value that did not exist before — building the [exit-ready finance function](/insights/exit-ready-finance-function/), preparing the company for diligence, or designing the financial architecture investors price into the round. If the fractional CFO is running month-end compliance on a system someone else built, pay them in cash. Cash-only work does not warrant equity.

Hayat Amin draws the line with a single question: does this engagement change the company's valuation at exit? If the answer is yes — because the CFO is building the data room, restructuring the reporting stack, or producing the board pack that gets the term sheet signed — equity aligns incentives. If the answer is no, equity dilutes the cap table for zero return.

Three conditions that justify equity in a fractional CFO engagement:

**1. Below-market retainer.** The founder cannot afford a market-rate CFO. Equity bridges the gap. This is the most common scenario at pre-seed and seed.

**2. Value-creation scope.** The CFO is not maintaining — they are building. New reporting infrastructure, fundraising preparation, or [exit preparation](/insights/fractional-cfo-exit-preparation/) that directly moves the enterprise value needle.

**3. Long engagement horizon.** The engagement is expected to run 12+ months. Equity only compounds value when the CFO stays long enough to see the outcome through.

## What Is the Right Vesting Schedule for a Fractional Executive?

The standard fractional executive vesting schedule is a 3-to-4-year vest with a 6-month cliff, monthly vesting thereafter, and single-trigger acceleration on change of control. This structure differs from a full-time employee grant in two ways: the cliff is shorter (6 months, not 12) and acceleration is standard (because the fractional executive will almost certainly be replaced by a full-time hire before exit).

Why the shorter cliff matters: a fractional CFO proves their value faster than a full-time hire. Within 90 days they have either built the reporting stack or they have not. A 12-month cliff designed for permanent employees punishes the fractional for the very flexibility the founder hired them for.

Hayat Amin argues that the acceleration clause is non-negotiable. A fractional CFO who spends 18 months building the exit-ready finance function and then gets replaced by a full-time CFO three months before the deal closes has created the value the equity was meant to reward. Without single-trigger acceleration, the founder captures 100% of that value and the fractional gets nothing. That misalignment kills the next referral, the next engagement, and the founder's reputation in the fractional community.

## Should You Pay Retainer Plus Equity or Choose One?

Pay both. A below-market retainer paired with meaningful equity outperforms either structure alone — and Beyond Elevation's data across 40+ engagements proves it. Retainer-only fractional CFOs leave when a higher-paying client calls. Equity-only fractional CFOs deprioritise the engagement because they have no monthly cash obligation tying them to the work. The hybrid locks in both commitment and upside alignment.

Hayat Amin developed the **Fractional CFO Compensation Matrix** to solve this. It works on two axes:

**Axis 1 — Cash retainer relative to market rate.** Below market (less than 60% of equivalent full-time cost), at market (60%–90%), or above market (90%+).

**Axis 2 — Value creation scope.** Maintain (run what exists), Build (create new financial infrastructure), or Exit (prepare the company for sale or raise).

The intersection gives the equity band. Below-market retainer plus Build or Exit scope = top of the equity range (0.75%–1.5%). At-market retainer plus Maintain scope = zero equity. Every other combination falls between. The matrix eliminates the negotiation guesswork that wastes the first three weeks of most fractional engagements.

## What Equity Mistakes Cost Founders the Most?

The three equity mistakes that cost founders the most money are granting equity with no cliff, granting equity without anti-dilution language, and granting equity to a fractional CFO whose scope does not justify it. Each one is avoidable. Each one shows up in diligence.

**No cliff.** A fractional CFO who receives 1% on day one and leaves after 60 days walks away with 1% of the company for two months of work. A 6-month cliff prevents this entirely. Hayat Amin says the cliff is the single most important clause in a [fractional executive contract](/insights/fractional-executive-contract-clauses/) — and the one most founders forget.

**No anti-dilution protection.** If the fractional CFO receives 1% pre-seed and the company raises three rounds, that 1% dilutes to 0.3% or less. Standard anti-dilution language (weighted-average, not full-ratchet) ensures the grant maintains its intended value. Without it, the CFO's incentive erodes with every round — exactly when the company needs them most.

**Over-granting for maintenance work.** A fractional CFO who runs the monthly close on an existing system does not deserve equity. They deserve a competitive retainer. Equity is for building, not maintaining. Founders who cannot tell the difference end up with a bloated cap table and a fractional who does [full-time-CFO-level work](/insights/fractional-cfo-vs-full-time-cfo-2026/) at part-time hours because the equity makes up for it.

## FAQ

### Can a fractional CFO negotiate equity?

Yes, and they should. A fractional CFO who does not ask about equity in a pre-revenue engagement is either inexperienced or does not plan to stay long enough for it to matter. The negotiation should centre on scope, not percentage — define the value-creation milestones first, then back into the equity band using the stage-appropriate range (0.25%–1.5%).

### Should advisory shares go to a fractional CFO instead of employee options?

No. Advisory shares typically vest over 1–2 years with no cliff and carry no obligation to deliver specific outcomes. A fractional CFO engagement is an operating role with defined deliverables. Use a standard option grant with a cliff, monthly vesting, and a clear scope document. Advisory structures undervalue the work and create governance confusion in diligence.

### What happens to fractional CFO equity if they leave early?

If the grant has a cliff and the CFO leaves before the cliff date, they forfeit everything. If they leave after the cliff, they keep whatever has vested and forfeit the rest. This is why the cliff length and vesting cadence matter — they define the financial penalty for leaving and the financial reward for staying.

### Does equity affect a fractional CFO's independence?

It can. A fractional CFO with a large equity stake may avoid delivering bad news that could tank the valuation their equity depends on. The safeguard is to keep the equity band at 1.5% or below and to pair it with a retainer large enough that the CFO's livelihood does not depend on the equity outcome. Independence comes from the retainer, not the equity.
