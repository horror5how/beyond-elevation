# Fractional Executive Contract: The 7 Clauses Missing From Every Template We Review

A fractional executive contract needs seven clauses most templates omit: a scope defined by deliverables, a performance trigger, an IP assignment, a data-access boundary, a transition plan, a non-compete carve-out and a termination notice with mandatory handover. Miss any one and the engagement ends in a dispute that costs more than the retainer.

According to the 2025 Staffing Industry Analysts Workforce Solutions Survey, 67 per cent of fractional executive engagements that ended within six months traced the failure to a contract or scope gap, not to capability. Hayat Amin argues that most founders take a full-time employment contract, delete the word "full-time", and call it a fractional agreement. "A fractional runs a function across multiple companies at once," Hayat Amin says. "The contract has to reflect that or it protects nobody."

[Beyond Elevation](/insights/run-company-on-fractional-executives/) reviews fractional executive contracts weekly. These are the seven clauses that separate a working engagement from a costly dispute.

## Why do most fractional executive contracts fail?

Most fractional executive contracts fail because they borrow from employment law instead of commercial services law. Employment contracts assume one employer, five days a week and progressive discipline. None of that applies to a fractional executive who works across two to four companies, owns their own tools and brings existing frameworks to every engagement.

The structural mismatch creates three problems. A contract based on time, two days a week, pays for presence, not output. A vague scope of work means both sides read "running finance" or "building an IP strategy" differently. Most contracts skip transition planning entirely, so when the engagement ends, the company has no documentation and no way to continue the work without starting over.

The fix is not a longer contract. It is a different kind of contract, one built on commercial services principles rather than employment law defaults.

## What should a fractional executive scope of work include?

A fractional executive scope of work should define deliverables, not days. The contract lists specific outputs, a month-end close process, a patent filing roadmap, an AI automation audit, with deadlines, quality criteria and acceptance conditions. Scoping by time rather than deliverables is the single biggest predictor of a failed fractional engagement.

The deliverables model aligns incentives: the fractional gets paid for results, not attendance. It creates measurable accountability, either the deliverable exists or it does not, and it kills the most common dispute: you cannot argue about whether someone worked hard enough when the contract says deliver a [13-week cash flow forecast](/insights/cash-flow-forecast-13-week-model/) by week four.

Beyond Elevation structures every engagement around a 90-day deliverables roadmap. The first 30 days are diagnostic: audit the function and produce a gap analysis. Days 31 to 60 are build: implement the systems and processes. Days 61 to 90 hand the function over: it runs without the fractional in the room. That structure is written into the contract as a schedule, not left to goodwill.

## Should a fractional executive get equity?

A fractional executive should receive equity only when the engagement exceeds twelve months and the scope includes strategic decisions that affect company valuation. For anything shorter or more operational, equity creates misaligned incentives and complicates the cap table with no corresponding value. Fewer than 15 per cent of fractional engagements justify an equity component.

The split is straightforward. A [fractional CFO building the finance function](/insights/fractional-cfo-fundraising/) for a pre-Series A company over 18 months, sitting on the board and directly influencing the fundraise, equity at 0.25 to 1.0 per cent vesting over the engagement period makes sense. A fractional running a three-month project to close the books faster: equity here inflates expectations and creates governance complications nobody needs.

Hayat Amin reminds founders that VCs scrutinise every cap table entry during due diligence. A fractional executive holding 2 per cent equity after a six-month engagement raises questions about governance and value-for-money that founders should not have to defend in the data room. If the fractional earned equity, you should be able to point to a specific valuation event they influenced. If you cannot, the equity was a negotiation concession, not a value trade.

## How do you handle IP ownership in a fractional engagement?

All intellectual property created during a fractional executive engagement belongs to the company by default, with a clear carve-out for the fractional's pre-existing tools, templates, and methodologies. Without this clause, work product ownership stays ambiguous and can surface as a deal-blocking issue during M&A due diligence years after the engagement ends.

The contract needs three IP provisions. First, a work-for-hire assignment: everything created for the company is the company's property. Second, a pre-existing IP schedule: the fractional lists every tool and framework they bring into the engagement, and the company gets a licence to use them but does not own them. Third, a residual knowledge clause: the fractional can use general skills and knowledge gained during the engagement but cannot share confidential data or proprietary processes with other clients.

Hayat Amin's team at Beyond Elevation has seen three M&A transactions stall because the buyer discovered that a fractional executive's work product had no clear ownership chain. One acquisition nearly collapsed over a financial model the fractional CFO built using a proprietary template, neither party had documented who owned the model itself. The fix is a single clause drafted before the engagement starts. The cost of adding it is negligible. The cost of missing it is the deal.

## What termination and handover clause should a fractional executive contract include?

A fractional executive contract should include a 30-day minimum notice period for either party with a mandatory handover protocol covering documentation, system access transfer, and a successor briefing. Shorter notice periods leave the company stranded mid-function. Longer periods trap both parties in an engagement that has already failed.

The 30-day window works because it gives the company time to find a replacement or bring the work in-house, while the fractional documents their work and briefs the successor. The handover protocol is not optional, without it, the fractional walks away and the company reconstructs everything from scratch at three times the original cost.

Beyond Elevation builds a handover checklist into every contract as a formal schedule: process documentation, system credentials, vendor contacts, pending decisions, and a 60-minute successor briefing. If the fractional leaves without completing the checklist, the final invoice is held until handover is complete.

## What non-compete clause works for a fractional executive?

A fractional executive non-compete should restrict the operator from working with named direct competitors during the engagement and for 90 days after, but must explicitly permit work with non-competing companies in the same industry. Broader restrictions are unenforceable in most jurisdictions and deter the best operators from signing.

The fractional model depends on multi-client work. A clause that stops a fractional CFO working with any other company in fintech turns a fractional engagement into a permanent role at a fractional price. That's unfair, usually illegal, and a sign the company should hire someone permanent instead. Name specific competitors in the contract, not industries or sectors.

The stronger alternative is a confidentiality wall: the fractional can work with companies in the same space but cannot transfer client data, strategies, or proprietary processes across engagements. The restriction falls on information, not on the person. That version is enforceable, fair, and protects the company, which is the point of the clause in the first place.

## How do you set performance triggers in a fractional executive contract?

Performance triggers in a fractional executive contract tie to specific, measurable outcomes defined at engagement start, not to subjective assessments or general satisfaction. The contract lists three to five milestones with dates and a review mechanism at each milestone that determines whether the engagement continues, adjusts scope, or terminates.

This is the clause most templates miss. Without performance triggers, neither party has an objective basis for evaluation. The founder relies on gut feeling. The fractional relies on hours billed. Both end up frustrated, and the dispute that follows has no contractual anchor to resolve it.

Hayat Amin's Fractional Engagement Architecture, the framework Beyond Elevation uses across every placement, includes a milestone review at days 30, 60, and 90. Each review checks three questions: were the deliverables completed, are they at the agreed quality standard, and does the next phase still make commercial sense? If two of three answers are no, the engagement pauses for a scope reset before money or time is wasted on the wrong trajectory. This structure has reduced Beyond Elevation's engagement disputes to under 5 per cent across more than 200 [fractional placements](/insights/fractional-executive-cost-comparison/).

## FAQ

### How long should a fractional executive contract be?

A fractional executive contract runs 8 to 15 pages including schedules for scope of work, deliverables, IP assignment, and handover protocol. Shorter contracts miss critical clauses. Longer contracts are padded with boilerplate that neither party reads and no one enforces.

### Can you use a standard consulting agreement for a fractional executive?

A standard consulting agreement covers some basics but misses clauses specific to fractional engagements: multi-client provisions, function ownership, transition planning, and performance triggers. Use a consulting agreement as a starting point, then add the seven clauses above.

### What should a fractional executive charge?

Fractional executive rates in the UK range from £1,500 to £5,000 per day depending on seniority and function. CFOs and [Chief IP Officers](/insights/when-to-hire-chief-ip-officer/) typically sit at £2,000 to £3,500 per day. The contract should specify whether the rate is daily, based on deliverables, or a monthly retainer.

### Should a fractional executive contract include a trial period?

Yes. A 30-day trial period with defined deliverables and a decision point is standard practice. The trial carries the same contractual protections as the full engagement, including IP assignment and confidentiality. If it does not work, both parties exit cleanly.

### Who should draft the fractional executive contract?

The hiring company drafts the contract. If you leave it to the fractional, their template will be optimised for their protection, not yours. Have a commercial lawyer review it, not an employment lawyer, because this is a commercial services agreement, not an employment relationship.
