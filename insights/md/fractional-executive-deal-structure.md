# Retainer, Day Rate, or Equity: How to Structure a Fractional Executive Deal That Works

The wrong deal structure kills more fractional executive engagements than the wrong operator.

According to a 2025 Staffing Industry Analysts report, the flexible executive talent market grew 31% year on year, and fractional C-suite placements are the fastest-growing segment within it. Hayat Amin argues the surge has created a mismatch: founders are hiring fractional executives faster than they're learning how to structure the deal. The pay structure ends up rewarding the wrong behaviour, timelines drift out of sync, and a productive engagement folds inside six months.

Three compensation models dominate fractional executive deal structures in 2026: monthly retainer, day rate, and equity-linked hybrid. Each suits a different company stage, scope, and exit horizon. The structure you pick decides whether the operator builds systems or just delivers slide decks.

## What are the three fractional executive deal structures?

The three fractional executive deal structures are the monthly retainer, the day rate, and the equity-linked hybrid. Each sets a different incentive, a different commitment level, and a different risk profile for the company and the operator. Most founders default to whichever structure the fractional executive proposes, which is exactly the wrong way to make this decision.

The monthly retainer pays a fixed fee, typically £3,000 to £12,000 per month for a fractional CFO, £4,000 to £10,000 for a fractional Chief IP Officer, and £5,000 to £15,000 for an AI Operations lead, in exchange for a defined number of days or an agreed scope per month. The retainer makes costs predictable: the company knows what it's paying, and the operator can plan their portfolio across clients.

The day rate charges per day worked, usually £1,200 to £3,500 depending on seniority and function. It suits work with a fixed scope and end date: a 90-day finance transformation, an IP audit before fundraising, or an AI operations sprint to automate a specific workflow. It's transparent, but it creates an incentive for the engagement to run longer than it should.

The equity-linked hybrid combines a reduced cash retainer with equity compensation, typically 0.25% to 1.5% of the company vesting over 12 to 24 months. This model ties the fractional executive's incentives to long-term value creation. It also needs the most careful structuring, because equity without the right vesting schedule, cliff, and trigger clauses creates more problems than it solves.

## When should you use a monthly retainer?

A monthly retainer works best when the company needs ongoing, embedded access to a fractional executive who operates as part of the leadership team: attending board meetings, managing direct reports, owning a function, and building the infrastructure that outlasts their engagement. Expect to commit for at least six months to see a return.

Beyond Elevation structures most fractional CFO and Chief IP Officer placements on a retainer. Hayat Amin's view is direct: "A day-rate operator is a consultant with a better title. A retainer operator is a team member with a defined exit date. The difference shows up in what they build versus what they advise."

The typical retainer engagement runs 6 to 18 months. Shorter than six months, a day rate usually serves better. Longer than 18 months, the company should ask whether a permanent hire makes more financial sense, though often it doesn't: the fractional model delivers senior capability at 30% to 40% of what a permanent hire costs once you add benefits and equity.

Every retainer agreement should include three non-negotiable clauses: a 30-day mutual termination notice, a quarterly scope review, and a monthly reporting rhythm tied to measurable business outcomes. Without these, the retainer drifts into a vague advisory relationship that neither side can evaluate or exit cleanly.

## When does a day rate make more sense than a retainer?

A day rate makes sense when the engagement has a defined deliverable, a clear end date, and a scope tight enough that the company can judge the output against a fixed benchmark. Think IP audits, financial model builds, due diligence preparation sprints, or AI operations assessments: discrete projects, not ongoing functions.

Day rates in 2026 vary by role and market. In London, a fractional CFO day rate sits between £1,500 and £3,000. In New York, $2,000 to $4,500. AI Operations leads command £1,800 to £3,500 depending on deployment complexity. These rates run 40% to 60% below what the same capability costs from a Big Four consultancy, with the critical difference that a fractional executive owns the outcome, not the report.

Hayat Amin warns founders about one specific failure mode with day rates: scope creep without rate renegotiation. A project that starts as a 10-day IP audit expands into a 40-day portfolio restructure, and the founder never adjusts the commercial terms. The fix is structural: build a rate-review trigger into any day-rate agreement. If the scope expands beyond 150% of the original estimate, both sides renegotiate before the next day is billed.

## How should equity be structured in a fractional executive deal?

Equity in a fractional executive deal should vest monthly over 12 to 24 months with a three-month cliff, and should represent 0.25% to 1.5% of the company depending on stage, role, and the size of the cash component. The cash retainer should still cover at least 60% of the operator's market rate so the equity supplements income rather than replaces it.

The mistake founders make most often is offering equity instead of cash to a fractional executive who needs cash flow. An operator managing three to five clients can't absorb a below-market cash deal on the promise of a future exit. The result is predictable: the operator prioritises clients paying full cash rate, and the equity client gets residual hours.

Hayat Amin's Fractional Equity Alignment Test asks three questions. First: is the company likely to have a liquidity event within the vesting window? If not, equity is a fiction, offer cash. Second: does the operator have the portfolio capacity to absorb a below-market cash rate? If not, the deal structure will underperform. Third: is the grant large enough to matter? Below 0.25% for a C-suite fractional is insulting. Above 1.5% for someone working only a few days a month raises governance questions from future investors.

The best fractional executive deals include acceleration tied to milestones. If the operator delivers a specific outcome, closes a fundraising round, completes an IP restructure, ships an AI automation that hits a defined ROI target, a portion of the equity accelerates. This rewards outcomes, not attendance.

## What are the red flags in a fractional executive deal structure?

The biggest red flags in a fractional executive deal structure are equity-only compensation from a funded company, retainers with no termination clause, and day rates with no scope cap. Each one signals a gap between what the company says it wants and what the commercial structure actually incentivises the operator to deliver.

An equity-only deal from a company with revenue or funding isn't a fractional executive offer. It's an advisory board seat disguised as an operating role. Operators who accept equity-only arrangements typically deliver advisory-level effort, not operator-level output. If the company can afford to pay cash but chooses not to, the operator should ask why.

Retainers without termination provisions lock both sides into an arrangement that may stop working. The best fractional engagements have a mutual 30-day notice clause and a quarterly scope review, protecting the company from paying for declining value and the operator from losing revenue without runway to replace it.

Hayat Amin reminds founders that the deal structure is a signal. The way a fractional executive negotiates their own arrangement predicts how they'll negotiate on your behalf. An operator who accepts vague terms, unclear scope, and no performance metrics in their own engagement is unlikely to bring rigour to your finance function, IP strategy, or AI operations.

## Choosing the right fractional executive deal structure for your stage

The right fractional executive deal structure depends on three variables: your cash position, the length of the engagement, and whether the operator's work creates measurable enterprise value. Pre-seed companies need hybrids, growth-stage companies need retainers, and scopes built around a single project, at any stage, need day rates with hard boundaries.

Pre-seed and seed-stage companies with limited cash should use a hybrid model: reduced retainer plus equity with acceleration tied to milestones. The cash component should still be meaningful, at least £2,000 per month, so the operator treats the engagement as a commitment, not a favour.

Series A and B companies should default to a full-cash retainer. At this stage the company has capital, and the fractional executive's value is in building infrastructure, not sharing speculative upside. Equity can supplement the retainer when the scope includes exit preparation or a specific value-creation mandate, but it should never replace cash below market rate.

Engagements built around a single project, at any stage, use day rates with a capped scope. Define the deliverable, agree the maximum number of days, and include a renegotiation trigger if scope expands. ## FAQ

### How much equity should a fractional CFO receive?

A fractional CFO typically receives 0.25% to 1.0% equity vesting over 12 to 24 months with a three-month cliff, alongside a cash retainer covering at least 60% of market rate. The exact percentage depends on company stage, time commitment, and whether the CFO has a value-creation mandate such as fundraising or exit preparation. ### What is the average monthly retainer for a fractional executive in 2026?

Fractional executive monthly retainers in 2026 range from £3,000 to £15,000 depending on role, seniority, and scope. A fractional CFO typically commands £3,000 to £12,000, a fractional Chief IP Officer £4,000 to £10,000, and an AI Operations lead £5,000 to £15,000. These figures cover two to four days per week of embedded operating time.

### Should a fractional executive be paid a retainer or a day rate?

Use a monthly retainer when the engagement is ongoing and the operator is part of the leadership team. Use a day rate when the engagement has a defined deliverable and end date. Retainers reward depth and system-building. Day rates reward speed on a specific project. The deciding question is whether you're hiring for a function or a project.

### What clauses should be in a fractional executive agreement?

Every fractional executive agreement should include a mutual 30-day termination notice, a quarterly scope review, reporting and KPI requirements, IP assignment provisions, confidentiality terms, and, if equity is included, vesting schedules with cliff, acceleration triggers, and treatment on termination.
