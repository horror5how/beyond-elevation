# The Fractional Executive M&A Playbook: Three Operators, 90 Days, One Deal Done Right

A fractional executive M&A team, a CFO, a Chief IP Officer, and an AI operations lead, delivers buy-side due diligence at the same quality as a Big Four advisory engagement for roughly a fifth of the cost. The model works for acquisitions between $3M and $50M enterprise value and runs in 90 days. According to Bain & Company's 2025 Global M&A Report, 60% of acquisitions fail to deliver their projected returns within three years. Hayat Amin argues targets aren't usually the problem. Buyers staff deals with advisors who have never operated the asset they're evaluating.

Three fractional executives can close that gap. Beyond Elevation has run the fractional executive M&A model on deals ranging from $5M to $40M enterprise value, and the pattern holds: operators find what analysts miss, because operators have sat in the chair the target company occupies. The cost difference is structural, not marginal.

## Why a fractional executive M&A team outperforms traditional advisors

A fractional executive M&A team outperforms because operators evaluate a business the way they would run it, not the way a spreadsheet models it. Advisory firms assign analysts who build models. Operators walk the floor, read the contracts, and stress-test the numbers against what they know breaks in real companies. The difference surfaces in deal outcomes: operator-led due diligence catches material issues that analyst-led reviews miss at a consistent rate.

The problem with traditional M&A advisory is specialisation without integration. The financial due diligence team runs their workstream. The legal team runs theirs. The technology team runs theirs. Nobody synthesises the findings into a single operating picture that tells the buyer whether this acquisition will work once the bankers leave.

Fractional executives solve this because each one has run a function, not reviewed one. A fractional CFO who has built a finance function from scratch sees different risks in a target's numbers than a Big Four analyst who has only audited them. A fractional Chief IP Officer who has restructured patent portfolios sees licensing value that a law firm's standard patent review misses. An AI operations lead who has shipped production systems sees [deployment gaps](/insights/ai-deployment-operations-gaps/) that a technology consultant's slide deck glosses over.

## What does a fractional executive M&A team look like?

The team has three roles, each covering a due diligence workstream and an integration planning workstream. The engagement runs 90 days: 60 days of due diligence, 30 days of integration planning that overlaps with deal close. Total cost for a mid-market acquisition typically runs $75,000 to $150,000, compared to $400,000 to $800,000 for a full advisory stack.

**The Fractional CFO: financial due diligence and integration.** Covers quality of earnings, working capital analysis, revenue quality, customer concentration, normalised EBITDA adjustments, and the financial model that determines your offer price. Post-close, the fractional CFO builds the combined reporting structure, integrates the chart of accounts, and runs the first three monthly closes. If you have read [what kills deals in financial due diligence](/insights/financial-due-diligence-kills-deals/), you know the top three: inconsistent revenue recognition, hidden working capital needs, and unreconciled intercompany transactions. A fractional CFO catches all three because they have built the systems that produce, and sometimes hide, these numbers.

**The Fractional Chief IP Officer: IP due diligence and portfolio consolidation.** Covers the full [acquirer IP due diligence checklist](/insights/ip-due-diligence-for-acquirers/): patent ownership chain, freedom-to-operate analysis, trade secret documentation, open-source compliance, and licensing obligations. The question most buyers miss: does the target's IP transfer clean in the transaction? Hayat Amin's IP Defensibility 7-Point Test runs on every target portfolio Beyond Elevation evaluates, and in roughly 60% of cases it surfaces at least one ownership gap that a law firm's standard review would have missed.

**The AI Operations Lead: technology due diligence and system integration.** Covers the production stack, technical debt assessment, infrastructure costs, vendor lock-in risks, and the realistic cost of integrating the target's technology with yours. This role has become essential since 2024 as more acquisitions are fundamentally about acquiring AI capabilities, proprietary data pipelines, or agentic workflow infrastructure. The AI ops lead also stress-tests whether the target's AI systems will survive the transition, a question that has killed more post-close integrations than any financial issue.

## How does the M&A Operator Sprint work for fractional executive teams?

Hayat Amin's M&A Operator Sprint is the 90-day framework that compresses the typical six-to-nine-month advisory-led process into three phases, each with defined deliverables and decision gates. The framework works because operators make decisions faster than committees. Beyond Elevation has used this sprint structure on every fractional executive M&A engagement since 2024.

**Phase 1: Target Validation (Days 1 to 20).** All three operators review the target simultaneously, each producing a risk scorecard within 20 days. The CFO delivers a preliminary quality of earnings. The CIPO delivers an IP ownership and risk map. The AI ops lead delivers a technology readiness assessment. At day 20 the team meets to synthesise findings into a single go/no-go recommendation. Roughly one in three targets fails Phase 1, saving the buyer $200,000 or more in aborted advisory fees.

**Phase 2: Deep Due Diligence (Days 21 to 60).** If the target passes Phase 1, each operator drills into their workstream. The CFO completes normalised financials and working capital modelling. The CIPO runs the full freedom-to-operate analysis and values the IP portfolio using the methods outlined in our guide to [IP valuation methods](/insights/ip-valuation-methods-explained/). The AI ops lead maps integration costs and identifies the 90-day technology integration path. Hayat Amin reminds founders that Phase 2 is where most deals should die, and that killing a bad deal at day 45 saves more money than closing it.

**Phase 3: Integration Preparation (Days 45 to 90).** Starting before close, each operator builds the integration playbook for their function. The CFO designs the combined reporting structure and cash management framework. The CIPO creates the [IP integration plan](/insights/post-acquisition-ip-integration-90-day-playbook/). The AI ops lead builds the system migration and data consolidation roadmap. By day 90 the buyer has three functional integration plans ready to execute from day one of ownership.

## When does the fractional executive M&A model break down?

This model works for acquisitions with an enterprise value between $3M and $50M where the buyer is a founder-led company, a private equity portfolio company, or a mid-market operator making their first or second acquisition. Below $3M, the cost of three operators may exceed the value they add, a single fractional CFO is usually enough. Above $50M, regulatory scrutiny and deal complexity typically require dedicated advisory resources.

The model also requires a buyer willing to give operators direct access to the target's management team, financial systems, and technology stack. Advisory-led processes gatekeep access through data rooms and controlled Q&A sessions. Operator-led diligence works best when the operators can sit with the target's finance team, walk through the codebase with the engineers, and interview the IP counsel directly.

Hayat Amin showed this in a recent engagement where the three-operator team identified $2.3M in unrecognised IP licensing revenue that the advisory team's standard financial review had categorised as one-off income. That reclassification changed the offer price by 15%, more than covering the entire cost of the fractional engagement.

## What does a fractional executive M&A engagement cost?

A 90-day, three-operator fractional executive M&A engagement typically costs between $75,000 and $150,000 all in. The breakdown: $25,000 to $50,000 for the fractional CFO, $25,000 to $50,000 for the fractional CIPO, and $25,000 to $50,000 for the AI operations lead. Each operator works two to three days per week during the engagement.

Compare this to the typical mid-market M&A advisory stack: $200,000 to $400,000 for a Big Four financial DD team, $100,000 to $200,000 for IP and technology counsel, plus investment banking fees of 1 to 2% of deal value. For a $20M acquisition, the full advisory cost can exceed $600,000. The three-operator fractional model delivers equivalent diligence quality for less than $150,000.

The cost advantage is not only about rates. Operators complete diligence faster because they know what to look for, they have run the functions they are evaluating. A fractional CFO who has built 15 finance functions knows within days whether a target's numbers are real. An IP strategist who has restructured patent portfolios knows within a week whether the IP transfers clean. Speed compresses timelines, reduces professional fees, and lowers the risk that the deal dies from diligence fatigue.

## FAQ

### Can a fractional executive handle M&A due diligence for a complex deal?

Yes, for mid-market acquisitions between $3M and $50M enterprise value. Fractional executives who have operated at the C-suite level bring the same analytical rigour as advisory teams, with the added advantage of operational context that pure advisors lack. For deals above $50M or in heavily regulated industries, fractional operators work best alongside a dedicated advisory team rather than replacing it.

### How long does a fractional executive M&A engagement take?

The standard engagement runs 90 days: 60 days of due diligence with 30 days of integration planning that overlaps with deal close. This is roughly half the timeline of a traditional advisory-led process because operators make decisions faster and need less ramp-up time on the target's operations.

### Do I still need an investment bank if I use fractional executives for M&A?

If you are buying, you typically do not need an investment bank for sourcing and negotiation on deals under $20M. The fractional executive team handles due diligence and integration. If you are selling, an investment bank handles the sell-side process and buyer outreach, while the fractional team prepares the company for buyer scrutiny, a process we cover in our [fractional CFO exit preparation](/insights/fractional-cfo-exit-preparation/) guide.

### What if I only need one operator, not three?

Start with the function that carries the most risk in your specific deal. For asset-heavy acquisitions, start with the fractional CFO. For IP-heavy acquisitions, AI companies, patent portfolios, data businesses, start with the fractional Chief IP Officer. For acquisitions built around technology, start with the AI operations lead. Add operators as the diligence reveals complexity that one person cannot cover alone.

### How is a fractional executive M&A team different from an M&A advisory firm?

An advisory firm evaluates a business from the outside using financial models, market comparables, and legal reviews. A fractional executive team evaluates a business from the inside out, each operator assesses the target's function the way they would run it, catching operational risks that financial models cannot surface. The result is due diligence that predicts post-close performance, not only pre-close valuation.
