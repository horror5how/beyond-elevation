# In a Downturn, the Winners Do Not Cut Executives. They Go Fractional.

A 2024 Deloitte survey found that 67% of companies that eliminated C-suite roles during a contraction rehired for the same position within 18 months — at an average recruitment cost of $240,000. The companies that won the downturn did not cut executive capability. They restructured it. Hayat Amin, who has operated as a fractional CFO through three separate market corrections, argues that the downturn is the single best moment to switch from a full-time executive model to a fractional one.

Fractional executives in a downturn are not a compromise. They are an upgrade. You get the same operator-grade capability — the same board-ready reporting, the same strategic decision-making, the same investor-fluent communication — at 30 to 40 percent of the loaded cost. No severance. No notice period. No equity dilution. And when the market turns, you scale up or swap in a full-time hire without a gap in coverage.

## Why Do Companies Cut Executives First in a Downturn?

Companies cut executives first because executive salaries are the largest single-line expense on the payroll that can be eliminated without touching revenue-generating headcount. A full-time CFO in London or New York costs £180,000 to £350,000 in base salary alone, plus bonus, equity, benefits and employer taxes — total loaded cost of £250,000 to £500,000 per year. Cutting that line delivers an immediate, visible P&L improvement that satisfies boards and investors.

The problem is what disappears with the savings. When you cut the CFO, you lose the person who manages your cash runway. You lose the person who renegotiates your debt covenants. You lose the person who presents credible financials to investors when you need a bridge round.

When you cut the Chief IP Officer, you lose the person protecting the intangible assets that account for over 90 percent of your enterprise value. According to Ocean Tomo, intangible assets represented 90% of the S&P 500's market capitalisation in 2024. Cutting the person who manages those assets to save a salary is arithmetic that only works if you ignore the balance sheet.

## What Does a Fractional Executive Cost During a Downturn?

A fractional executive typically costs between £3,000 and £12,000 per month, depending on the role, seniority and time commitment. That is 30 to 40 percent of the equivalent full-time loaded cost, with zero recruitment fees, zero severance obligations and no equity dilution. At Beyond Elevation, a fractional CFO engagement starts at two days per week — enough to run the close, manage the board pack and handle investor reporting — at a fraction of a full-time salary.

Here are the numbers side by side for a 12-month contraction period.

**Full-time CFO:** £300,000 loaded annual cost. If you cut at month 3 and rehire at month 15: £75,000 salary paid + £60,000 severance + £80,000 recruitment fee + 3 months without a finance leader = £215,000 spent and 90 days of strategic blind spot.

**Fractional CFO:** £8,000 per month × 12 months = £96,000. No severance. No recruitment fee. No gap in coverage. Scale to one day per week during the trough, back to three when activity picks up. Total saving over the full-time cut-and-rehire cycle: £119,000 — and you never lost the capability.

Hayat Amin's rule on downturn executive planning is blunt: the cost of losing strategic capability for 90 days always exceeds the salary you saved. Always.

## How Do Fractional Executives Perform Better in a Downturn?

Fractional executives outperform full-time hires in a downturn for three structural reasons, not because they are individually better operators. The model itself is built for volatile conditions.

**First: they have seen it before.** A fractional CFO who works across six companies has navigated six different cash crunches, six different board conversations about burn rate, six different debt renegotiations. A full-time CFO at one company has seen one. The pattern-recognition advantage is not marginal — it is the difference between a CFO who panics and one who has a playbook.

**Second: they are cost-variable, not cost-fixed.** When revenue drops 30 percent, a fractional executive's time commitment drops proportionally. You are buying days, not a salary. That means your executive cost flexes with your revenue — the exact behaviour that keeps a company solvent through a contraction. Full-time salaries do not flex. They sit on the P&L at the same number month after month until someone gets cut.

**Third: they have no political incentive to protect headcount.** A full-time executive builds a team. In a downturn, they protect that team — sometimes at the expense of the company. A fractional executive has no team to protect. Their incentive is aligned with the company's survival, not with the preservation of their department's budget. Hayat Amin reminds founders that the most expensive decision in a downturn is a CFO who defends a finance team of eight when the company needs a finance team of three wired to AI.

## What Is Hayat Amin's Executive Triage Framework?

Hayat Amin's Executive Triage Framework is a three-tier model for restructuring executive capability during a contraction. Beyond Elevation applies it in the first two weeks of every downturn engagement. It separates executive functions into three categories based on how they should be staffed when cash is constrained.

**Tier 1 — Keep full-time:** the CEO and any executive whose departure would trigger a customer or investor confidence crisis. This is usually one person, sometimes two. If cutting them would cause a key customer to review the relationship or an investor to call an emergency board meeting, they stay.

**Tier 2 — Switch to fractional:** every executive function that requires strategic capability but not daily presence. CFO, Chief IP Officer, AI Operations lead, COO. These roles need operator-grade judgment two to three days per week, not a full-time seat. Fractional replacements can start within two weeks and cost 30 to 40 percent of the full-time equivalent.

**Tier 3 — Automate or eliminate:** every function that a well-configured AI system or a junior operator can handle with fractional oversight. Month-end reconciliation, expense management, basic IP maintenance filings, standard reporting. These are not executive functions — they are processes that became executive functions because nobody automated them. The framework produces a typical outcome: one full-time executive, two to three fractional executives and a leaner operational layer underneath. Total executive spend drops 50 to 60 percent while capability stays at 90 percent or higher.

## How Do You Transition From Full-Time to Fractional Without Losing Institutional Knowledge?

The transition from a full-time executive to a fractional replacement takes 14 to 30 days when handled correctly. The risk is not capability — fractional executives are experienced operators who have onboarded into dozens of companies. The risk is knowledge transfer. Here is the process Beyond Elevation runs.

**Week 1: Documentation sprint.** The outgoing executive documents every recurring process, every key relationship, every board commitment and every open negotiation. This is not optional. If the outgoing executive cannot document their role in five days, the role was already dangerously person-dependent — and the downturn just exposed it.

**Week 2: Overlap and handover.** The fractional executive shadows the outgoing hire for the second week. They attend every meeting, review every report, meet every key stakeholder. The overlap costs one week of double salary. It saves months of ramp-up time.

**Week 3 to 4: Independent operation with safety net.** The fractional executive runs the function independently. The outgoing executive is available on call for two weeks to answer questions. After that, the transition is complete.

## When Is Going Fractional the Wrong Move in a Downturn?

Going fractional is the wrong move when the company needs a full-time executive presence to maintain external confidence. If you are mid-fundraise and your lead investor has a personal relationship with your CFO, replacing them with a fractional hire mid-process will spook the deal. If your company is in regulated financial services and the regulator requires a named, full-time compliance officer, fractional is not an option for that specific seat.

It is also wrong when the problem is not cost — it is performance. Replacing a bad full-time executive with a fractional one changes the cost structure but does not fix the strategic gap. The fractional hire needs a clear mandate and measurable outcomes, not just a smaller invoice.

Hayat Amin says the diagnostic is simple: if you would rehire for this role within 12 months at the same seniority, go fractional now. If the role should not exist at all, eliminate it. The only wrong answer is cutting capability you will need and paying double to get it back.

## FAQ

### How quickly can a fractional executive start?

Most fractional executives can begin within one to two weeks of engagement. At Beyond Elevation, operators maintain capacity for rapid deployment because downturn engagements are inherently time-sensitive. There is no three-month recruitment cycle.

### Can a fractional executive handle a board meeting?

Yes. Fractional CFOs and Chief IP Officers routinely present to boards, run audit committee meetings and handle investor Q&A. The board does not need a full-time employee — it needs a credible operator with current numbers and a clear narrative. Fractional executives deliver both.

### What happens when the downturn ends?

When activity picks up, you have three options: increase the fractional executive's time commitment, convert them to a full-time role if the fit is right, or use them to recruit and onboard a permanent replacement. The flexibility is the point — you are not locked into a structure that made sense during the contraction but does not fit the recovery.

### Is a fractional executive a consultant?

No. A consultant advises. A fractional executive operates. They own the function, run the team, sign off on the numbers and answer to the board. The difference is accountability: a consultant delivers a deck, a fractional executive delivers a result.
