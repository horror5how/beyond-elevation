# Fractional Executives for Scale-Ups: Why the £2M to £20M Stage Needs Operators, Not Advisers

Scale-ups between £2M and £20M revenue face the most expensive leadership gap in business. They have outgrown advisers and accountants, but three full time C-suite hires would cost a combined £450K to £600K in annual salary before equity and benefits. Fractional executives close this gap by embedding proven operators 2 to 3 days per week at 30 to 40 per cent of the full time cost.

According to Beauhurst's 2026 UK Scaleup Index, 78 per cent of companies between £2M and £20M revenue report senior leadership gaps as their primary barrier to the next funding round. Hayat Amin, who has served as a fractional CFO and Chief IP Officer through exits with American Express and TripAdvisor, argues that the default response to this gap is wrong. "Most founders at £5M revenue hire an adviser or a coach. They need an operator who closes the books, files the patents, and builds the function," says Amin. "An adviser tells you what to do. An operator does it."

## What is the scale-up leadership gap?

The scale-up leadership gap is the period between £2M and £20M revenue where a company's operational complexity outpaces its management team, but its budget cannot support a C-suite hired full time. A CFO on a permanent contract costs £150,000 to £200,000 a year. A Chief IP Officer costs £140,000 to £180,000. An AI Operations lead costs £120,000 to £160,000. Combined, that's nearly half a million pounds in fixed cost before a single deliverable ships.

Most scale-ups at this stage have 20 to 80 employees, a board that expects professional-grade reporting, and investors asking questions the founding team cannot yet answer. The gap isn't about intelligence, founders at the scale-up stage are sharp. The problem is that financial modelling, IP portfolio management, and AI workflow automation take ten or more years of operating experience to master. You can't learn them between product sprints. You either hire the experience or fly blind through the most capital-intensive phase of the company.

## Why do advisers fail at the scale-up stage?

Advisers fail at the scale-up stage because they deliver insight without execution. Telling a scale-up its month-end close is too slow doesn't fix it, someone has to rewire the close down to two days. A recommendation to "consider an IP audit" is just as thin, someone has to run the audit, value the portfolio, and map the filing roadmap to the next raise.

Hayat Amin's rule for scale-up founders is direct: if the person you hired cannot point to a deliverable they personally built in the last 90 days, they are not an operator. "I have seen scale-ups with three advisory board members and zero board packs," Amin says. "The advisory fees totalled more than a fractional CFO would have cost for the full year."

That doesn't mean all advice is useless. Strategic advisers add value once the execution layer already works. But at the scale-up stage, execution is the strategy. Close the month in two days, get the IP audited and valued, automate the finance reconciliation, and you close the round. Keep four advisers and a messy cap table instead, and you're still fundraising six months later.

## Which fractional roles close the gap first?

Three fractional roles close the scale-up gap faster than any other hire: the fractional CFO, the fractional Chief IP Officer, and the AI Operations operator. The order depends on what the next 12 months demand, but most scale-ups preparing for a raise should hire the [fractional CFO](/insights/fractional-cfo-cost-2026/) first, because investors audit the numbers before they audit anything else.

**Fractional CFO.** Cost: £4,000 to £8,000 a month for 2 to 3 days a week. Deliverables in the first 90 days: a clean month-end close under 5 days, a 13-week cash flow forecast, a board pack investors can read, and a financial model tied to the raise. Recruiting a CFO as a permanent hire takes 3 to 6 months, plus 3 months to onboard. A fractional CFO starts in week one because they've done this at the same revenue stage a dozen times before.

**Fractional Chief IP Officer.** Cost: £3,500 to £7,000 a month. Deliverables: a full IP audit, a patent filing roadmap aligned to the product plan, trade secret documentation, and a licensing strategy if the portfolio has monetisable claims. Companies with patents are 10.2 times more likely to secure early-stage funding. At the scale-up stage, an IP strategy either multiplies the valuation or sits dormant in a drawer, and which one depends on how it's structured.

**AI Operations Operator.** Cost: £3,500 to £7,000 per month. Deliverables: an automation audit of the back office, an AI agent rollout for the first 3 to 5 high-volume manual processes, and a measurement framework that proves ROI before month 4. Hayat Amin's [fractional C-suite model](/insights/run-company-on-fractional-executives/) treats AI Operations as the force multiplier underneath the CFO and CIPO. "The AI Operations operator is the one who makes the other two roles cost even less," Amin says, "because half the work the CFO and CIPO used to do manually is now automated."

## How should a scale-up structure a fractional engagement?

A scale-up should structure a fractional engagement around outcomes, not hours. The most effective model is a 6-month retained engagement at a fixed monthly fee, with a defined deliverable list agreed in week one. Beyond Elevation structures every fractional engagement around what Hayat Amin calls the Scale-Up Operator Readiness Test: five binary questions that determine whether the company's finance, IP, and operations functions are ready for the next raise, board meeting, or acquisition conversation.

**The five questions:**

1. Can you close the month in under 5 business days?
2. Is every piece of protectable IP documented, filed, or formally protected as a trade secret?
3. Do you have a 13-week rolling cash forecast updated weekly?
4. Can you name every manual process that runs more than 10 hours per month?
5. Does your board pack exist, and was it sent on time last quarter?

If the answer to more than two of those questions is no, the scale-up is not operationally ready. A fractional engagement should start with the question that carries the highest downside risk. For most pre-raise companies, that is question 3 (cash forecast) followed by question 1 (month-end close).

The cadence that works: 2 days a week on-site or embedded in the team's tools, a weekly 30-minute standup with the founder, and a report for the board every month. Fractional doesn't mean absent, the best fractional operators become indistinguishable from the team within 14 days. [The onboarding matters more than the contract](/insights/fractional-executive-onboarding-14-day-playbook/).

## What does a successful fractional scale-up engagement look like?

A successful engagement has three markers by month 6: a function that did not exist before now runs without the founder, a measurable financial or operational improvement is documented, and the company is closer to its next milestone than it was 6 months ago. At Beyond Elevation, the baseline target is a 2x return on the fractional fee within the engagement period.

The numbers are straightforward. A fractional CFO at £6,000 a month costs £36,000 over 6 months. If that CFO cuts the month-end close from 15 days to 2, puts in cash flow forecasting that prevents a single missed payroll event, and builds the board pack that helps close a £3M round, that's an 80x return, not 2x. Hayat Amin reminds founders that the real comparison isn't fractional versus a permanent hire. "The real comparison is fractional versus nothing," Amin says. "Most scale-ups at £5M are running finance on a spreadsheet and IP on hope. The fractional isn't a discount hire, it's the first real operator in the building."

## FAQ

### How many days per week does a fractional executive work at a scale-up?

Most fractional engagements at the scale-up stage run 2 to 3 days per week. The exact cadence depends on the function and the urgency. A fractional CFO preparing for a raise may increase to 4 days per week in the 6 weeks before the round opens. The time commitment typically decreases as systems and processes mature.

### Can a scale-up afford more than one fractional executive at the same time?

Yes. Two fractional executives at £5,000 a month each cost £120,000 a year combined. One CFO hired permanently at the same seniority costs £180,000 to £200,000 before benefits and equity. The fractional model lets scale-ups buy depth across multiple functions for less than the cost of one senior hire.

### When should a scale-up replace a fractional executive with a permanent hire?

The trigger is sustained volume, not capability. Once the workload consistently needs 4 or more days a week for 3 consecutive months, the function has matured enough to justify hiring someone permanent. The fractional operator should build the job description, hire their replacement, and manage the transition. That handover is part of the engagement.

### What is the difference between a fractional executive and a consultant for a scale-up?

A consultant advises, a fractional executive executes. A consultant produces a report, a fractional executive closes the books. That distinction matters because scale-ups at the £2M to £20M stage don't lack analysis, they lack someone who'll build the function, manage the team, and own the outcome. [Beyond Elevation](https://beyondelevation.com) places fractional operators who run the work, not review it.

### Do fractional executives work with the existing team or replace them?

Fractional executives work with the existing team and build it up, they don't replace junior staff. They add the senior operating layer that directs, trains, and professionalises the function. A fractional CFO turns a bookkeeper into a finance team. A fractional Chief IP Officer turns an ad hoc patent process into a documented IP strategy. The team gets stronger, not smaller.
