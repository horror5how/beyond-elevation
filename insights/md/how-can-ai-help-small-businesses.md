# How Can AI Help Small Businesses? Nine Jobs It Actually Does, Priced

AI helps a small business by taking over repeating jobs: moving data between apps, answering the same customer question at midnight, keeping the books, drafting routine writing, booking meetings, taking notes, and running the email list. Every one of those has named software behind it and a price printed on the vendor's own website.

This piece is that list. Nine tools, ranked by how much of an owner's week each one gives back per dollar, with the price read from the vendor's own pricing page on 12 September 2026, and, for each, the kind of business it is wrong for.

The reason to be this specific is that most small American companies still are not doing any of it. The United States Census Bureau's Business Trends and Outlook Survey, reported on 26 May 2026, found overall AI use among American firms hovering between 17 and 20 percent from December 2025 to May 2026. Thirty seven percent of firms with at least 250 employees said they used AI. Fewer than 20 percent of firms with four or fewer employees did. Over that same window use rose among firms with at least twenty employees and did not change significantly among firms with fewer than twenty. The gap is widening, and it is not a gap in software. It is a gap in knowing which jobs to hand over.

## How this list was ranked

Four tests, applied in order. First, the vendor publishes a price on its own website, with no sales call and no quote form. Second, a company of under fifty people can start on it this week without hiring an engineer. Third, the AI inside it does a job the owner is currently doing by hand, rather than making an existing screen prettier. Fourth, it is sold and supported in the United States.

Rank is by how much of an owner's week the job gives back per dollar, judged from the published price and the setup the tool actually needs. Every price below was read from the vendor's own pricing page on 12 September 2026 and is the United States price. Prices move. Read the page before you buy, and treat anything here older than a quarter as a starting point rather than a quote.

## 1. Move data between apps so nobody retypes it: Zapier

**Good at:** carrying a record from one system to the next on a trigger, so a form fill becomes a customer record becomes an invoice becomes a calendar entry, with nobody typing the same name four times. This is first on the list because it is the only job here that makes every other tool you own worth more. The free plan is enough to find out whether the job is real before you pay for it.

**Price on 12 September 2026:** free at 100 tasks a month with two step workflows. Professional starts at 19.99 dollars a month billed annually for 750 tasks, or 29.99 dollars billed monthly, and climbs with volume: 39.00 dollars annually at 1,500 tasks, 89.00 dollars at 5,000, 129.00 dollars at 10,000. Team starts at 69.00 dollars a month billed annually at 2,000 tasks and covers 25 users. AI by Zapier and AI fields sit on the Professional plan.

**Wrong for:** a business whose data already lives in one system. If everything runs inside a single platform, you would be paying a toll to cross a road you do not have.

## 2. Answer the repeat question at eleven at night: Intercom Fin

**Good at:** answering in writing the questions your customers ask over and over, at any hour, and handing the strange ones to a person. Fin is the clearest example on this list of an AI agent rather than an AI feature, because Intercom charges for the outcome instead of the seat.

**Price on 12 September 2026:** 0.99 dollars per Fin outcome. Intercom counts an outcome when a customer confirms the issue is resolved, or does not ask for more help after Fin responds, or Fin completes a workflow. Human seats are billed separately: Essential at 29 dollars per seat per month, Advanced at 85 dollars, Expert at 132 dollars.

**Wrong for:** low volume, high value work. If you take eight enquiries a week and each one is worth twenty thousand dollars, a person should answer all eight, and the ninety nine cents you saved will be the most expensive dollar you ever spent.

## 3. Keep the books current: QuickBooks Online

**Good at:** categorising transactions, chasing what is owed, and answering a question about your own numbers without an export to a spreadsheet. Intuit now sells the AI layer as Intuit Intelligence, listed across the plans with expert guided setup, an AI chat for instant insights, and separate accounting, payments, sales tax, customer, project management and finance tools.

**Price on 12 September 2026:** Simple Start 38 dollars a month, Essentials 85 dollars, Plus 140 dollars, Advanced 340 dollars. A fifty percent discount for the first three months was running on all four when the page was read.

**Wrong for:** a company with a bookkeeper it trusts and a ledger that already works. Migrating your books to reach a chat window is an expensive way to ask a question.

## 4. Draft the writing nobody wants to do: Google Workspace with Gemini

**Good at:** the first draft. The quote, the follow up, the job advertisement, the policy nobody has written. What changed the arithmetic for small teams is that the Gemini assistant in Gmail now sits on the entry plan rather than being sold as a separate add on.

**Price on 12 September 2026:** annual billing list prices of 7.00 dollars per user per month for Starter, 14.00 dollars for Standard and 22.00 dollars for Plus, with a promotional discount displayed on the page when it was read. Starter is listed with the Gemini assistant in Gmail and basic access to the Gemini app. Standard and Plus list the assistant in Gmail, Docs, Meet and more, with expanded access.

**Wrong for:** a company standardised on Microsoft. Never move your email to reach an assistant.

## 5. Think through the work that is not routine: Claude

**Good at:** the judgement work. Reading a long contract and telling you what in it is unusual, working a pricing decision through to a number, writing the thing that has to be right. It is not a feature inside another product. It is the seat you sit in when the answer is not on a form.

**Price on 12 September 2026:** Pro at 20 dollars per month billed monthly, or 17 dollars billed annually. Team at 25 dollars per seat per month billed monthly, or 20 dollars annually, with a premium seat at 125 dollars monthly or 100 dollars annually. Max starts at 100 dollars per month.

**Wrong for:** an owner who wants the work done without being in the room. This is a tool you operate, not a process that runs, and its value tracks the quality of your questions.

**Disclosure:** Beyond Elevation builds most of its client work on Claude. That is a reason to check this entry's placement rather than take it on trust.

## 6. End the four email exchange before every meeting: Calendly

**Good at:** letting someone book time without asking you twice what you are free. Calendly names two AI features on its pricing page: Callie, in beta, an assistant that schedules over email, and Notetaker, which produces meeting recaps and pre drafted follow up emails. Both are add ons to the paid plans.

**Price on 12 September 2026:** a free plan with no per seat charge. Standard at 10 dollars per seat per month billed monthly, with 17 percent off on annual billing. Teams at 16 dollars per seat per month billed monthly, with up to 20 percent off annually. Enterprise starts at 15,000 dollars a year with a fifty seat minimum.

**Wrong for:** a business whose customers phone. Nobody opens a scheduling link at the moment the pipe bursts.

## 7. Remember what was agreed: Otter.ai

**Good at:** giving a small company a record of its own decisions. The value is not the transcript. It is that the person who was not in the room can read what was decided without asking anyone to recall it.

**Price on 12 September 2026:** Basic free with 300 transcription minutes a month. Pro at 16.99 dollars per user per month billed monthly, or 8.33 dollars billed annually, with 1,200 in app recording minutes. Business at 30 dollars per user per month billed monthly, or 19.99 dollars annually, with unlimited meetings and in app recordings.

**Wrong for:** a team that does not read. A transcript nobody opens is a subscription with a nice interface.

## 8. Run the storefront: Shopify Sidekick

**Good at:** the store questions an owner would otherwise send to a developer. Change this, explain why that dropped, write these product descriptions. Shopify describes Sidekick as built into every store and lists it, with millions of tokens, on all four standard plans, so the assistant is not a separate line on the bill.

**Price on 12 September 2026:** Basic at 39 dollars a month billed monthly or 29 dollars annually. Grow at 105 dollars monthly or 79 annually. Advanced at 399 dollars monthly or 299 annually. Plus starts at 2,300 dollars a month. The page also lists an Agentic offering at 0 dollars a month for selling in AI channels, where the merchant pays only when a sale is made.

**Wrong for:** a services business. There is no storefront to run.

## 9. Send the right message to the list you own: Klaviyo

**Good at:** turning a customer list into repeat revenue instead of a spreadsheet nobody touches. The free tier covers up to 250 profiles, 500 emails a month and 5 dollars of mobile messages a month, which is enough to learn whether your list responds before it costs anything. The AI features are named on the page as Composer, with 5 dollars of Composer usage included on the free tier, and a Customer Agent.

**Price on 12 September 2026:** free up to 250 profiles. Paid pricing scales with the size of your list and was presented on the page as a calculated total rather than a fixed plan price, so price your own tier before committing to it. No fixed paid figure is quoted here because none was published as a plan price when the page was read.

**Wrong for:** a business without a list. Klaviyo multiplies an audience, and a multiplier on zero is zero.

## What did not make the list, and why

Four names were in the candidate pool and are not in the ranking.

Canva and Gusto both failed the first test on a technicality worth stating plainly: their pricing pages refused an automated read on 12 September 2026, so no price from either was verified this run. An unverified price does not get printed here, and both are worth checking yourself.

Dialpad's AI Agent failed the same test on substance. Dialpad sells it on a pool of AI Agent credits and states that credits are used only when AI delivers value, with no published per user price. That is a defensible way to sell a voice agent. It is not a number you can read before a sales call, so it is not ranked.

HubSpot was cut for a different reason. The marketing plans were legible, but the entry tier price read inconsistently between billing periods when the page was fetched, and a figure we cannot stand behind does not go into a list whose only value is that the figures are real.

## Frequently asked questions

### How can I automate my business?

Start with the handoff that costs you the most hours this month, not with the most impressive tool. In most small companies that handoff is data moving between two systems by hand, which is entry one on this list and free to test at 100 tasks a month. Automate one job, measure the hours it returns, and only then buy the second. Owners who begin with a platform rather than a job tend to own a platform.

### What is an AI agent for small business?

An agent is software given a goal, the tools to act, and permission to finish the job, rather than a chat window that describes what you should do. Intercom's Fin is the clearest case here because it is billed per resolved outcome. Klaviyo names a Customer Agent, Calendly's Callie schedules over email, and Shopify's Sidekick acts inside the store. The test an owner can apply: does it complete the work, or does it hand you a paragraph about the work?

### How do you build AI agents for small businesses?

For most owners the answer is that you do not build one, you configure one that already exists, because the agents above arrive with the integrations and the escalation paths already written. Building is worth it when the job is specific to your business and no vendor sells it: a quoting rule nobody else has, a scheduling constraint particular to your trade, a dataset that only you hold. That is the point at which an engineer inside the business earns the fee, and it is the work described on the [forward deployed engineering page](/fde).

### Is AI consulting for small businesses worth it?

It depends entirely on what is delivered at the end. A recommendation deck about AI is worth very little to a company of twelve people, because the constraint was never the idea. Something running in production, built in your own systems, with somebody answerable for it when it breaks, is worth a great deal. Ask any firm you call which of the two you are buying, and get the answer before the invoice.

### How can I use AI for small business marketing?

In this order: send better messages to the list you already own, then draft faster, then generate. The list comes first because it is the only audience you control, which is entry nine. Drafting comes second, and the assistant in an email suite you already pay for will cover most of it. Generating new creative comes last, because volume without an audience produces nothing but cost.

## Where to start this week

Take the nine jobs above and mark the one that consumed the most of your own hours in the last month. Buy that one, on the smallest published plan, and leave the other eight alone until the first is working and you can name the hours it returned. The failure pattern in small companies is not choosing the wrong tool, it is buying six at once and configuring none.

If the job that comes up is bigger than a subscription, which happens when the work sits across several systems and none of them talk, that is a forward deployment problem rather than a software purchase.
