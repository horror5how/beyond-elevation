# How is AI used in accounting? 8 jobs it already does in a small business

AI is used in accounting to do the repeat work in the books: categorising bank transactions, reading receipts and bills, coding supplier invoices, matching card spend, reconciling accounts, chasing payment, classifying sales tax and answering questions about cash. A person still signs the numbers off. Below are the eight jobs it already does in a small business, with the product and price for each.

Hayat Amin has spent twenty years as a technology chief financial officer and sold three companies, so he has read a lot of ledgers kept by hand and knows which parts a machine now does better. Every price here was read on the vendor's own page on 6 October 2026, in US dollars, and where a claim is the vendor's own we say so.

## how much of the bookkeeping is AI work already

Intuit surveyed more than 2,200 US businesses with up to 100 employees for its April 2025 QuickBooks Small Business Insights. 68 percent said they now use AI regularly, up from 48 percent the summer before, and 28 percent use it daily. Bookkeeping was the fifth most common use at 29 percent, behind marketing at 43 percent, customer service at 36, admin at 33 and data processing at 32. Intuit sells accounting software, so read it as a seller's survey. It's still the biggest US read on owners we found.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics counts 1,532,400 bookkeeping, accounting and auditing clerks in the United States in 2025, at a median of $50,670 a year, or $24.36 an hour. It projects the job to shrink 6 percent to 2035, a loss of 85,600, and gives the reason plainly: "Software innovations have automated many of the tasks performed by bookkeeping, accounting, and auditing clerks." The same agency expects accountants and auditors to grow 5 percent, to add 79,400 jobs, and says AI "is not expected to reduce overall demand" for them. The keying is going. The judgement isn't.

## how we picked the eight

A job made the list if it happens every week or every month in a small company's books, if a named product does it with AI for a price published on its own site, and if an owner can switch it on without an engineer. We ordered them the way the work flows through a month, from the transaction landing to the cash forecast. Nobody paid for a place. Beyond Elevation isn't one of the eight, and it appears once, after them, with the affiliation stated.

## 1. categorising bank transactions

Every card swipe and bank payment needs an account code before your profit and loss means anything. This is the job AI took first. QuickBooks Online lists "Categorizes expenses" on its Simple Start plan at $38 a month, and on Plus at $140 a month adds "Transactions auto-categorized and verified". Digits, a newer ledger built around AI, sells "24/7 AI Bookkeeping & Reconciliation" from $65 a month on Essentials and $100 on Core, with no per seat fees, and says it "grows smarter with every categorization".

Wrong for you if nobody ever reviews the coding. The software learns from your corrections, so a year of unreviewed guesses teaches it the wrong habits. Fifteen minutes a week reviewing the flagged ones is the job that stays human.

## 2. reading receipts and bills

The paper used to be the slow part. Dext takes a receipt or bill by email, WhatsApp or desktop upload and says it captures and categorises them "with over 99% accuracy", then syncs the result to your accounting software. Its US base plan is $25.21 a month billed annually, $302.50 a year, for 5 users and 250 documents a month. Dext says over 700,000 businesses and 12,000 accounting and bookkeeping firms use it.

Wrong for you if you already run spend through a card that collects the receipt at the till. Then you're paying twice for the same capture.

## 3. coding and paying supplier bills

A supplier invoice with twelve lines across four cost codes is where a bookkeeper's afternoon goes. BILL's Invoice Coding Agent "dynamically extracts and codes multi-line bills based on previous coding behavior", which BILL says cuts manual time by 20 percent. Its W-9 Agent emails your vendors to collect and enter W-9s, and BILL puts that at over 80 percent of the manual steps removed. Accounts payable plans are $49 a user a month on Essentials, $65 on Team and $89 on Corporate.

Wrong for you if you pay six suppliers a month. At that volume, the bill pay inside your accounting software will do.

## 4. coding card spend and matching it to the books

Ramp says its agents "automatically extract, and categorize every expense and invoice", and that an approval agent "Approves when it's safe. Escalates when it's not." Its Plus plan lists "AI coding for every field" at $15 per user per month plus a platform fee based on team size, with 20 percent off for annual billing. The vendor's own customer stories put Glossier at 90 percent of transactions coded automatically and Webflow at 75 percent less time on card reconciliation. Those are Ramp's numbers about Ramp's customers.

Wrong for you if your team pays on personal cards and claims it back later. Ramp is a corporate card first.

## 5. reconciling the accounts and closing the month

Reconciliation is matching every line in the ledger to the bank statement, and closing is doing that for every account before you trust the month. QuickBooks Plus at $140 a month lists "Reconciles your books" and "Finds and fixes anomalies". If you'd rather not own the job at all, Pilot sells bookkeeping where "AI categorizes transactions, reconciles accounts, and closes books monthly", with Essentials at $99 a month for up to $100,000 in monthly expenses, and a US based bookkeeper on its Core plan.

Wrong for you if your month has inventory, deferred revenue or work in progress. Those entries need a person who knows the business, and no product above claims to do them alone.

## 6. getting invoices out and getting paid

Intuit says businesses using its Payments Agent get paid "an average of 5 days faster", and that its agents save "up to 12 hours a month". Both are Intuit's figures, from its July 2025 announcement. On the pricing page, Essentials at $85 a month lists "Creates invoices in bulk, automatically" in beta and "Gets you paid faster" with Payments AI. Xero's assistant, JAX, can create invoices from earlier quotes, on plans of $27, $59 and $97 a month after the introductory offer.

Wrong for you if your invoices depend on timesheets or job costs kept in another system. The AI can't bill what it can't see, and that's a join, not a feature.

## 7. working out sales tax

A business shipping into several states faces more than 12,000 US sales and use tax jurisdictions, by Avalara's count. Avalara's AvaTax sells "AI-powered item classification" and claims "99.999% Accuracy in tax calculations" across more than 54 billion transactions a year. Avalara Select, its sales tax and returns plans, start at $69 a month, and its calculation and returns package starts at $699. QuickBooks Plus adds a check that will "Catch discrepancies pre-filing" with Sales Tax AI.

Wrong for you if you sell in one state. Your accounting software's built in tax rate will cover it.

## 8. forecasting cash and answering questions about it

This is the job an owner feels most and checks least. Xero's JAX answers plain questions like "What was my total income in the last six months?" QuickBooks Plus lets you "Chat to plan scenarios and save as budgets", in beta. For a proper rolling forecast, Float builds 12 month forecasts on Essentials at $105 a month billed annually or $130 monthly, and 36 month forecasts from $215. Float's pricing page doesn't sell itself as AI. It's on the list because a forecast fed by AI coded books is only as good as the coding underneath, and Float reads straight from Xero and QuickBooks.

Wrong for you if you won't open it. A forecast nobody reads on Monday changes no decision.

## where a person still matters

Every product above learns from history, so it repeats yesterday's mistakes with confidence. Someone has to own the exceptions, the year end adjustments and the questions from your accountant. Karbon's State of AI in Accounting 2026, a survey of nearly 600 accounting professionals by a vendor that sells practice software, found 98 percent of firms using AI but only 21 percent with a documented AI policy. In a small company that policy is one page: which accounts the AI may code alone, what goes to a person, and who looks at the flagged list each Friday.

The eight jobs also live in eight places. Receipts in Dext, bills in BILL, cards in Ramp, the ledger in QuickBooks, the forecast in Float. Each one is good at its job and none of them can see the others. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can AI do accounting?

It can do most of the bookkeeping. Categorising transactions, reading receipts, coding bills, reconciling bank accounts and drafting invoices are all sold as AI features today by QuickBooks, Xero, Dext, BILL, Ramp and Digits. It can't take responsibility for the numbers, make the year end judgement calls or sign a tax return. Plan on AI doing the keying and a person reviewing the exceptions.

### Can AI do accounting for me?

For a small company, mostly yes, with a weekly review. QuickBooks Plus at $140 a month lists "Reconciles your books" and auto categorised, verified transactions. If you want a person behind it, Pilot pairs AI bookkeeping with a US based bookkeeper on its Core plan, and its Essentials plan is $99 a month for up to $100,000 in monthly expenses. Keep your accountant for tax.

### Can AI do accounting jobs?

It's doing the clerk's share. The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects bookkeeping, accounting and auditing clerks to fall 6 percent between 2025 and 2035, a loss of 85,600 jobs, and names software as the reason. It projects accountants and auditors to grow 5 percent over the same years, about 115,300 openings a year.

### Will AI make accountants obsolete?

Not on the government's numbers. The Bureau of Labor Statistics expects 79,400 more accountants and auditors by 2035 and says new technology "is not expected to reduce overall demand" for them. The work shifts from entering numbers to checking and explaining them.

### How is AI used in accounting and finance?

In accounting it codes, captures and reconciles. In finance it forecasts and answers questions: Xero's JAX answers questions like "What was my total income in the last six months?", QuickBooks Plus plans scenarios by chat, and Intuit's Finance Agent is described as doing KPI analysis and forecasting. A forecasting tool like Float, from $105 a month, then turns the coded books into a 12 month cash view.

### How to use AI in accounting software?

Switch on the features you already pay for before you buy anything. Most QuickBooks Online and Xero plans now include AI categorisation and an assistant. Review the suggested categories every week for the first month so it learns your coding, then add one outside tool for the slowest job left, usually receipts or supplier bills.

### What is the best AI accounting for small business?

The one that matches your ledger. If you're on QuickBooks, its own AI on the Plus plan at $140 a month covers categorising, reconciling and anomalies. If you're on Xero, its assistant is JAX, and Xero's plans run from $27 a month. Add Dext at $25.21 a month for receipts or BILL from $49 a user a month for supplier bills only when the volume calls for it.
