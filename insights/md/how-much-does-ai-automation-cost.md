# How Much Does AI Automation Cost? The Four Lines on the Bill, Priced

AI automation costs a small business about 25 to 145 dollars a month to run for one process at 1,000 jobs a month, and 1,500 to 4,000 dollars to have built if the process is simple. The build is the big number. The software and the model are the small ones, and most quotes hide which is which.

Hayat Amin has spent twenty years as a technology chief financial officer and sold three companies, so he reads an automation quote the way a buyer reads a contract. He now builds this work inside companies himself. Every price below was read on the vendor's own page on 1 October 2026. If a figure is our arithmetic or our assumption, we say so next to it.

## the four lines on every automation bill

Every automation you pay for has four costs, whether the quote lists them or not. There's the build, which is somebody's time turning your process into steps. There's the platform, the subscription that runs those steps. There's the model, the per use charge when a step needs to read or write like a person. And there's upkeep, the hours each quarter when a supplier changes a form or a system updates its connection.

Ask for all four on one page before you compare two quotes. A 2,000 dollar build on a platform that costs 1,800 dollars a year is a dearer job than a 3,000 dollar build on one that costs 240.

## the platform, priced

These are the four platforms most small and mid sized companies in the United States end up on. The prices are the list prices on each pricing page this morning.

Zapier's Free plan runs 100 tasks a month and stops when you hit the limit. Professional starts at 19.99 dollars a month billed annually, or 29.99 monthly, for 750 tasks. The 5,000 task tier is 89 dollars a month annually and 133.50 monthly. Team starts at 69 dollars a month annually for 2,000 tasks. Go over with pay per task switched on and Zapier bills the extra at 1.25 times the base rate on annual plans and 2.5 times on monthly ones. That multiplier is the line people miss.

Make's Core plan is 12 dollars a month for 10,000 credits, Pro is 21 dollars and Teams is 38, each priced at 10,000 credits, with a free plan up to 1,000 credits. Annual billing saves 15 percent or more, according to Make.

n8n's Cloud Starter is 20 euros a month for 2,500 executions and Pro is 50 euros for 10,000. The Business plan is 667 euros a month, self hosted. The Community Edition is free on GitHub if you host it yourself, which moves the cost from a subscription to whoever keeps the server alive.

Microsoft's Power Automate Premium is 15 dollars per user per month, paid yearly. Unattended automation, the kind that runs with nobody logged in, is Power Automate Process at 150 dollars per bot per month, or 215 if Microsoft hosts the machine. If your company already lives in Microsoft 365 that's often the right answer, and it's priced per person rather than per task, so the arithmetic changes.

## the model, priced

The model is the cheapest line on the bill, and it's the one owners worry about most. Anthropic publishes Claude Haiku 4.5 at 1 dollar per million input tokens and 5 dollars per million output tokens, Sonnet 5.5 at 2 and 10, and Opus 5.5 at 4 and 20. OpenAI publishes gpt-5-mini at 0.25 dollars input and 2 dollars output, and gpt-5 at 1.25 and 10.

Take one job to make that real. Say your team reads 1,000 supplier invoices a month and types each one into QuickBooks. We'll assume each invoice is about 2,000 tokens going in and 500 coming out, which is our assumption for a one or two page invoice, not a vendor figure. That's 2 million input tokens and half a million output tokens a month.

On Haiku 4.5 that's 4.50 dollars a month. On Sonnet 5.5 it's 9 dollars. On gpt-5-mini it's 1.50. All three are our arithmetic off the published rates. If someone quotes you a four figure monthly "AI usage" line for a job this size, ask them to show you the token count.

## the build, priced

Nobody publishes a survey of what small companies pay for an automation build. The guides that exist are written by firms that sell the work, so treat them as asking prices.

Parix.ai, an automation agency, published its guide on 5 June 2026. It puts simple builds at 1,500 to 4,000 dollars, medium builds at 7,000 to 12,000, and custom AI work at 15,000 and up. It puts running costs at 5 to 15 percent of the setup cost per year.

Taskip, which sells management software to agencies rather than automation itself, updated its guide on 18 August 2026. It puts small business retainers at 500 to 1,500 dollars a month, and mid market work at 1,500 to 4,000 dollars a month plus 5,000 to 20,000 dollars of setup.

Beyond Elevation publishes its own prices too, so you can hold us to them. A two week AI audit is 3,000 dollars fixed. A project runs 8 to 14 weeks and starts from 30,000 dollars. Fractional work, one or two days a week, starts from 5,800 dollars a month. That's the top of the range on purpose. It's for companies joining four or five systems, not one Zap, and the client owns the code, the agents, the accounts and the documents when it ends.

## what the invoice job costs in total

Put the four lines together for the 1,000 invoice example. If the workflow takes three steps per invoice, that's 3,000 Zapier tasks, which lands on the 5,000 task Professional tier at 133.50 dollars billed monthly. Add Sonnet 5.5 at 9 dollars and the top of the range is about 142.50 dollars a month.

At the bottom, n8n's Starter covers 1,000 runs inside its 2,500 executions for 20 euros, and Haiku 4.5 adds 4.50 dollars. Call it 25 dollars a month. The build, on Parix's simple band, is 1,500 to 4,000 dollars once.

Now the other side of the scales. The Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the 2025 median pay for bookkeeping, accounting and auditing clerks at 24.36 dollars an hour. If each invoice takes three minutes to key in, which is our assumption and worth timing in your own office, 1,000 invoices is 50 hours a month, or 1,218 dollars at the median wage before payroll taxes and benefits.

At 4,000 dollars to build and 142.50 a month to run, that's under four months of those hours. At 1,500 to build and 25 a month to run, it's under six weeks. Both are our arithmetic, and both only become cash if the 50 hours go to work that earns something. Freed hours that turn into longer lunches save nothing.

## the costs that don't appear on the quote

Overage is the first. Zapier's 2.5 times multiplier on monthly plans means a busy month can cost far more than the tier price suggested, so put the job on annual billing once you know the volume.

Exceptions are the second. The quote prices the invoice that looks like every other invoice. The one with a handwritten credit note, the one in euros, the one from the supplier who changed their template in March, goes back to a person. Ask what share of cases the build handles on its own and what happens to the rest.

Ownership is the third. If the automation lives in the agency's Zapier account, under their keys, you're renting it. Leaving costs you a rebuild. Ask where the account sits before you sign.

Upkeep is the fourth. Parix's 5 to 15 percent a year is a fair rule. A retainer that costs more than that for a stable process is paying for hours nobody is working.

## when the cheap version is the right one

If the process sits inside one system and the rule fits on a page, don't hire anyone. Chasing late invoices, routing a web form to the right inbox, copying a closed deal into your accounting package. That's 20 dollars a month and a weekend, and we wrote the steps in [how to build AI agents for small businesses](/insights/how-to-build-ai-agents-for-small-businesses/).

Pay for a build when the job crosses three or more systems, when it has to write into your books rather than read from them, or when a mistake costs real money. That's where the hours go, and where a 4,000 dollar quote is cheaper than three weekends of your own. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much does AI automation cost?

For one process at about 1,000 jobs a month, 25 to 145 dollars a month to run and 1,500 to 4,000 dollars to build if it's simple. That's our arithmetic off n8n, Zapier, Anthropic and Parix.ai's published prices on 1 October 2026. Medium builds sit at 7,000 to 12,000 dollars on Parix's guide, and custom AI work starts at 15,000.

### How much do AI automation agencies charge?

Taskip's August 2026 guide puts small business retainers at 500 to 1,500 dollars a month, and mid market work at 1,500 to 4,000 a month plus 5,000 to 20,000 dollars of setup. Parix.ai prices single builds from 1,500 dollars. Both are asking prices published by firms in the trade, not a survey.

### How much does AI cost?

Less than people expect at small volume. Claude Haiku 4.5 is 1 dollar per million input tokens and 5 per million output, and gpt-5-mini is 0.25 and 2. Reading 1,000 invoices a month costs between 1.50 and 9 dollars on the models above, on our token assumption. The platform and the build cost more than the model.

### How much does Zapier cost per month?

Free for 100 tasks. Professional starts at 19.99 dollars a month billed annually or 29.99 monthly for 750 tasks, and the 5,000 task tier is 89 annually or 133.50 monthly. Team starts at 69 dollars a month annually. Overage on pay per task is 1.25 times the base rate on annual billing and 2.5 times on monthly.

### How much does it cost to build an AI system?

It depends on how many systems it joins. Parix.ai puts a simple build at 1,500 to 4,000 dollars and custom AI at 15,000 and up. A project at Beyond Elevation runs 8 to 14 weeks from 30,000 dollars, for companies connecting several systems rather than one.

### Is AI automation worth it in 2026?

It is when the hours it frees go somewhere useful. On our invoice example, 50 hours a month at the Bureau of Labor Statistics median of 24.36 dollars an hour is 1,218 dollars of time, against 25 to 142.50 dollars a month to run. Time the job in your own office before you trust anyone's payback figure, ours included.

### What should AI automation services pricing include?

Four lines, each priced separately: the build, the platform subscription, the model usage and the upkeep. Ask who owns the platform account and the keys, and what share of cases the build handles without a person. A quote that bundles all four into one monthly number is hard to compare and harder to leave.
