# How to Automate Administrative Tasks: the 9 to Hand Over First, With Prices

To automate administrative tasks, start with the ones that follow the same rule every week: logging expenses, booking meetings, sending and chasing invoices, entering bills and copying data between two systems. Pick one, connect the two products it moves between, and give the result a named owner. Most of the nine below cost under $50 a user a month.

Hayat Amin has spent twenty years as a technology chief financial officer and sold three companies, and the admin below is the work he now strips out of small and mid sized companies by building the automation himself. Every price in this piece was read on the vendor's own page on 2 October 2026, and where a sum is ours we say so.

## how much of the week admin takes

Time Etc, a virtual assistant firm that sells the alternative to doing it yourself, had Censuswide survey 251 US entrepreneurs between 22 and 28 September 2023. The average week was 45.5 hours, and more than a third of it, 36 percent, went on administrative tasks. In a typical week 59 percent logged expenses, 45 percent managed schedules, 44 percent created invoices and 43 percent did data entry. Read it as a seller's survey, because it is one. It's also the most specific list of US owner admin we could find, so we built the ranking around it.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics counts 3,515,600 secretaries and administrative assistants in the United States in 2025, at a median of $48,310 a year, or $23.23 an hour. It projects that number to fall 2 percent to 2035, a loss of 75,300 jobs, and gives the reason in plain words: technology lets staff prepare their own documents, and managers now do work that assistants used to do. The owner is doing that work now, and automation is how you stop.

## how we picked the nine

A task made the list if it repeats on a fixed rule, if a named product does it for a published price, and if a small company can switch it on in a week without an engineer. We ranked by how many owners do the task each week in the Time Etc numbers, then by how cheap the first step is. Prices are the list price on the vendor's page, in US dollars, read this run. ## 1. logging expenses and matching receipts

This is the admin task owners do most, 59 percent of them every week in the Time Etc survey, and it's the cheapest one to hand over. Ramp's free plan, at $0 per user per month, lists "Automatic receipt collection and matching", spend limits with accounting codes set in advance, and employee reimbursements. The Plus plan is $15 per user per month plus a platform fee based on team size, with 20 percent off for annual billing, and adds AI expense reviews and expenses completed over SMS or Slack.

Wrong for you if your team pays on personal cards and claims later. Ramp is a corporate card company first, so ask how it treats spend that never touched its card before you move everyone over.

## 2. booking meetings

Forty five percent of owners in the survey manage their own schedule every week. Calendly's free plan gives you one event type and one calendar connection, which is enough to kill the email back and forth on a single kind of call. Standard is $10 a seat a month billed monthly, 17 percent less billed yearly, and adds unlimited event types, multiple calendars and "Automations & reminders". Teams is $16 a seat and adds round robin scheduling and lead routing.

What saves time here is the reminder that stops a no show and the routing that sends a new enquiry to the right person without you reading it first, not the booking itself. If you run a sales team, Teams is the plan. If it's you and a calendar, free will do for months.

## 3. sending invoices and chasing them

Forty four percent of owners create invoices themselves each week, and the chasing is the part nobody enjoys. QuickBooks Online will send automatic reminders up to 90 days before or after the due date, three of them per invoice, with your own subject line and wording. One catch is in Intuit's own help article: QuickBooks sends a reminder "only for invoices you've already emailed". An invoice you printed or sent as a PDF from your own inbox won't get chased.

QuickBooks Online lists Simple Start at $38 a month for 1 user, Essentials at $85 for 3, Plus at $140 for 5 and Advanced at $340 for 25, with half off for the first 3 months. If you already pay for it, switching reminders on costs nothing extra. We'd do this one first.

## 4. entering and approving bills

Bills coming in are the mirror image of invoices going out, and in most companies we open up somebody is still keying them line by line. BILL's Essentials plan is $49 a user a month and covers bill entry, approval workflows, a central inbox for bills and W-9 collection. Team, at $65, adds a two way sync with QuickBooks Online and Xero and "AI multi-line bill coding". Corporate, at $89, adds purchase orders and two way matching.

Ramp's Plus plan does part of the same job, with line item coding and approval recommendations from what it calls its AP agent. If you already run Ramp cards, try that before you add BILL. If bills arrive from dozens of suppliers in dozens of formats, the $65 BILL plan is the one to price.

## 5. copying data from one system to another

Forty three percent of owners in the survey did data entry every week, and most of that is retyping: a form into the CRM, an order into the accounts, a new client into the project tool. This is where a connector earns its money. Zapier's free plan runs 100 tasks a month. Professional starts at $19.99 a month billed annually, or $29.99 monthly, for 750 tasks, and 2,000 tasks on the Team plan is $69 a month billed annually. Make's free plan gives 1,000 credits a month, and Core is $12 a month for 10,000 credits.

Count before you buy. Zapier's help pages say the trigger never uses a task and every successful action step uses one, so a form that writes to two systems costs two tasks, and a business taking 300 enquiries a month already needs the 750 tier. We cover the full arithmetic, including the cost of the AI model inside a workflow, in [how much AI automation costs](/insights/how-much-does-ai-automation-cost/).

## 6. running payroll

Payroll is admin with a legal deadline, and US payroll tax filing is the part an owner should never do by hand. QuickBooks Payroll's full service plans calculate, file and pay payroll taxes, and include "Auto payroll for salaried employees on direct deposit". Bundled with QuickBooks Online Simple Start, the list price is $88 a month plus $6.50 per employee. With Essentials it's $125 plus $6.50, and the Premium bundle with Plus is $203 plus $10 per employee.

At 10 salaried staff on the Simple Start bundle that's $153 a month, which is our sum off Intuit's published prices. Hourly staff are the hard case. Auto payroll covers salaried staff, so hourly timesheets still need approving before each run, and that approval chain is where the hours go.

## 7. sending contracts and collecting signatures

Engagement letters, offer letters and supplier contracts are admin because of the chasing, not the signing. DocuSign's Personal plan is $11 a month billed annually for 5 envelopes a month, with reusable templates and automatic reminders. Standard is $30 a user a month with shared templates and 100 envelopes per user per year. Business Pro, at $45, adds web forms, bulk send and payment collection, so a client can fill in the details, sign and pay in one link.

Watch the envelope cap. One hundred a user a year is under two a week. A firm sending a contract with every new job will hit it, so price it on volume.

## 8. writing up meetings

The notes and the follow up email after a call are admin every single time, and the follow up is the bit that gets dropped. Otter's Basic plan is free for 300 transcription minutes a month. Pro is $8.49 a user a month billed annually, or $16.99 monthly, for 1,200 minutes. Business is $19.99 billed annually, or $30 monthly, with unlimited meetings. Calendly also sells a notetaker as an add on to its Standard plan.

Wrong for you if your calls carry client confidential detail and you haven't read where the recording is stored. Read that page before you switch it on for a law or medical practice.

## 9. sorting the inbox and drafting replies

Email is where every task above starts, and the cheapest AI help for it is probably already on your bill. Google lists Gemini in Gmail on every Workspace plan, from Starter at $7 a user a month to Plus at $22. Drafting and summarising inside Gmail is the easy half. The hard half is getting the email to become a task with a date in the system that does the work, an invoice in QuickBooks or a bill in BILL, and that is a connector job from number 5, not a mail feature.

## what to add up before you start

Here is one stack for a single owner, with our arithmetic off the published prices: Ramp free at $0, Calendly Standard at $10 monthly, QuickBooks Online Essentials at $85, Zapier Professional at $19.99 billed annually and Otter Pro at $8.49 billed annually. That's $123.48 a month. At the Bureau of Labor Statistics median of $23.23 an hour for an administrative assistant, it pays for itself if it saves 5.3 hours a month, which is about 74 minutes a week.

Three limits are worth saying out loud. First, every tool above does its own task well and none of them talks to the others without a connector, so the gaps between them are where the admin goes next. Second, a rule you haven't written down can't be automated, and most small companies have never written down who approves a bill. Third, the price on the page is per user, so a 12 person team pays twelve times, and that changes which plan wins.

## Frequently asked questions

### What admin tasks can be automated?

Any task that follows a written rule and moves data between two systems. The nine with the clearest payback are expense receipts, meeting booking, invoice sending and chasing, bill entry, data entry between systems, payroll, contract signatures, meeting notes and inbox sorting. In the Time Etc and Censuswide survey of 251 US entrepreneurs, 59 percent logged expenses weekly, 45 percent managed schedules, 44 percent created invoices and 43 percent did data entry, so start with those four.

### How to use AI to automate administrative tasks?

Use AI for the step that needs reading, and a plain rule for everything else. Reading a receipt, coding a bill line or summarising a meeting is AI work, and Ramp, BILL and Otter already sell it inside their plans. Moving the result into your accounts is a rule, and a connector like Zapier or Make does that for $19.99 or $12 a month at the entry tier. An AI step costs more and fails less visibly, so keep it to the step that needs it.

### Will AI replace administrative assistants?

The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects secretaries and administrative assistants to fall 2 percent between 2025 and 2035, from 3,515,600 jobs, a loss of 75,300, and still expects about 314,400 openings a year. It names technology and managers doing their own admin as the reasons, and expects medical secretaries to grow. That's a slow decline, not a replacement. In a small company the assistant usually ends up owning the automations instead of the keying.

### What is the best AI for administrative tasks?

There isn't one product that does all nine, and the best one is whichever already sits on your bill. If you use Google Workspace, Gemini in Gmail is included from $7 a user a month. If you use QuickBooks Online, automatic invoice reminders are already there. For receipts, Ramp's free plan matches them. Buy a new product only for the task your current stack can't do.

### How to automate office work?

Write down one process from start to finish, with who does each step and which system it happens in. Then find the step where a person copies something from one screen to another, and automate that step first. In most small offices it's invoices or bills, because QuickBooks Online and BILL both publish the feature and the price. Do one a month. Twelve months later you have twelve fewer manual steps and nothing broke at once.

### What are examples of administrative tasks?

Logging expenses, managing calendars, creating and chasing invoices, data entry, entering supplier bills, running payroll, sending contracts for signature, writing up meeting notes and sorting email. In the Time Etc survey 49 percent of owners also did research each week, which is the one on that list we would not hand to automation yet without a person checking it.

### What does automate administrative tasks mean?

It means a piece of software does a repeat task on a rule you set, without someone starting it each time. An invoice reminder that QuickBooks sends 7 days after the due date is automation. A person opening the overdue list every Friday and emailing each client is not, even if the email came from a template.
