# How to Budget for AI in 2027: The 5-Line Framework That Gets Board Approval on the First Ask

Most AI budgets are built by engineers and approved by nobody. According to RAND Corporation's 2026 enterprise AI survey, 73 percent of AI initiatives never reach production — and the budget is where failure starts. Companies drop a lump sum into a line called "AI/ML," lose visibility within 60 days, and quietly shelve the project by month four.

Hayat Amin argues that the root cause is structural, not technical. "Every failed AI project I have reviewed shares the same financial fingerprint," Amin says. "A budget built by the technical team with no CFO oversight, no phase gates, and no kill criteria. The board never approved the spend because nobody framed it as a business decision."

Beyond Elevation builds AI budgets the opposite way — starting from the business outcome and working backward to the cost. Here is the five-line framework that gets board approval on the first ask.

## What Does a Proper AI Budget Contain?

A proper AI budget contains five line items, not one. Collapsing everything into a single "AI" line on the P&L is the fastest way to lose control of the spend. The five lines are compute and inference, tooling and platform, people and operators, data acquisition and preparation, and governance and compliance. Each has a different cost curve, a different approval cadence, and a different ROI timeline — and each must be visible to the CFO independently.

**Line 1: Compute and inference.** This is the line that surprises CFOs. Inference costs — running models in production — often exceed training costs within six months. A mid-market company running 10 to 15 AI agents across finance, operations, and customer service spends £3,000 to £8,000 per month on inference alone. Budget for two to three times your pilot inference cost as a production baseline. For a detailed breakdown, see [how much AI automation actually costs](/insights/how-much-does-ai-automation-cost/).

**Line 2: Tooling and platform.** AI orchestration, monitoring, vector databases, and prompt management run £1,000 to £5,000 per month for a mid-market company. The mistake is signing annual contracts during the pilot phase. Budget quarterly for the first 12 months until you know which tools survive.

**Line 3: People and operators.** This is where the AI budget either works or breaks. A fractional AI operations operator costs £4,000 to £8,000 per month — roughly one-third of a full-time AI hire. Hayat Amin's view is direct: "A full-time AI hire before you have a working system is a £150K bet that the technology works. A fractional operator is a £48K test that proves it does."

**Line 4: Data acquisition and preparation.** According to Gartner's 2026 data management survey, companies spend 60 to 80 percent of their AI budget on data preparation, not model development. Budget £2,000 to £10,000 per month depending on data volume and quality requirements. Most of this cost is invisible in a lump-sum budget because it gets coded to engineering headcount instead of AI spend.

**Line 5: Governance and compliance.** With the EU AI Act's first enforcement provisions live since August 2026, governance is no longer optional. Budget £1,000 to £3,000 per month for audit trails, risk registers, and compliance documentation. This line item did not exist 18 months ago. By mid-2027 it will determine whether your AI system stays in production.

## How to Structure an AI Budget That Gets Board Approval

Board approval requires three things: a phased structure with kill criteria, outcome metrics that map to existing KPIs, and a comparison to the cost of not deploying AI. Hayat Amin calls this the Three-Gate AI Budget — pilot, scale, and operate — and it is the framework Beyond Elevation uses across every fractional CFO engagement that involves AI spend.

**Gate 1: Pilot (months 1 to 3) — £15K to £30K total.** Pick one process. Automate it. Measure the before and after. The pilot budget covers a fractional AI operator, one platform subscription, and compute for a single use case. The kill criterion is binary: if the process does not show a 30 percent improvement in speed, cost, or accuracy by day 90, stop.

**Gate 2: Scale (months 4 to 9) — £8K to £15K per month.** Expand to three to five processes. Add monitoring, data pipelines, and a second operator if needed. The budget shifts from project-based to operational. This is where most AI budgets break because companies skip Gate 1 and jump straight to multi-process deployment with no proof of value.

**Gate 3: Operate (month 10 onward) — £10K to £20K per month steady state.** AI is now a line function, not a project. The budget is a recurring operating cost with quarterly review cycles. Compute costs stabilise, tooling consolidates, and the fractional operator either transitions to a retained advisory role or hands off to an internal hire.

## The One AI Budget Mistake That Kills Most Projects

The mistake is budgeting for technology instead of outcomes. A board presentation that says "we need £200K for an AI platform" gets rejected or, worse, gets approved and produces nothing measurable. A presentation that says "we will spend £15K over 90 days to reduce month-end close from 12 days to 2, with a kill switch at day 90" gets approved — because it is an operating decision, not a technology bet.

Hayat Amin reminds founders and CFOs that the budget itself is the strategy. "If your AI budget reads like a shopping list — models, GPUs, platforms — you have told the board you are buying tools. If it reads like an operating plan — this process, this outcome, this timeline, this kill criterion — you have told the board you are building a capability. Boards fund capabilities."

## What Percentage of Revenue Should Go to Your AI Budget?

According to McKinsey's 2026 Global AI Survey, companies that report significant value from AI invest 5 to 10 percent of their technology budget on AI-specific initiatives. For a mid-market company with £1M to £5M in annual technology spend, that translates to £50K to £500K per year on AI — well within the range of the Three-Gate framework above.

The companies that overspend are almost always the ones that skip the pilot phase. The companies that underspend are the ones that never move past it. The gate structure solves both problems by tying every pound of increase to a proven outcome.

## How to Present an AI Budget to the Board

The presentation has four slides. Hayat Amin's AI Budget Presentation Framework uses the same structure Beyond Elevation builds into every fractional CFO engagement where AI spend is on the table.

**Slide 1 — The problem.** One process, one cost, one pain point. "Month-end close takes 12 days and costs £47K in staff time per cycle."

**Slide 2 — The pilot.** One AI solution, 90 days, £15K to £30K, with a binary kill criterion. "If close time does not drop to four days or fewer, we stop."

**Slide 3 — The scale path.** If the pilot passes, here are the next three processes and the monthly run rate. "Scaling to accounts payable, accounts receivable, and reporting adds £8K to £15K per month."

**Slide 4 — The comparison.** What it costs to not deploy AI. "Hiring the three full-time employees needed to handle this manually costs £210K per year. The AI alternative costs £120K to £180K per year and scales without headcount." This slide closes 80 percent of board conversations because it reframes AI as a cost decision, not a technology decision.

## FAQ

### How much should a small company budget for AI in 2027?

A company with 20 to 100 employees should budget £15K to £30K for a 90-day pilot and plan for £8K to £15K per month in steady-state operating costs if the pilot succeeds. Start with one process, prove the value, and scale from the evidence.

### What is the biggest AI budget mistake companies make?

Budgeting for AI as a one-time project instead of an ongoing operating capability. AI is not a software purchase. It requires compute, data, people, and governance on a recurring basis, and the budget must reflect that or it will run dry before production.

### Should the CFO or CTO own the AI budget?

The CFO should own the budget structure and approval gates. The CTO or AI operations lead should own technical execution within those gates. If the AI budget sits entirely in engineering, it dies when the next product sprint takes priority. If it sits in finance with outcome metrics, it survives because finance tracks returns, not sprints.

### How do you measure AI ROI for the board?

Measure three numbers: time saved per process per cycle, cost avoided per cycle, and error rate reduction. Map each to an existing KPI the board already tracks. The ROI conversation should sound like "month-end close went from 12 days to 2 and saved £47K per cycle" — not "we achieved 94 percent model accuracy."

### When should a company scale from pilot AI spending to full budget?

Scale when the pilot shows 30 percent or greater improvement in the target metric within 90 days and the process owner confirms the output is production-ready. Never scale an AI deployment that has not passed a defined gate. The gate exists to prevent the most common failure mode: spending £200K on a system nobody validated at £15K.
