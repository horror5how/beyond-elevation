# How to use AI for small business marketing: the four jobs to hand it first, and what to keep

To use AI for small business marketing, give it the repeated work behind the marketing rather than the ideas. That means answering and routing every new lead within minutes, writing first drafts of emails and posts, drafting replies to reviews, and reporting each week what your spend brought in. Keep the offer, the voice and the final edit with a person.

Hayat Amin has spent twenty years as a technology chief financial officer and sold three companies, and he now builds this kind of AI operations work inside companies himself. This piece covers the order we'd do it in, what the tools charge, and the parts we'd never hand over. Every price was read on the vendor's own page on 9 October 2026, in US dollars.

## how many small businesses already use AI for this

Most of your competitors have started. The U.S. Chamber of Commerce's 2025 small business AI report, published on 18 August 2025, found that 58% of small businesses say they use generative AI, up from 40% in the 2024 edition and 23% in 2023. The same report found 82% of small businesses using AI increased their workforce over the past year.

Using a chatbot to write a caption is the easy part, and it's where most owners stop. The time in a small company's marketing goes on everything around the caption: the enquiry that sat in an inbox over the weekend, the review nobody answered, the spreadsheet someone builds on a Monday to work out which ads paid. That's where we start.

## 1. answer every lead before it goes cold

A lead comes in through a web form, a Facebook message, a Google call or an email. In most small companies it lands in one of four places and waits for whoever checks that place next. The first job we give AI is reading each one as it arrives, pulling out the name, the need, the budget and the location, writing it into the CRM, and sending a reply that answers the question and offers a time to talk.

The reply is drafted by a language model from your own price list and FAQ, and a rule decides whether it goes straight out or waits for a person. We'd let a simple "yes, we cover Brooklyn, here are three slots" go automatically and hold anything that quotes a price outside the list. If you want the mechanics of a workflow like this, we wrote up [how workflow automation works](/insights/how-does-workflow-automation-work/), part by part.

## 2. write the first draft, never the last

Language models are good at first drafts of the things a small company sends every week: the monthly email, three social posts from one blog, product descriptions, a follow-up after a quote. They're bad at knowing your customers, your prices this month and the one thing you'd never say. The rule we set: AI writes the draft from a brief and your past best emails, and a named person edits and sends it.

You may already pay for this inside a tool you own. Mailchimp's pricing page lists generative AI features as a no additional cost add-on on Standard, from $20 a month, and on Premium, from $350 a month, with both starting at 501 to 1,500 contacts. They aren't included on Essentials, from $13 a month, and the Free plan covers up to 250 contacts. Mailchimp also says its Intuit Assist features are in beta and available at no additional cost "at this time", so check before you build a habit on them.

HubSpot's Marketing Hub Starter includes 1,000 marketing contacts and 500 HubSpot Credits a month, which pay for its AI agents, and extra credits cost $0.010 each. Its pricing page names a Breeze Assistant, a Customer Agent and a Content Agent, the last marked beta, for blog posts, social posts and landing pages. We haven't quoted HubSpot's seat price here because the page showed two different Starter figures when we read it.

## 3. reply to every review the same week

Reviews are marketing a customer writes for you, and most small companies answer them in bursts. Google's own help page says a reply, once approved, "will be publicly posted under the customer's review" and "will appear like your business replied", and that replies usually take up to 10 minutes to review, though sometimes up to 30 days.

We'd have AI read each new review, sort it as praise, a complaint or a question, and draft a reply in your voice that names the thing the customer mentioned. Praise can go out after a quick read. A complaint goes to the owner with the draft and the customer's order history next to it, because the reply to a one-star review is the one a hundred future customers read.

## 4. see what each dollar of spend brought in, every Monday

This is the job that changes decisions, and it's the one AI tools sold for marketing rarely touch. Ad spend sits in Google Ads and Meta, leads sit in the CRM, and revenue sits in the accounting system or Stripe. Nobody joins them until month end, so a campaign that stopped working in week one keeps spending for four.

We connect those systems so one dashboard shows, every week, spend by channel, leads by channel, how many became customers, and what each customer cost. AI's part is the messy matching, tying a "Jon S" in the CRM to a "Jonathan Smith" invoice, and writing a three-line note on what moved. The arithmetic stays on fixed rules. A chief executive who can see cost per customer on a Monday can move budget on a Monday.

## 5. what we'd keep human

Four things stay with a person. The offer and the price, because a model will happily invent a discount. Anything sent to a customer who's angry or owes you money. The final edit of anything with your name on it. And the decision about where the money goes, which the dashboard informs but doesn't make.

We also write one page for every workflow: what it reads, what it sends, who owns it and what it must never do. A small company with eight automations and no owner has eight things that will quietly break the week someone renames a form field.

## what it costs, and what it saves

The software is the cheap part. Most companies already pay for a CRM or an email tool with AI built in, so the first month can cost little or nothing extra. The cost is in connecting it properly to the systems you already run, which is where [how much AI automation costs](/insights/how-much-does-ai-automation-cost/) goes through build fees line by line.

For a sense of what the time is worth, the Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the median wage for market research analysts at $78,760 a year, or $37.87 an hour, in May 2025. If AI drafting and lead replies take 5 hours a week off one person's marketing work, which is our assumption and not a measured figure, that's about $189 a week at that rate, or roughly $9,800 over 52 weeks, on our arithmetic. Your own number depends on who does the work today and what they'd do with the hours.

## a 30 day order to do it in

In week 1, list every place a lead can arrive and who checks it, then turn on the AI features in the tools you already pay for. In week 2, build the lead reply, with a person approving every message for the first 50. In week 3, add review replies and the first draft rule for your weekly email. In week 4, connect ad spend, CRM and revenue into one weekly view. By day 30 you'll know which of the four is worth the most to you, and that's where the next month's work goes.

## Frequently asked questions

### How to use AI in marketing for a small business?

Start with the repeated work: replying to new leads, first drafts of emails and posts, review replies and the weekly spend report. Keep prices, offers, complaints and final edits with a person.

### What are the best AI tools for small business marketing?

The AI already inside the tool you pay for is usually the best one. Mailchimp includes generative AI features on Standard, from $20 a month, and HubSpot's Marketing Hub Starter includes 500 credits a month for its AI agents. A general chatbot covers drafting. Connecting them to your CRM and accounts is the part that needs building.

### Is there free AI for small business marketing?

Some. Mailchimp's Free plan covers up to 250 contacts but doesn't include its generative AI features, which start on Standard. Free chatbot tiers will draft copy. The lead routing and reporting described above need paid tools or a build.

### How to use AI as a small business owner?

Pick one job you do every week that follows the same steps, such as answering enquiries, and let AI draft it while you approve. Once that runs for a month, add the next one. Our guide on [how to automate your business](/insights/how-can-i-automate-my-business/) lists where to look.

### How many small businesses use AI?

The U.S. Chamber of Commerce's 2025 report found 58% of small businesses say they use generative AI, up from 40% in 2024 and 23% in 2023.
