# How to Use AI in HR Operations: 8 Jobs Worth Automating First

To use AI in HR operations, start with the work that repeats every week and follows rules: screening applicants, booking interviews, sending offer letters and onboarding paperwork, ordering background checks, answering policy questions, approving time off and hours, reading survey comments, and giving the owner a live view of headcount and turnover.

Hayat Amin has spent twenty years in technology and sold three companies as chief financial officer, so he reads HR the way a buyer does, line by line, starting with the data nobody trusts. He now builds this work inside companies himself, as a forward deployed engineer. Every price and figure below was read on the publisher's own page on 11 October 2026. Where a vendor publishes no price, we say so.

## where HR operations stand in 2026

Most small companies haven't started. SHRM previewed its State of AI in HR 2026 report in March, drawing on more than 1,900 HR professionals, and found that 60% of extra large organizations had adopted AI in HR against about 1 in 3 small ones. 31% hadn't begun using AI at all. Recruiting led at 27%, then HR technology at 21% and learning and development at 17%, and 7 of the top 10 AI use cases in HR were in recruiting. Only 2% of organizations used AI in compliance.

The people doing this work aren't going anywhere. The Bureau of Labor Statistics counted 939,700 human resources specialists in 2025, at a median of $75,940 a year, or $36.51 an hour. It projects 6 percent growth from 2025 to 2035, with about 73,700 openings a year. SHRM's own finding points the same way: only 7% of organizations reported job displacement due to AI, and job responsibilities were 5.5 times more likely to change than be eliminated. The job is moving from keying to judgement.

Three symptoms tell you a company is ready. An offer letter gets typed by hand from details already sitting in the applicant tracking system. Hours come in on paper or by text and somebody keys them into payroll. And the owner can't say what headcount, overtime and turnover cost this month until the books close.

## how we picked the eight

A job made the list if it happens every week in a US company of 10 to 250 people, if a named product does it today, and if the time it takes lands on someone who should be dealing with people rather than forms. We ordered them the way an employee moves through the business, from application to the owner's report. Performance claims belong to the vendor that makes them. Nobody paid to appear here. ## 1. screening applicants

This is where most companies start, and SHRM's numbers show why: recruiting is the highest volume HR job with the clearest rules. Workable now sells an agent that sources, screens and qualifies candidates, priced by what it does. It costs 1 AI credit to screen a candidate against your profile, 2 to source a passive candidate and 10 each time a candidate chats with it. Every paid account starts with 3,000 free credits, and 5,000 more cost $600, or $0.12 each. Deel's Hire product, at $14 per worker per month, includes AI screening, offer generation and interview scheduling.

Wrong for you if you hire two people a year. Read every application yourself and spend the money elsewhere.

## 2. booking interviews

Three candidates, two managers, one free slot, and a week of email to find it. Workable sells texting as an $89 a month add on and video interviews at $109 a month on its Standard plan, both included on Premier. Deel's Hire product schedules interviews and writes AI interview summaries. The gain is the coordinator's afternoon, and a candidate who hears back the same day instead of on Thursday.

## 3. offer letters and onboarding paperwork

The offer letter, the handbook signature, the tax forms and the I-9 all use details the company already holds. BambooHR's Core plan includes email and offer letter templates that fill in candidate details, new hire packets, an onboarding checklist and e-signatures. The federal side is free. E-Verify checks Form I-9 information against records held by DHS and the Social Security Administration at no charge. The build is making sure the new hire's details are typed once, at the offer, and flow everywhere else.

## 4. background checks ordered from the hiring system

A hire stalls when someone has to log into a second system to order the check and then chase the result. Wired to the applicant tracking system, the check orders itself when the candidate accepts, and the result writes back. Checkr publishes its prices: Basic from $29.99 a report and Essential from $59.99, with pass-through fees such as county court charges on top. A motor vehicle record, identity verification and drug screening are add ons.

## 5. answering the same policy questions every week

How many vacation days do I have left? Is Juneteenth a paid holiday here? Where's the dental plan summary? An HR lead in a 60 person company answers these all week. BambooHR's AI assistant answers everyday questions from your own data, PTO and holidays, on Core. On Pro it reads your policies, handbook and benefits documents too, and on Elite it analyses your HR data and benchmarks. Lattice includes an AI agent in its Foundations package at $13 a seat a month that answers your team's questions and coaches managers.

Wrong for you if the handbook is out of date. The assistant will quote it with confidence, so fix the document before you switch it on.

## 6. time off, hours and payroll inputs

Payroll rarely fails on the calculation. It fails because the inputs arrive late and in different formats: a timesheet by text, overtime agreed in the corridor, a leaver confirmed after the cut off. BambooHR's Time and Attendance add on runs automated approval workflows and calculates overtime, and it takes 15% off when you bundle its payroll and benefits administration with any plan, for US employees. If your hours live in a separate scheduling app, the join between that app and payroll is the first thing worth building, because every hour keyed twice is an hour that can be keyed wrong.

## 7. reading engagement survey comments

Most owners run a survey, read the score and never get to the 200 written comments. That's where the reason people leave usually sits. BambooHR uses AI to sort eNPS free text responses into themes on every plan. Lattice's Engagement product, at $4 a seat a month, adds pulse, onboarding and exit surveys with AI analysis and trends. Exit survey themes are the most useful of the lot, because they tell you what turnover is costing before the next resignation.

## 8. a live view of headcount, overtime and turnover

Most owners see their people costs a month late. The questions that matter can't wait for month end. Which team ran overtime three weeks in a row? Which roles have been open more than 30 days? Who resigned this quarter, from which manager? The answers sit across the HR system, payroll, scheduling and the accounting file. BambooHR's Elite plan builds custom dashboards from live HR data, and Rippling says it builds dashboards and reports across every system you run, priced on a custom quote. If your systems don't share a vendor, a tool such as n8n joins them, from 20 euros a month billed annually for 2,500 workflow runs. The build is one screen, refreshed daily, that the owner reads on Monday morning.

This is our own arithmetic on why this pays. If items 3, 5 and 6 give an HR specialist back 5 hours a week, at the BLS median of $36.51 an hour that's $182.55 a week, or about $9,490 a year. The 5 hours is our assumption, not a published figure. Check it against your own week before you rely on it.

## what the systems cost

The plan you're on decides which of the eight you can switch on and which need a build. Prices as published on 11 October 2026, in US dollars unless stated.

- Workable: Standard at $299 a month, Premier at $599 and Enterprise at $719, for 1 to 20 employees, recruiting and HR bundled. Agent credits are extra, from $0.12 each.

- Deel: Hire at $14 per worker per month, contractors at $49 a month, US PEO at $125 per employee per month and employer of record at $599.

- Lattice: Foundations at $13 a seat a month, Performance at $10, Goals at $8, Engagement at $4.

- Checkr: from $29.99 a report on Basic and $59.99 on Essential.

- Rippling: custom quote only.

- Gusto and Justworks: both pricing pages refused our reader on 11 October 2026, so we don't quote either.

Before you sign, ask each vendor one thing: is the API, or the AI feature you want, on the plan you're being quoted, or is it an upgrade? The answer decides whether item 8 is configuration or a build.

## what to watch before you switch it on

Governance hasn't caught up. SHRM found fewer than half of organizations have formal AI governance policies, and 57% of HR professionals in states with AI regulations were unfamiliar with those laws. Before an AI screens a single applicant, know which states and cities you hire in and what they require.

## Frequently asked questions

### How is AI used in HR?

Mostly in recruiting so far. SHRM's 2026 research found recruiting led AI adoption in HR at 27%, ahead of HR technology at 21% and learning and development at 17%, with resume screening, job posting creation and candidate matching the common uses. Policy questions, time off and onboarding paperwork are the next jobs most small companies hand over.

### How to use AI in HR processes?

Pick one process that repeats weekly, write down every step and every system it touches, and switch on the AI feature your HR system already includes before buying anything new. BambooHR, Workable, Deel and Lattice all include AI features in the plans listed above. Build only the joins the vendors leave open.

### How to use AI in HR admin?

Start with the paperwork that reuses data you already hold: offer letters, new hire packets, e-signatures and the I-9. BambooHR's Core plan fills offer letters from candidate details, and E-Verify checks I-9 information against government records at no charge. Then hand everyday questions about PTO and holidays to an assistant that reads your own data.

### How to use AI agents in HR?

Give an agent one narrow job with a clear rule and a person who checks its work. Workable's agent screens, sources and chats with candidates for 1, 2 and 10 credits per action. Lattice's agent answers employee questions and coaches managers. Keep hiring decisions with a person, and check your state's rules before any agent screens applicants.

### What are the AI use cases in HR operations?

The eight above: screening applicants, booking interviews, offer letters and onboarding paperwork, background checks, policy questions, time off and payroll inputs, engagement survey comments, and a live view of headcount, overtime and turnover.

### How is generative AI used in HR operations?

Writing and reading. It drafts job posts, offer letters and interview summaries, answers questions from your handbook, and sorts written survey comments into themes. Anything it writes that goes to a candidate or an employee should be read by a person first.

### What are examples of AI in HR?

Workable's agent screening candidates against a profile, Deel generating offers and scheduling interviews, BambooHR answering PTO questions and sorting eNPS comments into themes, Lattice analysing exit surveys, and Checkr running a background check ordered automatically when a candidate accepts.
