# Interim CFO Day Rates vs Fractional Retainers: the 2026 Price Gap

An interim CFO in the UK costs between £1,500 and £3,500 per day. A fractional CFO costs between £3,000 and £8,000 per month. That is a five-to-ten-times price gap for two people with the same three letters after their title. Seniority doesn't explain it. The difference is what you're buying: a temporary fix or a permanent function.

According to the Interim Management Association's 2025 Rate Survey, the median interim CFO day rate in the UK sits at £2,200. A three-month engagement at four days per week costs roughly £176,000. Hayat Amin, who has placed and replaced interim and fractional CFOs across three continents, argues that most companies paying interim rates are solving a problem a fractional retainer handles at one-fifth the cost. The distinction matters because choosing wrong wastes money and delays the finance function you need.

## What does an interim CFO cost per day in 2026?

An interim CFO in the UK charges between £1,500 and £3,500 per day in 2026, with the median sitting at £2,200 for companies between £5 million and £50 million in revenue. US interim CFO rates run $2,000 to $5,000 per day. Gulf-region rates land between $2,500 and $4,500 per day.

Interim engagements typically run three to nine months. The CFO works four or five days per week, embedded in the business on a fixed-term contract rather than a permanent one. At four days per week for six months, a UK interim CFO engagement totals roughly £211,000 to £336,000.

Three things push the day rate higher: sector complexity (regulated industries add 15 to 25 per cent), transaction work (M&A, IPO, or fundraising mandates command premium rates), and urgency (a CFO needed within two weeks costs more than one placed in six). Interim agencies typically add a 20 to 30 per cent margin on top of the CFO's personal rate.

## What does a fractional CFO cost per month?

A fractional CFO costs between £3,000 and £8,000 per month in the UK in 2026, typically working one to two days per week on a rolling retainer. Annual cost: £36,000 to £96,000. That is less than a single quarter of most interim engagements.

The fractional model works differently from interim. A fractional CFO isn't embedded in the business day to day. They operate on a retained basis, owning a defined scope, monthly close, board reporting, cash-flow forecasting, investor relations, and delivering outputs rather than billing hours. Most fractional CFOs serve two to four clients at once, which is how the economics work for both sides.

Beyond Elevation's fractional CFO engagements start at £4,000 per month for companies in the £2 million to £10 million revenue range, scaling to £7,500 for pre-exit or fundraising mandates that require heavier weekly involvement. The engagement includes AI-assisted reporting, which compresses two full days of work into half a day of review and decision-making.

## Why is there a 5x price gap for the same job title?

The price gap exists because interim and fractional CFOs solve different problems. An interim CFO is a crisis-grade hire priced for urgency and total commitment, like a hotel room booked by the night. A fractional CFO is a retained operator priced for ongoing value over months or years, more like a lease.

Interim CFOs charge premium day rates because they accept short-term risk: no job security, no equity, no benefits, no guarantee of the next engagement. They also carry transition costs, learning your business from scratch, then handing it back when the contract ends. The day rate compensates for that instability.

Fractional CFOs charge lower monthly rates because the relationship is ongoing. They build institutional knowledge over time and don't need to re-learn your chart of accounts every quarter. Hayat Amin calls this the compounding advantage: a fractional CFO who has been in your business for twelve months knows where the numbers sit, where the leaks are, and what the board will ask before they ask it. An interim CFO walking in cold needs six to eight weeks just to reach baseline competence.

## When should you pay interim CFO day rates?

Pay interim rates when you need someone in the seat every day for a defined, time-bound event that cannot wait. The three scenarios where interim CFO pricing makes economic sense are genuine emergencies, transactions, and regulatory mandates.

**CFO departure with no succession plan.** Your CFO resigns with two weeks' notice. You are mid-audit or mid-fundraise. An interim CFO can start within days and provide continuity while you recruit a permanent replacement. The cost is high, but the alternative, a gap in finance leadership during a critical window, is higher.

**Transaction execution.** An IPO, trade sale, or major acquisition needs a CFO-level operator on the deal five days a week for three to six months. The day rate reflects the transaction value at stake. A well-run sell-side process can add 15 to 30 per cent to the final price.

**Regulatory remediation.** A regulator has flagged material weaknesses in your financial reporting. You need someone senior, working every day, in place immediately, to fix the controls and satisfy the regulator. Speed and intensity justify the premium.

Outside these three scenarios, Hayat Amin argues most companies paying interim rates are overspending. The numbers support it: a six-month interim engagement at median UK rates costs more than two full years of fractional CFO retainer.

## When is a fractional CFO retainer the smarter spend?

A fractional CFO retainer is the better investment when you need ongoing finance leadership but cannot justify, or do not need, someone in the role five days a week. That describes the majority of companies between £1 million and £30 million in revenue.

Hayat Amin's Finance Leadership Pricing Test is a three-question filter Beyond Elevation uses with every new enquiry:

**Question one: Is there a deadline forcing that kind of intensity?** If not, if what you need is better numbers and forecasting rather than a deal closing in 90 days, a fractional retainer covers it at a fraction of the interim cost.

**Question two: Will you need this person in twelve months?** If yes, you are building a function, and functions are built by retained operators who compound knowledge over time. An interim hands over a binder and leaves.

**Question three: Can the scope fit into one to two days a week?** If yes, a fractional CFO can deliver what you need without paying for someone in the seat five days a week. Modern finance stacks, AI-assisted reconciliation and live dashboards, mean a skilled fractional CFO can run a finance function in two days that used to take five.

If you answer no, yes, yes, or even two out of three, the fractional model wins on cost and continuity.

## What is the hidden cost most companies miss?

The hidden cost of interim CFO engagements is the transition tax. Every time an interim CFO leaves and a replacement arrives, whether another interim, a fractional, or a permanent hire, the business pays an invisible cost in lost context, repeated onboarding, and decisions that stall during the handover.

Hayat Amin quantifies this at Beyond Elevation: the average interim-to-permanent CFO transition creates a six-to-eight-week productivity gap where financial reporting quality drops, board decks arrive late, and strategic decisions get deferred. In a company doing £10 million in revenue, that gap costs roughly £40,000 to £80,000 in delayed decisions, missed early-payment discounts, and management time spent re-briefing the new hire.

A fractional CFO who stays two or three years never incurs that cost. Institutional knowledge builds up, reporting gets tighter month on month, and the board relationship deepens. The total spend over three years, roughly £108,000 to £288,000, is still less than two six-month interim engagements plus the transition costs between them.

## The bottom line: match the model to the problem

Interim CFOs are crisis tools priced for urgency. Fractional CFOs are retained operators priced for value. The 2026 price gap between them, five to ten times on an annualised basis, comes from two different service models solving two different problems, not from a market inefficiency.

The mistake is using the expensive model when the cheaper one delivers a better outcome. Most companies between £2 million and £30 million in revenue need a fractional CFO, not an interim one. The maths is not close.

## FAQ

### Can an interim CFO become a fractional CFO?

Yes, but it rarely works well. Interim CFOs usually work five days a week between permanent roles. Fractional CFOs are built for retained work spread across multiple clients, a day or two a week each. The skillset overlaps, but the operating model and pricing expectations differ. Asking an interim to shift to fractional often means paying a premium rate for a lighter schedule.

### Is a fractional CFO cheaper than hiring a permanent CFO?

Yes, by a wide margin. A permanent CFO in the UK costs £150,000 to £250,000 in salary, plus benefits, equity, and overhead. A fractional CFO costs £36,000 to £96,000 a year. For companies under £30 million in revenue, the fractional model delivers 80 per cent of the output at 30 per cent of the cost.

### How quickly can an interim CFO start?

Most interim CFO agencies can place a candidate within one to two weeks. For urgent placements, a sudden departure or regulatory crisis, some interims can start within 48 hours. Speed is the primary advantage of the interim model, and it is priced accordingly.

### Do fractional CFOs work on-site?

Most fractional CFOs work a hybrid model, one day per week on-site for team alignment and board interaction, with the remainder remote. The exact split depends on the company's needs and the CFO's other client commitments. Beyond Elevation's fractional CFOs typically spend one day per week on-site during the first 90 days, then shift to fortnightly visits once systems and rhythms are in place.
