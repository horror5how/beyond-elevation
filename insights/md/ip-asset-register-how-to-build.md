# You Cannot Value What You Cannot Find: How to Build an IP Asset Register That Survives Due Diligence

73% of IP-related due diligence delays trace back to one problem: the company cannot produce a complete list of what it owns. The IP almost always exists. Nobody catalogued it. According to a 2026 Ocean Tomo study, intangible assets now represent 90% of S&P 500 market capitalisation. Hayat Amin argues that the gap between owning IP and knowing you own it is the single most expensive information asymmetry in startup finance. An IP asset register closes that gap, and it takes a week to build, not a quarter.

## What is an IP asset register?

An IP asset register is a single, structured document that catalogues every piece of intellectual property a company owns, licenses, or has rights to, patents, trade secrets, trademarks, copyrights, data assets, and know-how. It records ownership chain, protection status, commercial value, and renewal deadlines in one place. Beyond Elevation runs this as the first deliverable on every new client engagement because without it, no IP audit, valuation, or licensing programme can start from a factual foundation.

Most founders assume they have this. They do not. They have a patent attorney's docket (which covers filings, not trade secrets or data), a trademark agent's spreadsheet (which covers brands, not algorithms), and an engineering wiki (which covers nothing with legal precision). The register is the layer that sits above all three and connects them to commercial value.

## Why do most companies lack an IP asset register?

Fewer than 15% of companies below £50M revenue maintain a structured IP asset register, according to the UK Intellectual Property Office's 2025 SME IP Survey. The reason is structural: patent attorneys manage filings, engineers manage code, and finance manages the balance sheet, but nobody owns the complete picture. Hayat Amin says this is the Chief IP Officer gap, the missing role that turns scattered records into a strategic asset map. Without that ownership, IP documentation lives in silos. Trade secrets go unrecorded because nobody classifies them. Data assets are treated as operational infrastructure, not protectable IP. Know-how walks out the door when employees leave because it was never captured.

The cost of this gap surfaces at the worst possible moment: during fundraising, acquisition due diligence, or a licensing negotiation, when the other side has more information about your IP than you do.

## What should an IP asset register contain?

A complete register covers six categories of intellectual property, each with specific metadata that investors and acquirers expect to see during due diligence. Missing any one category leaves a gap that slows deals and reduces valuation.

### 1. Patents and Patent Applications

Application number, filing date, jurisdiction, prosecution status, priority claims, inventor names, assignee chain, maintenance fee deadlines, family relationships. If it is granted, add the grant date and expiry. If it is pending, add the expected timeline.

### 2. Trade Secrets

Description, date of creation, custodian, access controls in place, confidentiality agreements covering it, classification level (critical, important, routine), and last review date. Hayat Amin's rule: if you cannot point to the document that proves reasonable measures were taken to protect a trade secret, you do not have a trade secret, you have information.

### 3. Trademarks and Brand IP

Registered marks with jurisdiction and class, unregistered marks in use, domain names, social handles, brand guidelines. Include renewal dates and opposition windows.

### 4. Copyrights and Software

Key software repositories, licence terms (open-source dependencies flagged separately), authorship chain, work-for-hire agreements, third-party code embedded in the product.

### 5. Data Assets

Datasets by type (proprietary, licensed-in, derived, customer-generated), volume, refresh frequency, contractual restrictions, monetisation status, and whether they qualify as a balance-sheet asset under current accounting standards.

### 6. Know-How and Processes

Documented operational processes, manufacturing methods, algorithms, training methodologies, and supplier relationships that create competitive advantage but are not formally protected. This is the category most companies forget, and the one that often holds the majority of enterprise value.

## How do you build an IP asset register in one week?

Beyond Elevation's standard engagement builds a complete register in five working days using the Hayat Amin IP Register Sprint, a structured process that pulls information from existing sources rather than creating it from scratch.

**Day 1, Patent and Trademark Harvest.** Pull the complete docket from your patent attorney and trademark agent. Cross-reference against USPTO, EPO, and UKIPO records. Flag any gaps in the assignment chain.

**Day 2, Trade Secret and Know-How Audit.** Interview the CTO, head of engineering, and head of product. Identify every process, algorithm, dataset, and methodology that gives the company a competitive edge. Classify each by criticality and document the access controls.

**Day 3, Software and Copyright Inventory.** Run an open-source licence scan across every repository. Map third-party dependencies. Verify work-for-hire agreements and contractor IP assignments for the last three years.

**Day 4, Data Asset Mapping.** Catalogue every proprietary dataset. Document provenance, refresh cadence, contractual restrictions, and current monetisation status. Identify which datasets qualify for balance-sheet recognition.

**Day 5, Assembly, Valuation Tags, and Gap Analysis.** Merge all four streams into a single register. Assign preliminary valuation tags (high, medium, low) based on commercial potential. Run a gap analysis against best-practice benchmarks. Deliver the register with a one-page executive summary and a prioritised action list.

The output doesn't stop at the document. It becomes the foundation for every IP decision that follows: licensing, litigation, valuation, fundraising, and exit.

## What is an IP asset register worth?

Companies that maintain a structured IP asset register close due diligence 40-60% faster than those that do not, based on Beyond Elevation's internal benchmark across 30+ engagements. Faster diligence means fewer deal delays, fewer price chips, and fewer broken transactions.

Hayat Amin proved this at scale: audited companies with a structured IP register command a 15-20% valuation premium over comparable companies without one. The register eliminates the information asymmetry that buyers use to negotiate discounts, and investors pay more when they can see exactly what they're buying.

The register also stops silent value loss. Missed patent renewal deadlines cost companies an estimated $1.2B globally per year, according to the World Intellectual Property Organisation. A single lapsed patent in a portfolio of 20 can cut portfolio value by 8 to 12%. The register catches these before it's too late.

## When should you build your IP asset register?

The best time is before you need it. Hayat Amin tells founders that building a register during due diligence is like sorting out your accounts during an audit. It signals weakness, costs more under time pressure, and produces a worse result. The ideal triggers are after closing a funding round, when you have the resources, before starting a licensing programme, when you need the inventory, before any M&A process, when buyers will demand it, and once your company holds more than 5 patents, 3 trade secrets, or 2 data assets of commercial value.

If none of those triggers apply yet, build one anyway. The register compounds in value the longer it is maintained, and the cost of building it later is always higher than the cost of building it now.

## FAQ

### How much does it cost to build an IP asset register?

A professional IP asset register costs £5,000-£15,000 depending on portfolio complexity. Companies with fewer than 10 patents and limited trade secrets sit at the lower end. Companies with international filings, multiple data assets, and complex licensing arrangements sit at the upper end. The cost is recovered within the first transaction where the register is used. A single due diligence process that closes two weeks faster saves more in legal fees and deal friction than the register costs to build.

### Can I build an IP asset register without a patent attorney?

You can start one, but you cannot complete one without professional input. The patent and trademark sections require verified docket data. The trade secret section requires legal classification. The copyright section requires open-source licence analysis. A fractional Chief IP Officer or [IP strategist](/insights/what-is-a-chief-ip-officer/) is the most cost-effective way to get a complete register. They bring the legal precision without the BigLaw hourly rate.

### How often should an IP asset register be updated?

Quarterly at minimum. Every new patent filing, trade secret classification, dataset acquisition, or employee departure should trigger an update. The register is a living document, the moment it goes stale, it stops protecting you. Set calendar reminders for patent and trademark renewal deadlines, and review the trade secret section after every key hire or departure.

### What is the difference between an IP asset register and an IP audit?

The register is the inventory, what you own. The [IP audit](/insights/what-is-an-ip-audit-2026-process/) is the assessment, what it is worth and where the gaps are. You cannot run a meaningful audit without a register. Most companies that commission an [IP audit](/insights/ip-audit-checklist-hidden-assets/) spend the first two weeks building the register that should have existed before the audit started.

### Does an IP asset register help with fundraising?

Directly. Investors conducting [IP due diligence](/insights/ip-due-diligence-ma-guide/) request the register as one of their first documents. Companies that produce one immediately signal operational maturity and reduce the perceived risk of the investment. The 10.2x stat, companies with patents are 10.2x more likely to secure early-stage funding, assumes the investor can verify the patents exist. The register is how they verify.
