# Non-Executive Director or Fractional Executive? One Advises. The Other Ships.

A non-executive director advises. A fractional executive runs the function. That single distinction costs companies years of stalled execution when they pick the wrong one. According to the UK Institute of Directors (2025), 58 percent of SMEs with revenue between two and twenty million pounds have at least one NED on the board — yet fewer than 15 percent have a fractional executive in an operating role. Hayat Amin argues that this ratio is backwards: most companies at that stage need someone who ships, not someone who sits in a monthly board meeting and asks better questions.

The non-executive director vs fractional executive decision is the most consequential leadership hire most founders get wrong. Both cost a fraction of a full-time executive. Both bring senior experience. But they solve fundamentally different problems — and confusing the two leaves a critical function without an owner.

## What Is a Non-Executive Director?

A non-executive director is a part-time board member who provides independent governance, strategic oversight, and accountability without managing day-to-day operations. NEDs attend board meetings, challenge the executive team's assumptions, and bring an outside perspective shaped by their own operating or investment experience. They do not hire staff, manage budgets, or own deliverables.

The NED role exists to protect shareholders and improve board-level decision-making. In regulated industries — financial services, healthcare, energy — NEDs are often mandatory. For growth-stage companies, a good NED brings a network, credibility with investors, and the hard questions nobody internally wants to ask.

What a NED does not do: run your finance function, restructure your IP portfolio, or deploy AI agents across your back office. A non-executive director advises on whether those things should happen. They do not make them happen.

## What Does a Fractional Executive Do That a Non-Executive Director Cannot?

A fractional executive embeds in your company two to three days per week and runs a specific function — finance, intellectual property, AI operations, or general management — as if they were a full-time hire, but at a fraction of the cost. They own outcomes, manage teams, build processes, and are accountable for the numbers their function produces. Hayat Amin says a fractional executive is an operator, not an adviser — they show up, do the work, and leave the function stronger than they found it.

Where a NED sits above the executive team, a fractional executive sits inside it. A fractional CFO closes the books, builds the forecast, and presents to the board. A fractional Chief IP Officer runs the patent filing roadmap, negotiates licensing deals, and values the portfolio for fundraising. An AI Operations operator deploys agents, measures ROI, and eliminates manual processes across the back office.

The difference is not seniority. It is accountability for execution.

## How Do You Decide Between a Non-Executive Director and a Fractional Executive?

The decision comes down to one question: does your company need governance or execution? If you need someone to hold the CEO accountable, bring investor credibility, and challenge strategy at the board level — hire a NED. If you have a function with no owner, no process, and no one doing the work — hire a fractional executive. Hayat Amin developed the Governance-vs-Execution Test at Beyond Elevation to help founders make this call in under five minutes.

**Hire a non-executive director when:**

- Your board lacks independent oversight or specific sector expertise

- Investors or regulators require an independent director

- You need someone to challenge the executive team's blind spots

- The function already has a competent owner and just needs strategic direction at board level

**Hire a fractional executive when:**

- A critical function — finance, IP, AI operations — has no senior owner

- The founder is personally doing CFO or CTO work alongside running the company

- You need someone to build a process, not just advise on whether to build one

- You cannot justify a full-time salary of £150,000 or more for a function that needs two to three days per week

## What Does Each Role Cost in 2026?

A non-executive director in the UK typically costs between fifteen and forty thousand pounds per year, depending on company size, sector, and the NED's profile. Most NEDs attend ten to twelve board meetings per year plus preparation time, committee work, and ad hoc calls. FTSE 250 NEDs earn significantly more, but for SMEs the range is consistent.

A fractional executive typically costs between three and eight thousand pounds per month — roughly thirty-six to ninety-six thousand pounds per year — for two to three days per week of embedded operational work. The cost is higher than a NED in absolute terms, but the output is fundamentally different. Hayat Amin reminds founders that a fractional CFO who closes the books in two days instead of twelve is not a cost — it is a margin improvement that pays for itself by month two.

The comparison that matters is not NED vs fractional. It is fractional vs full-time. A full-time CFO costs £120,000 to £200,000 in salary alone, plus benefits, equity, and recruitment fees. A fractional CFO delivers the same critical outputs at a fraction of that cost — and steps aside when the function no longer needs that level of seniority.

## When Does a Company Need Both?

Some companies need both a non-executive director and a fractional executive — and knowing when is part of scaling well. A Series A company with investor board seats but no CFO needs a fractional CFO to build the finance function and a NED to provide independent governance. A company preparing for exit needs a fractional Chief IP Officer to value and structure the patent portfolio and a NED with M&A experience to guide the board through the sale process.

The mistake is hiring one when you need the other. A NED cannot close the month-end books. A fractional CFO cannot provide the independent board-level challenge that investors expect. ## The Bottom Line

The non-executive director vs fractional executive decision is not about seniority, cost, or prestige. It is about whether your company needs someone to ask better questions or someone to do the work. Most companies between two and twenty million pounds in revenue need execution first — a function built, a process owned, a senior operator accountable for the numbers. That is a fractional executive. The NED comes later, or alongside, once the function has an owner and the board needs independent oversight.

## FAQ

### Can a non-executive director also be a fractional executive?

Technically yes, but it defeats the purpose. A NED's value comes from independence — they challenge the executive team from outside. If they also run a function, they lose that independence. Most governance codes and investor expectations keep the roles separate. If you need both governance and execution, hire two people.

### Is a fractional executive cheaper than a non-executive director?

No. A fractional executive typically costs more in absolute terms — thirty-six to ninety-six thousand pounds per year versus fifteen to forty thousand for a NED. But the outputs are different, so the comparison is misleading. A NED provides governance. A fractional executive provides execution. Compare fractional cost against the full-time salary it replaces, not against a NED fee.

### When should a startup hire its first non-executive director?

Most startups benefit from a NED after raising institutional funding, when the board formalises and investors expect independent oversight. Before that point, a fractional executive — particularly a [fractional CFO](/insights/what-is-a-fractional-cfo/) — usually delivers more immediate value by building the finance function that makes the company investable.
