# 93% of Patent Portfolios Generate Zero Licensing Revenue. Here Are the 4 Metrics That Change That.

According to the World Intellectual Property Organization, fewer than 7% of granted patents ever generate licensing revenue. The rest sit in maintenance-fee queues, costing their owners thousands per year while producing nothing. Hayat Amin argues this is not a patent quality problem — it is a measurement problem. Most companies have never calculated the ROI of their patent portfolio because nobody taught them what to measure.

Patent portfolio ROI is the ratio of economic value generated by your patents — through licensing revenue, competitive exclusion, valuation premium, and litigation avoidance — to the total cost of obtaining and maintaining them. When you measure it correctly, the number tells you exactly which patents to keep, which to license, and which to abandon. When you do not measure it at all, you are writing cheques to the patent office on faith.

## What Is Patent Portfolio ROI and Why Do Most Companies Get It Wrong?

Patent portfolio ROI is the total economic return your patents deliver divided by their total cost of ownership, measured across four value dimensions rather than just licensing revenue alone. Most companies get it wrong because they only count one dimension — or none at all.

The standard mistake is treating patents as a binary: either they block a competitor or they do not. That framing ignores three of the four ways patents generate economic value. A patent that has never been asserted or licensed can still be driving ROI through valuation premium in fundraising rounds, through defensive positioning that prevents litigation, and through the competitive exclusion effect that keeps rivals from entering your market segment.

Hayat Amin's rule on this is direct: if you cannot put a number on what your patent portfolio returned last year, you are not managing it — you are hoarding it. The difference between the two is the difference between a performing asset and a line item your CFO wants to cut.

## Why Do 93% of Patent Portfolios Produce Zero Revenue?

The overwhelming majority of patent portfolios produce zero direct revenue because they were built for defence without any licensing or monetisation architecture, and because the companies that own them have no system for identifying which patents have commercial value beyond their own products.

Hayat Amin says the root cause is a structural one. Patent attorneys are paid to file and prosecute. Nobody in the typical organisation is paid to extract revenue from the portfolio after the patent grants. The filing roadmap ends at the grant date. There is no licensing roadmap, no royalty-rate analysis, no target-licensee mapping. The patent exists. It costs money every year. And it generates nothing because nobody is working it.

This is where the disconnect becomes expensive. According to Ocean Tomo's 2025 Intangible Asset Market Value Study, intangible assets now represent 90% of S&P 500 market capitalisation. Yet fewer than 5% of those intangible assets — including patents — are ever pledged, licensed, or monetised as standalone revenue streams. The gap between what patents are worth on paper and what they return in practice is the single largest unrealised asset class in corporate finance.

## What Are the 4 Metrics in a Patent Portfolio ROI Audit?

A patent portfolio ROI audit measures four dimensions of economic value — licensing yield, defensive savings, valuation lift, and competitive exclusion — and compares their sum to total portfolio cost. Hayat Amin developed this framework, the Patent Portfolio ROI Scorecard, after restructuring portfolios where companies were spending six figures annually on maintenance with no visibility into what the spend was returning.

**Metric 1: Licensing yield.** This is the most direct ROI measure. Total licensing revenue generated by the portfolio in the trailing twelve months, divided by total portfolio cost (filing fees, prosecution fees, maintenance fees, attorney fees). A healthy licensing yield ratio is 3:1 or higher. Most portfolios score 0:1 because no licensing programme exists. Beyond Elevation's benchmark data from IP audit engagements shows that the median mid-market technology company with 15 or more granted patents has at least three patents with licensable coverage that have never been offered to the market.

**Metric 2: Defensive savings.** This measures litigation cost avoided because the portfolio deters infringement claims, enables cross-licensing at reduced rates, or provides freedom-to-operate coverage that prevents design-around costs. To calculate it, estimate the cost of the two most likely IP disputes you would face without patent protection and discount by the probability of occurrence. Companies with strong defensive portfolios typically avoid one to three potential disputes per decade, each worth $2M to $10M in legal fees and settlement costs.

**Metric 3: Valuation lift.** Companies with patent portfolios receive measurably higher valuations in fundraising and M&A. The 10.2x stat is well documented: companies with patents are 10.2 times more likely to secure early-stage funding. Beyond that, Hayat Amin's work on IP-backed M&A positioning consistently shows a 2x to 4x exit multiple premium for companies with structured, well-documented patent portfolios versus comparable companies without IP protection. To measure this, compare your last fundraising or valuation event against sector benchmarks for unpatented companies at the same revenue stage.

**Metric 4: Competitive exclusion value.** This captures the revenue you retain because competitors cannot legally replicate your patented technology. Estimate the market share erosion you would experience if your core patents did not exist and a well-funded competitor could freely copy your approach. For companies in markets with three to five direct competitors, this exclusion value typically ranges from 10% to 25% of annual revenue — a number that dwarfs the annual cost of maintaining the portfolio.

## How Do You Run a Patent Portfolio ROI Audit in Practice?

A patent portfolio ROI audit starts with a complete inventory of costs, a claim-mapping exercise against your products and your competitors' products, and a structured scoring of each patent against all four ROI dimensions. The entire process takes two to four weeks for a portfolio of 10 to 50 patents.

**Step 1: Calculate total portfolio cost.** Sum every dollar spent on each patent: filing fees, prosecution fees, office action responses, maintenance and renewal fees, foreign filing costs, and outside counsel time. Most companies undercount by 30% to 40% because prosecution costs are buried across multiple law firm invoices spanning years.

**Step 2: Map claims to commercial products.** For each patent, map the independent claims to your own products (defensive value) and to competitors' products (licensing potential). This is the step most companies skip — and it is the step that reveals licensing revenue sitting unclaimed. A single patent with broad claims practised by three competitors in a $500M market segment is worth more than twenty narrow patents covering features nobody else uses.

**Step 3: Score each patent on the four metrics.** Assign a dollar value or range to each patent across all four ROI dimensions. Many patents will score zero on licensing yield today but high on defensive savings or competitive exclusion. The total across all four dimensions is the patent's economic value. Compare it to that patent's annual maintenance cost.

**Step 4: Rank and act.** Sort your portfolio by ROI score. The top quartile are your performing assets — maintain, license actively, and feature in investor materials. The middle 50% are candidates for licensing outreach or cross-licensing leverage. The bottom quartile should be evaluated for abandonment. Every patent abandoned saves $5,000 to $15,000 per year in maintenance fees across major jurisdictions — money better allocated to filing new claims with higher commercial potential.

## When Should You Abandon a Patent Instead of Maintaining It?

A patent should be abandoned when its total ROI score across all four dimensions is less than its annual maintenance cost and there is no credible path to improving that score within 24 months. This is a difficult decision for founders who spent $20,000 to $40,000 obtaining the patent, but the sunk cost is irrelevant to the forward-looking ROI calculation.

Hayat Amin reminds founders that abandoning a low-value patent is not admitting failure — it is capital allocation. The maintenance fees saved on five abandoned patents can fund two new provisional filings on innovations with 10x the commercial potential. The best patent portfolios are pruned portfolios. They contain fewer patents, each with higher individual ROI, and the total portfolio generates more value at lower cost than the bloated version it replaced.

## What Does a High-ROI Patent Portfolio Actually Look Like?

A high-ROI patent portfolio is concentrated, commercially mapped, and actively worked. It typically contains 8 to 20 patents covering core differentiators, with each patent mapped to at least one product in the market — either the company's own product for defensive value or a competitor's product for licensing potential. The portfolio generates measurable returns across multiple dimensions and is reviewed quarterly.

The companies that achieve this are the ones that treat their patent portfolio like a financial instrument rather than a legal filing cabinet. They assign a portfolio owner — increasingly a fractional Chief IP Officer — who is accountable for ROI, not just for prosecution outcomes. They run annual audits using all four metrics. And they make explicit keep-license-abandon decisions on every patent, every year.

## FAQ

### What is a good ROI for a patent portfolio?

A well-managed patent portfolio should deliver a minimum 3:1 return when all four value dimensions are measured — licensing revenue, defensive savings, valuation lift, and competitive exclusion. Top-performing portfolios in technology sectors achieve 10:1 or higher, driven primarily by licensing yield and [valuation premium](/insights/ip-valuation-methods-explained/) at exit.

### How much does it cost to maintain a patent portfolio?

Annual maintenance costs range from $2,000 to $15,000 per patent depending on jurisdiction, patent age, and entity size. A 20-patent portfolio with US, EP, and one Asian jurisdiction coverage typically costs $80,000 to $150,000 per year in maintenance fees alone, before counting outside counsel time for any active prosecution or enforcement.

### Should startups measure patent portfolio ROI?

Yes — but the measurement weights shift. Early-stage companies should weight valuation lift and competitive exclusion heavily, since licensing revenue is unlikely before product-market fit. The 10.2x funding stat means the ROI of even a single well-placed patent can be transformative for a startup's fundraising trajectory. Beyond Elevation recommends a first ROI audit at the 5-patent mark or before any Series A process.

### How often should you audit your patent portfolio?

Annually at minimum, and before any major capital event — fundraising round, acquisition process, or [licensing campaign launch](/insights/patent-licensing-revenue-model/). Quarterly reviews of the top-performing and bottom-performing quartiles keep the portfolio actively managed without requiring a full audit each time.

### What is the biggest mistake companies make with patent portfolio management?

Filing without a licensing or monetisation plan. Most companies spend 90% of their IP budget on obtaining patents and less than 10% on extracting value from them. Flipping that ratio — even partially — is the single highest-leverage change a company can make to its patent portfolio ROI.
