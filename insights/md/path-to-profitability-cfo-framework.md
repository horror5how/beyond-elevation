# Your Investor Deck Says 'Path to Profitability.' Your CFO Should Be Able to Name the Month.

73% of Series A decks include the phrase "path to profitability." Fewer than 11% contain an actual date. That gap is where term sheets die. Hayat Amin, who has reviewed over 200 startup finance functions as a fractional CFO, argues that a profitability timeline without a month attached is not a plan — it is a wish dressed in a spreadsheet.

The shift happened in 2023 and it has not reversed. According to Carta's 2025 State of Private Markets report, 68% of lead investors now require a defined profitability milestone before committing to a Series B term sheet — up from 29% in 2021. "Path to profitability" is no longer a slide title. It is a deliverable. And the person who builds it is not your CEO or your head of sales. It is your CFO.

## What Is a Path to Profitability and Why Do Most Startups Get It Wrong?

A path to profitability is a month-by-month financial model that shows exactly when a company's contribution margin covers its fixed cost base — the crossover month where the business stops requiring external capital to survive. Most founders confuse it with a revenue forecast, which is why most paths to profitability collapse under investor scrutiny.

The distinction matters because revenue growth does not guarantee profitability. A company growing 100% year-over-year can burn faster in year two than year one if customer acquisition costs scale nonlinearly — and in SaaS, they almost always do past the early-adopter cohort.

Hayat Amin's Profitability Crossover Model breaks the path to profitability into four components that most founders miss:

**1. Fully loaded unit economics.** Not gross margin. Not contribution margin on a slide. The actual cost to acquire, serve, and retain one customer, including the support headcount, infrastructure, and payment processing that most models exclude.

**2. Cohort-level contribution margin.** Your first 100 customers are not your next 1,000. Early cohorts convert cheaper through warm networks and founder-led sales. Later cohorts cost more and churn faster. Model them separately or your crossover month is a fantasy.

**3. Step-function fixed costs.** Rent does not scale linearly. Neither does compliance, legal, or the office manager you hire at 30 people. Model these as steps, not slopes.

**4. The crossover month.** The specific month when cumulative contribution margin from all active cohorts exceeds the total fixed cost base. This is the number your investor wants. It is also the number 89% of founders cannot name when asked.

## What Do Investors Actually Check in a Path to Profitability Model?

Investors check five things in a profitability model, and they check them in under fifteen minutes. A model that fails any one of these five checks gets filed under "not ready" regardless of the revenue line. [Beyond Elevation's](https://beyondelevation.com) fractional CFOs see the same five failure modes every quarter.

**Assumption transparency.** Every line in the model must trace back to an assumption that is named, sourced, and defensible. "We assume 10% monthly growth" without a trailing-six-month trendline is not an assumption — it is a guess.

**CAC payback by cohort.** Investors want to see that newer cohorts pay back acquisition costs within 12-18 months. If your model shows a blended average, they will ask for the cohort view. If you do not have it, the meeting is effectively over.

**Gross margin trajectory.** A healthy SaaS business runs 70-80% gross margin at scale. A marketplace runs 40-60%. If your model shows margin expanding without explaining which cost line shrinks, you will be asked to explain.

**Headcount plan tied to revenue triggers.** "We will hire a VP Sales at £2M ARR" is a plan. "We will hire when we need to" is not. Every hire in the model needs a revenue or milestone trigger.

**Scenario modelling.** Investors want to see the base case, the downside, and the "what breaks" case. Hayat Amin reminds founders that the downside scenario is not 80% of the base case — it is a fundamentally different growth curve with different unit economics, because when growth slows, CAC rises and churn accelerates simultaneously.

## Why Do Founders Build the Wrong Path to Profitability Without a CFO?

Founders build the wrong profitability model for three structural reasons, none of which reflect intelligence or effort. The problem is perspective — a founder optimises for the story that raises money, while a CFO optimises for the model that survives investor scrutiny. Those are different outputs.

**Mistake one: confusing gross margin with contribution margin.** Gross margin excludes customer success, onboarding, and support costs. Contribution margin includes them. A company with 75% gross margin and 35% contribution margin is not as healthy as the pitch deck implies. Beyond Elevation's CFO team sees this gap in roughly 6 out of 10 models they audit.

**Mistake two: modelling revenue growth without modelling cost growth.** Revenue at £5M requires infrastructure, compliance, and headcount that revenue at £1M does not. If the cost side of the model grows linearly while the revenue side grows exponentially, the crossover month is a mathematical artefact, not a business reality.

**Mistake three: using a single growth rate for all cohorts.** Hayat Amin argues this is the mistake that kills the most term sheets. "Your first 200 customers came from your network. Your next 2,000 come from paid acquisition at 3x the CAC. Model them identically and you are lying to yourself before you lie to your investors."

## How Does a Fractional CFO Build a Credible Path to Profitability?

A fractional CFO builds a credible path to profitability in 60 to 90 days by working backwards from the crossover month, not forwards from the revenue line. The process is the same whether the company is pre-revenue or at £3M ARR — only the data inputs change.

**Weeks 1-3: actuals audit.** Pull every cost from the accounting system and reclassify it. Most startups have costs buried in the wrong categories — a customer success salary sitting in G&A, infrastructure costs hidden in R&D. Until the numbers are clean, the model is unreliable.

**Weeks 4-6: unit economics build.** Calculate the fully loaded cost to acquire, onboard, serve, and retain one customer. Break this by cohort. Identify which customer segments are profitable and which are subsidised by the profitable ones.

**Weeks 7-9: scenario modelling.** Build three models — base, upside, downside — with different growth rates, different churn rates, and different cost structures. The crossover month should appear in all three scenarios, even if the date moves. If the downside scenario shows no crossover within the runway, the business has a structural problem, not a modelling problem.

**Weeks 10-12: board and investor package.** Translate the model into a narrative that answers the five investor checks above. ## When Should You Build Your Path to Profitability?

Build your path to profitability six months before you need it — which means starting it the day after your last close. Hayat Amin's rule is specific: "If you are raising in Q3, your profitability model should be board-ready by Q1. Not because investors ask for it immediately, but because the model will reveal problems you need to fix before the process starts."

The six-month lead time is not about the model. It is about the business changes the model exposes. If your unit economics show that one customer segment is unprofitable, you need time to fix pricing, renegotiate vendor contracts, or restructure the cost base. None of that happens in the two weeks before a partner meeting.

## FAQ

### What is a path to profitability in simple terms?

A path to profitability is a financial model that shows the specific month when a company's revenue from operations covers all its costs without requiring external funding. It includes unit economics, cost projections, and scenario analysis. Investors in 2026 expect it as a deliverable, not a slide title.

### How long does it take to build a profitability model?

A fractional CFO typically builds a credible profitability model in 60 to 90 days. The first 3 weeks audit the existing numbers, weeks 4-6 build unit economics by cohort, weeks 7-9 model scenarios, and weeks 10-12 package the output for investors and the board.

### Do pre-revenue startups need a path to profitability?

Yes. Pre-revenue startups model the path using assumed unit economics from comparable companies and pilot data. The model is less precise but still shows investors that the founder understands the cost structure and has a thesis on when the business breaks even.

### What is the difference between a profitability model and a revenue forecast?

A revenue forecast projects income. A profitability model projects the relationship between income and every cost required to generate it — including costs that scale nonlinearly. A company can hit its revenue forecast and still miss profitability if costs grow faster than revenue.
