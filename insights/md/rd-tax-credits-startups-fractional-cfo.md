# R&D Tax Credits for Startups: Why 40% of Qualifying Spend Never Gets Claimed

The average tech startup leaves 40% of its qualifying R&D expenditure unclaimed every year. According to a 2025 HMRC analysis of SME R&D tax credit claims, one in three submissions is materially undervalued — not because the rules are complicated, but because the person filing the claim has never set foot in an engineering stand-up.

Hayat Amin, who has rebuilt finance functions across multiple portfolio companies as a fractional CFO, argues that R&D tax credits are the single highest-ROI finance task a startup can fix — and the one most founders hand to the wrong person. "Your accountant sees invoices. A fractional CFO sees qualifying activities," Hayat Amin says. "The gap between those two perspectives is usually six figures."

That six-figure gap compounds. A startup that underclaims by £80,000 per year for three years has left £240,000 on the table — enough to fund two senior hires or extend runway by a full quarter. Companies with patents are [10.2x more likely to secure early-stage funding](https://beyondelevation.com), and the R&D tax credit is often the bridge that keeps the lights on while the patent portfolio matures.

## What Are R&D Tax Credits and Why Do Most Startups Underclaim?

R&D tax credits are government incentives that refund between 15% and 33% of qualifying research and development expenditure, depending on jurisdiction, company size, and profitability. In the UK, the merged R&D scheme launched in April 2024 offers an above-the-line credit of 20% for most companies, with enhanced rates up to 27% for R&D-intensive loss-making SMEs. In the US, IRC Section 41 provides a federal credit of up to 20% of qualifying expenditure, and startups with less than $5 million in gross receipts can offset up to $500,000 against payroll taxes annually.

Most startups underclaim for one reason: the person preparing the claim works from accounting records, not engineering records. An accountant captures obvious costs — lab equipment, specialist software licences, patent filing fees. They miss the engineering team's time spent on technical uncertainty, the cloud compute costs for training an internal model, the subcontractor who built a prototype integration, and the failed experiments that never shipped.

That gap averages 35–45% of total qualifying expenditure according to ForrestBrown's 2025 UK R&D claims analysis. For a startup spending £800,000 a year on engineering, that is £80,000 to £120,000 in annual tax relief left uncollected.

## What Qualifies as R&D for Tax Credit Purposes?

Qualifying R&D activities are projects that seek to achieve an advance in science or technology by resolving scientific or technological uncertainty — and the definition is broader than most founders expect. You do not need a lab, a patent, or a PhD on staff. You need an engineering problem where the solution was not readily deducible by a competent professional at the time the work started.

The qualifying cost categories most startups miss:

**Engineering salaries and employer NICs.** Not just the time spent coding the feature, but the time spent investigating why the first three approaches failed. Debugging a novel integration counts. Routine development does not.

**Cloud infrastructure costs.** Compute, storage, and networking costs directly attributable to R&D projects. Training a machine learning model on AWS qualifies. Running the production workload does not.

**Subcontractor and externally provided worker costs.** The freelance engineer who built the data pipeline prototype, the consultancy that ran the feasibility study. These are left out of claims entirely when the accountant does not know the project was R&D.

**Consumables and utilities.** Materials consumed in the R&D process, including power costs for compute-heavy workloads. Small individually, material in aggregate.

**Failed experiments.** The project you abandoned after eight weeks because the approach did not work still qualifies. R&D tax relief rewards the attempt to resolve uncertainty, not the outcome.

## How Much Are R&D Tax Credits Actually Worth to a Startup?

R&D tax credits are worth enough to fund a senior hire, cover three months of cloud costs, or extend runway by a full quarter — and the exact amount depends on three variables: where you are incorporated, whether you are profitable, and how aggressively you capture qualifying costs.

**UK numbers (merged RDEC scheme, April 2024 onward):** Most companies receive an above-the-line credit of 20% of qualifying expenditure. R&D-intensive loss-making SMEs qualify for an enhanced rate of up to 27%. A startup spending £600,000 per year on qualifying R&D generates between £120,000 and £162,000 in annual credit — cash in the bank, not a deduction on paper.

**US numbers (IRC Section 41):** The federal credit is 20% of qualifying expenditure above a base amount, or a simplified credit of 14% of expenditure exceeding 50% of the three-year average. Startups under $5 million in gross receipts can apply up to $500,000 of the credit against payroll taxes — a meaningful cash benefit before profitability. Most states stack an additional 5–10% credit on top.

## Why Does a Fractional CFO Find More R&D Tax Credits Than an Accountant?

A fractional CFO finds more qualifying expenditure because they sit inside the business, not outside it. An accountant receives a year-end spreadsheet of costs. A fractional CFO attends the engineering stand-up, reads the sprint tickets, and knows which project spent three weeks resolving a technical uncertainty that never made it into any invoice.

Hayat Amin calls this the **R&D Capture Gap**: the difference between what the accounting records show and what actually qualifies. "Most accountants are not negligent — they are working from the wrong data source," Hayat Amin says. "The qualifying costs live in Jira, not in Xero."

The **Hayat Amin R&D Capture Method** works in three layers:

**Layer 1 — Project mapping.** The CFO maps every active engineering project to a qualifying/non-qualifying determination within the first 30 days. This creates the universe of claimable work. Most accountants skip this step entirely and work backward from costs instead of forward from projects.

**Layer 2 — Time allocation.** The CFO builds a lightweight time-tracking protocol that separates R&D hours from routine development. This is not a timesheet exercise — it is a weekly 10-minute tag on existing sprint data. The output is a defensible allocation ratio per project, per engineer.

**Layer 3 — Technical narrative.** The CFO works with engineering leads to write the competent-professional-test narrative that HMRC or the IRS requires. This is the step most claims fail on audit — not because the costs were wrong, but because nobody documented the uncertainty or how the team tried to resolve it.

The result: the same engineering team, the same spend, a claim that is 30–50% larger, and audit documentation built from day one.

## When Should a Startup Start Claiming R&D Tax Credits?

A startup should start claiming R&D tax credits from the first year it has qualifying expenditure — which, for most tech companies, is year one. The common mistake is waiting until the company is profitable, which wastes two to three years of claimable losses that in the UK can be surrendered for cash and in the US can be carried forward for up to 20 years.

## FAQ

### Can a pre-revenue startup claim R&D tax credits?

Yes. In the UK, loss-making companies can surrender R&D losses for a cash credit worth up to 27% of qualifying expenditure. In the US, startups under $5 million in gross receipts can offset up to $500,000 of the credit against payroll taxes. There is no revenue threshold for eligibility — only qualifying R&D expenditure.

### Do software companies qualify for R&D tax credits?

Yes, provided the work involves resolving genuine technological uncertainty. Building a standard CRUD application using existing frameworks does not qualify. Building a novel data pipeline that solves an unsolved scale or integration problem does. The test is whether a competent professional could have solved the problem without experimentation.

### How long does it take to prepare an R&D tax credit claim?

For companies already working with a fractional CFO, the consultancy fee is wasted margin.

### Can I claim R&D tax credits for AI and machine learning projects?

Yes. AI and machine learning projects frequently qualify because they involve systematic experimentation to resolve uncertainty. Training a model to achieve a performance target, developing a novel architecture, or building tooling that enables a capability that did not previously exist are all qualifying activities. The key is documenting the uncertainty at the outset and the experimental approach taken to resolve it.
