# Virtual CFO vs Fractional CFO: Same Title, 10x Price Gap, Completely Different Jobs

A virtual CFO costs £300 to £800 a month. A fractional CFO costs £3,000 to £12,000. Most founders hire the wrong one and discover the gap during a fundraise, a due diligence process, or a board meeting where the numbers cannot answer the question being asked.

Hayat Amin, who has built and restructured finance functions across three exits including acquirers American Express and TripAdvisor, argues that the label confusion is not accidental. "The vendors selling virtual CFO packages at £500 a month want you to believe you are buying strategic finance," Amin says. "You are buying automated reporting with a human checking the output."

According to Deloitte's 2025 CFO Signals survey, 64% of mid-market companies outsource at least one finance function — but only 19% say their outsourced provider operates at a level they would trust in a boardroom. The gap between what founders think they are buying and what they actually receive is where the real cost sits.

## What Is a Virtual CFO?

A virtual CFO is a remote finance professional — usually employed by an accounting firm or bookkeeping platform — who delivers financial reporting, management accounts, and basic compliance oversight on a monthly retainer. The service runs between £300 and £1,500 a month depending on complexity. It does not include strategy, capital structure advice, or investor communication.

Virtual CFO services typically include monthly management accounts, cash flow reports, tax return preparation, VAT or sales tax compliance, and basic budgeting. The provider works from your accounting software — Xero, QuickBooks, or Sage — and delivers standardised reports on a set schedule.

This is a reporting function. It tells you what happened last month. It does not tell you what to do about it, how to present it to investors, or how to redesign the function for your next stage of growth.

## What Is a Fractional CFO?

A fractional CFO is a part-time C-suite executive who operates inside your business one to three days a week, owns the financial strategy, and is accountable for outcomes a board or investor can underwrite. The role typically costs £3,000 to £12,000 a month and covers fundraising preparation, financial modelling, cash flow management, board reporting, and exit readiness.

The difference is not scope — it is altitude. A fractional CFO does not produce reports. They interpret reports, challenge the numbers, build the finance function, and sit in the room when the decisions are made.

Hayat Amin's Finance Function Maturity Scale breaks this into four levels. Level 1 is bookkeeping: recording transactions. Level 2 is compliance: producing accounts and tax returns. Level 3 is management information: monthly reporting with basic KPIs. Level 4 is strategic finance: capital allocation, scenario planning, investor-grade modelling, and exit preparation. A virtual CFO operates at Level 2 or 3. A fractional CFO operates at Level 4 and builds the infrastructure underneath it.

## When Should You Hire a Virtual CFO?

A virtual CFO is the right hire when your finance needs are compliance-driven and your next twelve months do not include a fundraise, acquisition, or board-level reporting requirement. You are pre-revenue or early revenue. Your accounting is straightforward. You do not have outside investors demanding monthly board packs. Your main pain point is producing accurate numbers on time rather than using those numbers to make decisions.

If you are a solo founder running a services business at £500K revenue with no plans to raise, a virtual CFO at £500 a month will produce your management accounts, handle your VAT, and keep Companies House happy. That is a legitimate, cost-effective finance function for that stage.

The problem is not the virtual CFO. The problem is the founder who stays at this level past the point where the business needs more.

## When Should You Hire a Fractional CFO?

A fractional CFO becomes necessary when the financial decisions facing your business are more complex than the numbers your current finance function can support. Five signals make it clear: you are preparing to raise a round and cannot produce an investor-grade financial model. Your board is asking questions your accountant cannot answer. Your cash flow forecast does not extend beyond next month. You are approaching an exit and have no idea what your business will look like in due diligence. Your finance function is two stages behind your revenue growth.

Hayat Amin reminds founders that investors do not evaluate your product in isolation — they evaluate your numbers. "The CFO is the first person a serious investor calls after the pitch meeting," Amin says. "If that person is a virtual bookkeeper who has never sat across from a term sheet, you have already lost the negotiation before it starts."

## Can You Start With a Virtual CFO and Upgrade Later?

Yes, and most companies do. The mistake is waiting too long. Hayat Amin's rule is specific: upgrade the moment your next milestone — a raise, an exit, a board expansion, a pricing restructure — requires financial decisions, not just financial data. "The founders who lose six months are the ones who believe better reports will solve a strategy problem," Amin argues. "Reports describe the past. A CFO decides the future."

The transition typically happens between £1M and £3M in revenue, when the founder realises the monthly management accounts are no longer enough to run a board meeting, close a funding round, or negotiate a deal. By that point, upgrading to a fractional CFO is not an expense — it is the thing that unlocks the next stage of value.

## FAQ

### Is a virtual CFO the same as a fractional CFO?

No. A virtual CFO provides remote financial reporting and compliance — typically from an accounting firm at £300 to £1,500 per month. A fractional CFO is a part-time C-suite executive who owns your financial strategy, attends board meetings, and prepares the business for raises or exits at £3,000 to £12,000 per month. The roles serve different stages and different needs.

### When should I switch from a virtual CFO to a fractional CFO?

Switch when your next business milestone requires financial decisions, not just financial data. The most common triggers: preparing for a fundraise, approaching an exit, expanding the board, or hitting the £1M to £3M revenue range where investor-grade reporting becomes a requirement.

### Can a fractional CFO do everything a virtual CFO does?

Yes. A fractional CFO can produce management accounts and handle compliance, but that is not the best use of their time or your budget. Most fractional CFO engagements pair with a bookkeeper or accounting platform that handles the transactional work, while the CFO focuses on strategy, modelling, and stakeholder communication.

### How many hours a week does a fractional CFO work?

Typically one to three days per week, depending on the company's stage and the complexity of the finance function. During a fundraise or exit process, the commitment may increase to four or five days per week for a defined period.

### Is a fractional CFO worth it for a small business?

It depends on the business's trajectory, not its size. A lifestyle business generating steady revenue with no plans to raise, exit, or scale aggressively is well served by a virtual CFO. A growth-stage company targeting a raise, a strategic acquisition, or a significant expansion needs the strategic depth a fractional CFO provides. The question is not size — it is where the business is heading next.
