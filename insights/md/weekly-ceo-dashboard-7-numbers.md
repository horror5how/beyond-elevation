# Your Finance Pack Is 40 Pages. You Need One. The Weekly CEO Dashboard That Runs a Company on 7 Numbers.

A weekly CEO dashboard is a single-page view of seven financial and operational numbers that tell a CEO whether the company is on track or bleeding. It replaces the monthly 40-page finance pack — which most CEOs skim two pages of and ignore the rest — with a view that takes 90 seconds to read and drives decisions the same morning. According to the 2026 FP&A Trends survey by the Association for Financial Professionals, 68% of CEOs at companies under $50M revenue receive financial data too infrequently to act on it. Hayat Amin argues the fix is not more data — it is fewer, better numbers delivered every Monday before the CEO’s first meeting: “The 40-page monthly pack exists because the CFO is covering their back, not serving the CEO. A weekly CEO dashboard serves one person and answers one question: are we winning or losing this week?”

## What Belongs on a Weekly CEO Dashboard?

A weekly CEO dashboard should contain exactly seven numbers: cash balance, cash runway, revenue (trailing four weeks), gross margin, net new pipeline, customer churn, and the single most important operating metric for the company’s stage. Everything else is noise that belongs in the CFO’s working files, not the CEO’s decision screen.

Most finance functions overreport. They send the CEO a P&L, a balance sheet, a cash flow statement, an AR aging report, an AP summary, a budget variance report, and a department breakdown — 40 pages that answer every question nobody asked. The weekly CEO dashboard answers only the questions that drive action: do we have cash, are we making money, is the pipeline growing, and are we keeping customers.

## The 7 Numbers on the Hayat Amin Monday Dashboard

Hayat Amin’s Monday Dashboard is the exact framework Beyond Elevation installs in the first week of every fractional CFO engagement. Seven numbers, one page, updated by 8am Monday, sent before the CEO’s first meeting of the week. Here is what goes on it:

**1. Cash balance.** Not a forecast — the actual bank number as of Monday morning. This is the single most important number in any growth-stage company. If the CEO does not know the cash balance on Monday, the finance function has already failed.

**2. Cash runway.** Months of operating cash at current burn rate. This number determines urgency. Below six months triggers a fundraise or a cost review. Below three months triggers emergency mode. The runway figure should be recalculated weekly, not rolled forward from last month’s close.

**3. Revenue (trailing four weeks).** Rolling, not calendar month, so the CEO sees momentum in real time. A calendar-month view hides a bad final week behind a strong first three weeks. Trailing four weeks makes the trend visible the moment it changes.

**4. Gross margin.** The number that tells you whether growth is profitable or whether you are buying revenue at a loss. If gross margin is declining while revenue is rising, the company is scaling a problem. This is the number most CEOs learn about two months too late.

**5. Net new pipeline.** Total qualified pipeline added minus deals lost in the past seven days. Revenue is a lagging indicator. Pipeline is the leading one. A CEO who sees pipeline shrinking for two consecutive weeks can act before the revenue line reflects it.

**6. Customer churn.** Number of customers or logos lost in the past 30 days, updated weekly. For subscription businesses, this is the number that determines whether growth is real or a treadmill. For services businesses, it signals delivery problems before they reach the P&L.

**7. One stage-specific metric.** Pre-revenue companies track burn multiple (net burn divided by net new ARR). Series A companies track net revenue retention. Series B and beyond track the rule of 40 (revenue growth rate plus profit margin). This seventh number is calibrated to the company’s stage in the first week of the engagement.

## Why Does Weekly CEO Reporting Beat Monthly?

Weekly financial reporting lets CEOs act on problems within days instead of discovering them four weeks late. A monthly P&L tells you what happened. A weekly CEO dashboard tells you what is happening — early enough to change it.

The maths is direct. A company burning £120,000 per month that discovers a margin problem in the monthly close has already lost £120,000 before anyone sees the number. A weekly dashboard catches the same problem after £30,000 of damage. Over a year, that cadence difference is the difference between a recoverable slip and a cash crisis.

Hayat Amin proved this during a fractional CFO engagement where the company moved from monthly to weekly reporting. The CEO identified a customer churn spike in week two of the quarter, resolved it by week four, and recovered £180,000 in annual recurring revenue that would have been invisible until the quarterly close. “The CEO did not need more data,” Hayat Amin says. “They needed the same data 21 days sooner.”

## How Does a Fractional CFO Build a Weekly CEO Dashboard?

A fractional CFO builds a weekly CEO dashboard by connecting three systems — the accounting platform, the CRM, and the bank feed — into a single automated view that updates every Monday morning. The build takes five working days in a typical engagement.

**Day 1:** Audit the CEO’s current reporting stack and delete everything that does not answer one of the seven questions above. Most companies discover they are producing 30 pages of reports nobody reads.

**Day 2:** Configure automated data pulls from the accounting platform (cash balance, gross margin), the CRM (pipeline, churn), and the bank API (real-time cash). No manual spreadsheets. Every number must pull automatically.

**Day 3:** Build the one-page dashboard in a live format — Google Sheet, Notion database, or a lightweight BI tool depending on the company’s stack. No PDFs. No static reports. The CEO should be able to drill into any number with one click.

**Day 4:** Test the automated update. Every number must refresh by 8am Monday with zero manual input from the finance team.

**Day 5:** Run the first live Monday review with the CEO. Calibrate the seventh metric to the company’s stage. Set the weekly cadence.

## What Should a CEO Do With These Numbers Each Monday?

The CEO should spend 90 seconds reading the weekly CEO dashboard and 10 minutes making one decision. Not reviewing, not discussing, not requesting more data — making one decision that changes the trajectory of the week.

If cash runway dropped below six months: trigger a cost review or accelerate the fundraise timeline. If gross margin declined two weeks in a row: find out why before the next pipeline call. If net new pipeline fell below the four-week average: get the sales lead on a call before lunch. If churn spiked: escalate to the customer success team the same morning.

The weekly CEO dashboard is a decision tool, not a reporting tool. The moment it becomes something the CEO “reviews” instead of “acts on,” the finance function is not serving the business. Hayat Amin’s rule is direct: if the CEO opens the dashboard and makes no decision, either the company is perfectly on track or the dashboard has the wrong numbers on it.
