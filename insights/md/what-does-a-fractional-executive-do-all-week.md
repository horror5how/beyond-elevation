# What Does a Fractional Executive Actually Do All Week? A Real Schedule, Not a Sales Pitch

Most fractional executive job descriptions read like a LinkedIn fantasy. "Strategic leadership. Visionary guidance. Transformational outcomes." Zero specifics. Zero accountability. Zero insight into what the person actually does between Monday and Friday.

A fractional executive runs a specific function inside your company — finance, IP strategy, or AI operations — on a part-time basis, typically two to four days per week, delivering full executive outcomes without the full-time salary. According to a 2026 Deloitte survey, 73% of mid-market companies now use at least one fractional executive, up from 41% in 2023. The growth happened because founders discovered that what a fractional executive does in 12 focused hours often exceeds what a full-time hire delivers in 50 distracted ones. Hayat Amin argues this is not a staffing trend — it is a permanent restructuring of how companies buy executive capability.

Here is the week. No jargon. No sales pitch. Just the schedule.

## What Does a Fractional Executive Do in a Typical Week?

A fractional executive spends roughly 60% of their week on delivery work — the actual outputs the company hired them to produce — and 40% on the management, communication, and strategic planning that makes those outputs land. The split varies by role, but the rhythm is consistent across every operator Beyond Elevation places.

The structure below reflects what Hayat Amin calls the **Fractional Operator Cadence Framework** — the weekly rhythm every Beyond Elevation operator runs regardless of whether they are serving as a [fractional CFO](/insights/what-is-a-fractional-cfo/), a [Chief IP Officer](/insights/what-is-a-chief-ip-officer/), or an [AI Operations lead](/insights/what-is-ai-operations/).

**Monday: alignment and triage.** The week starts with a 30-minute standup with the CEO or founder. The fractional executive reviews the prior week's deliverables, flags blockers, and locks the week's priorities. A fractional CFO uses this slot to confirm cash position and flag any covenant or reporting deadlines. A fractional Chief IP Officer reviews the patent filing pipeline and flags approaching deadlines. An AI Operations lead checks agent uptime dashboards and triages any production failures from the weekend. Total time: 2-3 hours including prep and follow-up.

**Tuesday: deep delivery work.** This is the core output day. No meetings before noon. A fractional CFO builds the monthly management accounts, reconciles intercompany balances, or prepares investor reporting packs. A Chief IP Officer drafts patent claims, reviews prior art searches, or structures licensing term sheets. An AI Operations lead deploys new agent workflows, debugs inference pipelines, or runs cost-optimisation analysis on the AI stack. Total time: 4-6 hours of uninterrupted execution.

**Wednesday: stakeholder and team sessions.** Midweek is for the people work. The fractional executive runs a team meeting with the function they own — the finance team, the IP counsel, or the engineering squad responsible for AI infrastructure. This is also the day for external calls: auditors, patent attorneys, vendors, investors requesting data. A fractional CFO walks the board through draft financials. A Chief IP Officer briefs outside counsel on filing strategy. An AI Operations lead demos a new automation to the leadership team. Total time: 3-4 hours.

**Thursday: strategic and forward-looking work.** With the week's fires handled, Thursday is for work that compounds. A fractional CFO builds the 13-week cash flow forecast, models scenario analysis for the next fundraise, or redesigns the chart of accounts. A Chief IP Officer maps the competitive patent landscape, identifies white-space filing opportunities, or values the portfolio for an upcoming board presentation. An AI Operations lead evaluates new tools, architects the next automation sprint, or writes the business case for expanding the AI function. Total time: 3-4 hours.

**Friday: documentation, handover, and async reporting.** The fractional executive closes the week by documenting everything they did — not for compliance, but so the company retains the knowledge whether the operator stays or goes. A written weekly update goes to the CEO covering: what shipped, what is blocked, what needs a decision, and what is coming next week. This handover discipline is what separates a fractional operator from a freelancer. Total time: 1-2 hours.

## How Many Hours Does a Fractional Executive Work Per Week?

A fractional executive typically works 10 to 16 hours per week for each client, with surge capacity for high-stakes periods like board meetings, fundraising rounds, due diligence processes, or regulatory deadlines. The contracted hours are not the point — the contracted outcomes are.

Hayat Amin puts it directly: "A full-time CFO works 50 hours a week and spends 30 of them in meetings about meetings. A fractional CFO works 12 hours and spends 10 of them producing the actual deliverables the board reads. The company gets better output at a third of the cost."

Standard engagement structures at Beyond Elevation run two to four days per week, with the specific allocation depending on the complexity of the function and the company's stage. A pre-Series A startup with simple books needs two days of fractional CFO time. A £15M revenue company preparing for exit needs four days — the same hours a full-time hire would spend on productive work after stripping out internal politics, all-hands meetings, and HR processes.

## What Is the Difference Between What a Fractional Executive Delivers and What an Adviser Delivers?

An adviser produces recommendations. A fractional executive produces outcomes. The gap between those two words is the entire value proposition — and it is the reason companies pay £3,000 to £8,000 per month for a fractional operator instead of £500 per hour for a consulting engagement that ends with a slide deck.

Advisers attend quarterly board meetings, review materials someone else prepared, and offer opinions. Fractional executives build the materials, run the function, manage the team, and own the number. When Hayat Amin places an operator into a client company, the brief is explicit: you own the function's output the same way a full-time executive would. The only difference is the hours.

Concretely, here is what each role delivers in a typical month — not advises on, delivers:

**Fractional CFO deliverables:** closed monthly books within 5 working days, board-ready financial pack, 13-week rolling cash flow forecast, investor data room maintenance, audit preparation, and covenant compliance reporting.

**Fractional Chief IP Officer deliverables:** patent filing roadmap updates, competitive landscape monitoring reports, licensing pipeline management, IP portfolio valuation for board reporting, and trade secret audit documentation.

**AI Operations lead deliverables:** deployed and monitored AI agents, automation ROI tracking dashboards, agent failure analysis and remediation, vendor evaluation reports, and AI infrastructure cost management.

The test is simple: if the person leaves, does the function keep running? An adviser's departure changes nothing because they were never running anything. A fractional executive's departure leaves a gap — which is exactly why the documentation discipline described in the Friday cadence above exists.

## How Do Fractional Executives Manage Multiple Clients?

Most fractional executives serve two to three clients simultaneously, structured so that no two clients share the same deep-work day. The Fractional Operator Cadence Framework handles this by assigning each client a fixed weekly rhythm — same days, same time blocks, same reporting cadence — so the operator builds muscle memory and the client gets predictability.

Three practices make multi-client operation work without quality erosion.

**Async-first communication.** Fractional executives do not sit in Slack all day. They batch communications into defined windows — typically the Monday alignment call and the Friday written update — and handle urgent items via a single escalation channel. This protects deep-work blocks from the ambient noise that destroys full-time executives' productivity.

**Documented playbooks.** Every process the fractional executive builds is documented as a repeatable playbook the company's team can run independently. The month-end close checklist. The patent filing workflow. The AI agent deployment runbook. These playbooks mean the operator's value compounds even when they are working on another client's deliverables.

**Surge protocols.** Board meetings, fundraising sprints, and due diligence periods require more hours than the standard retainer. Beyond Elevation structures every engagement with a defined surge mechanism — pre-agreed additional days at a known rate — so neither party negotiates under pressure when a time-sensitive event lands.

## When Does a Company Outgrow a Fractional Executive?

A fractional executive engagement is not permanent by design — it is built to either scale into a full-time role when the company's complexity demands it, or to continue indefinitely when the function does not require a full-time seat. Hayat Amin says the signal is volume, not importance: "When the weekly deliverable list exceeds what 16 focused hours can produce, the function needs a full-time owner. That usually happens between £10M and £25M in revenue, depending on the role."

The transition from fractional to full-time is itself a deliverable. A good fractional executive builds the job description, interviews candidates, onboards their replacement, and hands over every playbook and process they created. The company should never lose institutional knowledge in the transition — if they do, the fractional operator failed at the documentation layer.

## FAQ

### What does a fractional executive do differently from a consultant?

A fractional executive owns a function and produces its deliverables — closed books, filed patents, deployed AI agents. A consultant advises on what should be done and leaves the execution to your team. The fractional executive is accountable for outcomes, not recommendations. They manage the team, hit the deadlines, and own the numbers.

### How many hours per week does a fractional executive work?

Typically 10 to 16 hours per week per client, structured across two to four fixed days. Hours increase during surge periods like fundraising rounds, board meetings, or due diligence processes. The contracted outcomes matter more than the contracted hours — a fractional executive is measured by what they ship, not by time logged.

### Can a fractional executive work for competitors simultaneously?

No. Reputable fractional executive firms enforce strict conflict-of-interest policies. Beyond Elevation will not place an operator with two companies in the same market. Each engagement includes a non-compete clause covering the client's direct competitive set for the duration of the engagement plus a cooling-off period.
