# We Turn Away 40% of Inbound Leads. Here Are the 5 Signs a Company Is Not Ready for a Fractional Executive.

A fractional executive is the wrong hire for roughly 40 percent of the companies that contact Beyond Elevation. Not because those companies are bad — because they are at the wrong stage, the wrong scope, or the wrong timeline for fractional leadership to work. According to Harvard Business Review's 2026 workforce analysis, the fractional C-suite market has grown over 300 percent since 2020 — but growth has attracted a flood of both unqualified operators and unprepared buyers. Hayat Amin tells every Beyond Elevation operator the same thing on day one: the fastest way to damage the fractional model is to let a company hire you when you cannot deliver. That is why the first call is a diagnostic, not a pitch.

This post covers the five company profiles where a fractional executive engagement fails — and what those companies should hire instead.

## What Makes a Company Not Ready for a Fractional Executive?

A company is not ready for a fractional executive when it lacks the decision-making infrastructure, revenue base, or engagement clarity to extract value from part-time leadership. The fractional model works because an experienced operator compresses years of strategic work into two or three days per week. That compression only functions when the company can act on what the operator builds. Without that, you are paying executive rates for consulting advice that sits in a slide deck.

## Sign 1: Your Revenue Is Below £500K and You Have No Finance Function at All

A company generating under half a million in annual revenue almost never needs a fractional CFO. It needs a bookkeeper and a cloud accounting stack. Hayat Amin argues that the single most expensive mistake at this stage is hiring upstream — bringing in a strategist when the plumbing does not exist. A fractional CFO assumes there are numbers to work with: a chart of accounts, bank reconciliations, aged receivables. If those do not exist, the fractional CFO spends the first three months building what a £25-per-hour bookkeeper should have built, at ten times the cost.

**What to hire instead:** A qualified bookkeeper on Xero or QuickBooks, plus a part-time financial controller if you are approaching £1M. Save the fractional CFO for when you need board reporting, fundraising preparation, or exit-readiness work that a controller cannot do.

## Sign 2: The CEO Will Not Delegate Decision Authority

This is the failure mode Hayat Amin's First-Call Diagnostic catches most often. The CEO says they want a fractional executive — but what they actually want is validation. They want someone senior to agree with decisions they have already made. A fractional executive without decision authority is an expensive consultant who cannot ship anything. The engagement stalls within six weeks because every recommendation requires a founder sign-off that never comes.

The diagnostic question Beyond Elevation uses: *Name one decision in the last quarter that you would have delegated to this role if it existed.* If the CEO cannot name a single one, they are not ready to let go. And if they cannot let go, fractional leadership will not work.

**What to hire instead:** A non-executive director or an advisory board member who provides strategic counsel without needing execution authority. This satisfies the CEO's desire for a sounding board without the cost or friction of a fractional operator who has no room to operate.

## Sign 3: You Need Daily Presence for a Crisis or Turnaround

Fractional executives work two to three days per week. That cadence is designed for building systems, not fighting fires. If the company is in genuine distress — a cash crisis, a regulatory investigation, a failed product launch that threatens the business — it needs someone in the building five days a week until the bleeding stops. A fractional operator in a turnaround is like a part-time surgeon: technically qualified, but not present enough to keep the patient alive.

**What to hire instead:** An interim executive on a three-to-six-month full-time contract. Interim executives are turnaround specialists who embed completely, stabilise the situation, then exit. Beyond Elevation's own data shows that companies in crisis who hire fractional instead of interim take 2.4 times longer to stabilise — because the fractional operator loses context between visits and the fire reignites every Thursday.

## Sign 4: The Scope Is Pure Execution, Not Strategy

Fractional executives are strategic operators. They design the finance function, build the IP portfolio architecture, or wire the AI operations stack. They do not process invoices, file patent forms, or configure automation tools full-time. If the company's primary need is execution — someone to do the daily work of a function — then it needs a senior individual contributor, not a fractional C-suite operator.

Hayat Amin reminds founders that the test is simple: if the job description is a list of tasks, you need a hire. If the job description is a set of outcomes, you need a fractional executive. Tasks are inputs. Outcomes are results. A fractional CFO's outcome is investor-ready numbers. The task of reconciling bank statements is not their job — it is the infrastructure they build so the reconciliation happens without them.

**What to hire instead:** A senior individual contributor — a financial controller, an IP paralegal, or an AI engineer — who owns the execution layer. Pair them with a fractional executive later, once the execution base exists and you need someone to set the direction.

## Sign 5: Your Engagement Timeline Exceeds 18 Months With No Off-Ramp

Fractional engagements are designed to be finite. The operator builds a function, hires the permanent team, transfers knowledge, and exits. If the company's plan requires a fractional executive indefinitely — with no intention to hire full-time or build internal capability — then the economics stop working. Beyond 18 months at two to three days per week, a full-time hire at the same seniority level is almost always cheaper and more effective. The fractional model's value is speed and expertise compression, not permanent headcount substitution.

**What to hire instead:** A full-time executive recruited through a specialist search firm. Use the fractional engagement as a bridge — three to six months to define the role, build the function, and create the job specification — then hire permanently and let the fractional operator hand off.

## What Does Hayat Amin's First-Call Diagnostic Actually Test?

Hayat Amin's First-Call Diagnostic is a five-question filter that Beyond Elevation runs on every inbound enquiry before any proposal is written. It takes fifteen minutes and catches the five failure modes above before they cost either side money. The five questions are:

**1. Revenue and finance maturity.** Is the company generating enough revenue to justify executive-level spend, and does a basic finance or operations infrastructure already exist?

**2. Decision authority.** Will the fractional executive have the authority to make and execute decisions within their domain, or will every move require founder approval?

**3. Urgency profile.** Is this a build engagement (two to three days per week, six to twelve months) or a crisis engagement (five days per week, immediate)? If crisis, fractional is the wrong model.

**4. Scope clarity.** Can the company articulate three to five outcomes they need from this engagement? If the answer is a task list, they need an employee. If the answer is outcomes with measurable targets, fractional works.

**5. Exit timeline.** Does the engagement have a defined end state — a full-time hire, a completed function build, a successful fundraise — or is it open-ended?

Companies that score three out of five or below are redirected. Beyond Elevation either recommends a different hire or suggests they return when their stage has changed. This is not generosity — it is quality control. A failed fractional engagement damages the operator's reputation, the company's trust in the model, and the broader market's credibility.

## When Is a Company Ready for a Fractional Executive?

The 60 percent of companies that pass the diagnostic share specific characteristics. They have revenue above £1M. They have at least a basic operational infrastructure. The CEO has identified specific outcomes — not tasks — that they need delivered. The engagement has a clear timeline and exit condition. And the CEO is willing to delegate real authority within the fractional executive's domain. Hayat Amin says the single best predictor of a successful fractional engagement is whether the CEO can answer this question: *What does done look like?* If they can describe the end state in one sentence, the engagement will work. If they cannot, it will not.

## FAQ

### What percentage of fractional executive engagements fail?

Industry data varies, but Beyond Elevation's internal figures show that engagements which pass the First-Call Diagnostic have a 91 percent completion rate. Engagements that skip the diagnostic or ignore red flags fail at roughly three times that rate. The diagnostic is the difference.

### Can a fractional executive work for a pre-revenue startup?

Rarely. A pre-revenue startup typically needs execution — building the product, closing the first customers — not C-suite strategy. The exception is fundraising preparation, where a fractional CFO or Chief IP Officer can add immediate value by structuring the investor narrative, financial model, or patent portfolio before a raise. Outside of that specific use case, wait until you have revenue and repeatable operations.

### How do I know if I need an interim or a fractional executive?

If the engagement requires full-time presence for fewer than six months to stabilise a crisis, you need an interim. If it requires two to three days per week for six to eighteen months to build a function, you need a fractional executive. The simplest test: is the building on fire, or does it need a new wing? Interim puts out fires. Fractional builds wings.

### What does a fractional executive engagement cost compared to a full-time hire?

A fractional CFO typically costs £3,000 to £7,000 per month for two days per week. A full-time CFO at the same seniority level costs £120,000 to £180,000 per year in salary alone, plus benefits, equity, and recruitment fees. The fractional model is 40 to 60 percent cheaper on an annual basis — but only if the engagement is scoped correctly and has a defined end state.
