12 Questions That Expose a Weak Fractional CFO in the First Call

Hayat Amin · Updated 2026-08-29

The 12 questions to ask a fractional CFO that separate operators from credential-holders in the first call — plus the red-flag answers that should end the conversation.

Most founders hire a fractional CFO the same way they hire an accountant: check the credentials, glance at the rate, and hope for the best. That approach works fine for bookkeeping. It is a disaster for the hire that will own your cash, your close, and your fundraise.

Hayat Amin argues the problem is not the candidate pool — it is the interview. "Founders ask about qualifications when they should be asking about outcomes," Hayat Amin says. According to a 2025 Deloitte CFO Survey, 42 percent of companies that hired interim or fractional finance leaders reported the engagement failed to meet expectations. The gap was not competence. It was fit, operating rhythm, and scope alignment — all things the right questions to ask a fractional CFO on the first call would have caught.

Here are the 12 questions that separate operators from credential-holders in under 60 minutes.

What Questions Should You Ask a Fractional CFO About Their Operating Experience?

The first questions to ask a fractional CFO should test whether they have sat in the seat, not beside it. A real operator has run a close, owned a board pack, and survived a due diligence process. An advisor has watched someone else do it. The difference shows up in the answers.

Question 1: How many month-end closes have you personally owned, and what was your fastest?

The answer you want: a specific number and a specific timeline. "I have owned 60-plus closes across four companies. My fastest stable close was two business days." A vague answer — "I have experience with the close process" — means they advised on it. They did not own it.

Question 2: Walk me through the last fundraise you ran the finance side of. What was the round size and what did you build for the data room?

This forces specifics. A strong candidate names the round size, the investor, and the exact deliverables: three-statement model, cohort analysis, cap table, legal entity structure. A weak candidate generalises. Hayat Amin's rule is blunt: "If they cannot name the investor and the round size, they were not in the room."

Question 3: Have you ever been through a due diligence process as the finance lead on the sell side?

Sell-side diligence is where fractional CFOs earn their fee or expose their limits. An exit-ready CFO has built the diligence pack before the bankers arrived, not scrambled to assemble it after the LOI. If exit is anywhere on your horizon, this question is non-negotiable.

What Questions to Ask a Fractional CFO About Your Finance Infrastructure?

A fractional CFO who cannot describe what they will build in the first 90 days is a contractor, not an operator. These questions test whether the candidate can architect a finance function, not just maintain one that already exists.

Question 4: What does my finance stack look like on day 90 if you start next Monday?

The answer should be specific to your stage: ERP or accounting platform choice, AP/AR automation, reporting cadence, KPI dashboard, and cash flow forecasting model. Generic answers — "I will assess your needs and make recommendations" — signal a consultant, not a builder. Beyond Elevation's fractional CFOs deliver a written 90-day plan before the first invoice.

Question 5: What is the most broken finance function you inherited, and what did you do in the first 30 days?

This is the war story question. Operators have them. Advisors do not. You want to hear about specific problems — a 25-day close they compressed to five, a cash forecast that was off by 40 percent, a revenue recognition mess that was blocking an audit. The fix matters less than the specificity.

Question 6: How do you handle board reporting? Show me a board pack you have built.

If they cannot show you one, they have not built one. A board pack is the single most visible deliverable a CFO produces. Hayat Amin says most fractional CFOs fail this question because "they have built management reports, not board packs. The audience is different, the metrics are different, and the narrative is different."

What Questions Reveal Whether a Fractional CFO Can Support a Fundraise or Exit?

Fundraising and exit preparation demand a skill set that most bookkeeping-grade CFOs do not have. These questions to ask a fractional CFO separate the ones who have sat across from investors from the ones who have only built reports for them.

Question 7: What is the lowest-quality metric you see founders put in front of investors, and what do you replace it with?

This tests financial judgement, not just technical skill. A strong CFO names a specific vanity metric — gross revenue instead of net, customer count instead of cohort retention, total contract value instead of ARR — and explains what investors actually read. This is the kind of pattern recognition that Hayat Amin calls the Investor Translation Test: can your CFO speak the language VCs use to price risk?

Question 8: If I told you we are 18 months from an exit, what would you start building this week?

An exit-ready CFO has a checklist: quality of earnings prep, normalised EBITDA build, customer concentration analysis, revenue quality audit, IP asset register, working capital model. If the answer is "depends on the situation," the candidate has not done it before. Beyond Elevation starts exit preparation 18 months out because that is how long it takes to fix the problems that discount your multiple.

Question 9: Have you ever had to defend a number in a due diligence meeting? What happened?

Due diligence is adversarial. The buyer's team is looking for reasons to reduce the price. A CFO who has defended numbers under pressure understands how to prepare for it. A CFO who has not will build the data room like a filing exercise instead of a defence brief.

What Questions Expose How a Fractional CFO Actually Works?

The operating model matters as much as the skill set. A fractional CFO who charges full-time rates for part-time availability, or who disappears between monthly visits, will cost you more in frustration than no CFO at all. These questions set expectations before the engagement starts.

Question 10: How many clients do you work with simultaneously, and what does my week look like?

More than four simultaneous clients and you are buying calendar scraps, not strategic attention. The answer should include a specific weekly rhythm: "I am in your systems Monday and Thursday, I run your weekly cash meeting Tuesday, and I am available on Slack for ad hoc questions." If the answer is vague, the engagement will be too.

Question 11: What is your handover plan if we outgrow the fractional model?

A good fractional CFO plans their own exit. They should describe the documentation they leave behind, the systems they build so a junior hire can run them, and the trigger points that signal when you need a full-time CFO. A candidate who does not have a handover answer is building dependency, not a function. Hayat Amin's view is direct: "If the finance function breaks when the CFO leaves, they did not build a function. They rented one."

Question 12: What will you say no to?

This is the question most founders skip and most regret skipping. A fractional CFO is not a bookkeeper, a tax preparer, or an FP&A analyst. They should clearly define what falls outside their scope and who should own it. The ones who say yes to everything deliver nothing well. The best fractional CFOs have a sharp no list because they know exactly where they add value and where they do not.

What Do Red-Flag Answers Sound Like?

Red-flag answers share three traits across all 12 questions: they are generic instead of specific, they describe processes instead of outcomes, and they reference credentials instead of results. Any candidate who answers more than three of these questions with "it depends" has not operated at the level your company needs.

The other red flag is pricing avoidance. If a candidate cannot state their rate structure — monthly retainer, day rate, or project fee — within the first call, their commercial model is unclear. According to a 2026 Fractional Executive Association survey, the median fractional CFO retainer sits between £3,000 and £8,000 per month depending on scope and stage. A candidate who will not name a number before the second call is either overpriced or unsure of their own value.

FAQ

How many questions should I ask a fractional CFO in a first call?

Ask at least eight of these 12 questions in a 45 to 60 minute first call. Prioritise the operating experience questions (1 through 3) and the working model questions (10 through 12), because these reveal fit faster than technical questions. Save the exit and fundraising questions for a second call if exit is not imminent.

What is the biggest mistake founders make when hiring a fractional CFO?

The biggest mistake is treating the fractional CFO hire like a senior accountant hire. Founders check qualifications and rates but skip the operating experience questions that separate builders from advisors. A fractional CFO who has never owned a close or built a board pack will cost you more in wasted months than the retainer difference between them and an operator.

How much should a good fractional CFO cost?

Fractional CFO retainers range from £3,000 to £8,000 per month for one to two days per week of engagement, depending on company stage and scope. Day rates typically run £1,200 to £2,500. If a candidate quotes significantly below these ranges, question the experience level. If they quote above, ensure the scope justifies the premium. See our full breakdown at What a Fractional CFO Actually Costs in 2026.

When should I hire a fractional CFO instead of a full-time one?

Hire fractional when your company needs CFO-grade thinking but cannot justify or afford a £150,000 to £250,000 full-time salary. That typically means pre-Series B companies, companies preparing for a specific event like a fundraise or exit, and companies that need to build a finance function from scratch before hiring a permanent team.

What should a fractional CFO deliver in the first 90 days?

A strong fractional CFO delivers five things in 90 days: a clean month-end close process running in under five business days, a cash flow forecast updated weekly, a board pack template with the metrics your investors actually read, a finance stack recommendation with implementation started, and a written plan for the next 90 days. If they have not delivered at least three of these, the engagement is underperforming.
