Rob Eijgenraam: 9 Years Raising Capital Without Equity

TokenMinds · Updated 2026-11-27

TokenMinds founder Rob Eijgenraam has spent nine years helping companies raise capital through token sales while the owners keep their equity.

Guest article from TokenMinds, published as part of a content exchange with Beyond Elevation.

TL;DR: Rob Eijgenraam is the founder of TokenMinds. Since 2017, he has helped various companies raise capital through token sales. This method allows founders to raise funds while retaining full ownership of their companies. His work, alongside his team, has resulted in a documented total of over $25 million raised. Rob believes this method is most effective for established businesses with real revenue. Therefore, he wants to raise awareness about it to a wider audience.

Companies have several ways to fund their growth. One well-known way is to sell shares, which reduces the founders' ownership. Another is to borrow money, which must be repaid with interest. A token sale is another option, and it is now receiving more attention. On August 18, 2026, the US Securities and Exchange Commission proposed Regulation Crypto Assets. Under this proposal, a company could raise up to $5 million through a token offering over four years. Members of the public could also invest, up to a set limit.

However, a proposed rule does not make a company ready to sell a token. Most established businesses have revenue and a good track record. They often lack the token design and the investor network that a sale needs. Rob Eijgenraam has spent nine years helping companies with exactly this problem.

Who Is Rob Eijgenraam?

Rob Eijgenraam did not begin his career in crypto. He is the founder of TokenMinds, a global company that manages token launches. Before that, he built his early career in e-commerce and digital marketing and worked as a director at the online retailer Zalora. In 2017, he founded TokenMinds, and he has led the company since then.

The following years were not always easy. After the collapse of the FTX exchange in 2022, TokenMinds lost funds and reported losses for two years. To keep the company running during this period, Rob took over the daily management himself and made its projects simpler. Today, he says that this period made the business stronger. "Hard times make the company better," he told Entrepreneurship Life.

Rob now shares these lessons with the wider business community, often through industry events. At iFX Expo Asia 2026, he described how the 2022 crash destroyed five years of work and how TokenMinds recovered by focusing on real businesses that want to enter crypto. In the same year, he gave a presentation at Money20/20 Asia, a major fintech conference, and discussed AI as a panelist at Coinfest Asia. His audience is not limited to Asia. In 2025, he was a speaker at Zebu Live, a Web3 conference in London, and gave a talk about entrepreneurship at Blockchain Community Day. He has been a conference speaker since at least 2018, when he appeared at Blockchain Expo Europe. Outside of events, he also mentors founders at Techstars and Startup Boost.

He continues the same discussion online. In recent months, he was a guest on Fintech Fridays, What The Tech? and the CallumConnects Micro-Podcast. On stage and in podcasts, he often returns to one question, which is how an established company can raise capital without selling any shares.

How Does a Company Raise Capital Without Giving Up Equity?

Rob's answer to that question is a token sale. A token is a digital asset that a company creates and sells to buyers, and each sale is recorded on a blockchain, which works as a shared public record. Because a token is not a share in the company, the founders keep full ownership while the company receives new capital.

In return, buyers receive something they can use in the business. One example from TokenMinds' work is LOL Token, which was launched by an education group that has run schools in Thailand for over 20 years. Learners who paid for courses with the token received a 20% discount.

However, a token sale is not the right choice for every company, and Rob addresses this point directly. As a guest on the Fintech Fridays podcast, he discussed the situations where traditional financing is the better option. The same episode covered why placing an asset on a blockchain does not automatically attract investors. In addition, the law on tokens is different in each country, so a company should get legal advice before it starts a sale.

This is where a specialist such as TokenMinds can help. Rob and his team guide companies on whether a token sale is suitable for them and, if it is, on how to prepare and run the sale correctly.

What Results Have Companies Seen?

Several companies have already raised capital this way with the support of Rob and his team. TokenMinds has published five of these cases, and together the companies raised more than $25 million.

The most recent case is SpaceWay, the token of an astronaut training company in Houston. In July 2026, it raised more than $350,000 through two sales, and both sales sold out. This amount was 19.7 times the fee that the company paid to TokenMinds.

Companies from other industries have used the same method. GoMeat, a US delivery marketplace for halal and kosher stores, raised $2.02 million in 2021, which was 61.2 times its fee. In the same year, MMAON, a sports technology company from Slovenia, raised $800,000. LOL Token, the education group from Thailand mentioned earlier, raised $3 million.

Another example is Remme, a cybersecurity company from Ukraine. It raised $19 million and reached its target within 24 hours after the sale opened.

These figures come from TokenMinds' own case studies, and the older sales took place in different market conditions. As the company states on those pages, past results do not guarantee future results.

Although these five companies work in different industries, their sales have several things in common. Rob often describes these common points when he speaks.

What Has He Learned in 9 Years of Token Raises?

After nine years, Rob's main lesson is that a token sale succeeds only when a real business stands behind it. On the What The Tech? podcast, he said that an established company with real revenue already has the credibility that crypto investors look for. That is why TokenMinds works with established companies from outside the crypto industry.

The second lesson is that the token must have a real use for the people who buy it. For this reason, TokenMinds starts each project with the design of the token and its purpose.

Rob has also learned that investors do not arrive automatically, even when the company is strong. This is the reason his team first tests the interest of verified crypto investors, before a company pays for a full campaign.

The final lesson is that the work does not end when the sale closes, because people continue to buy and sell the token afterwards. As a result, TokenMinds continues to support its clients after the sale, with ongoing marketing and trading support.

For founders who want to raise capital and keep their equity, these four lessons are a practical place to start. A company can check each of them before it spends any money, and this is also where Rob and his team begin with every new client.

Raise Capital Through a Token Sale With TokenMinds

TokenMinds has managed token launches since 2017 and acts as the Web3 department for companies that do not have their own crypto team. Rob and his team take care of the full process. It starts with the technical foundation, such as token design, the whitepaper, smart contract development and the security audit. It continues through marketing and promotion, which includes investor outreach, PR, community building and campaigns with key opinion leaders (KOLs).
