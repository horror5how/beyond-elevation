When to Hire a Fractional CFO: 5 Signals You Have Outgrown Your Accountant

Hayat Amin · Updated 2026-09-14

Knowing when to hire a fractional CFO separates a controlled fundraise from a fire drill. Here are five signals that your company has outgrown its accountant and needs a CFO-grade finance function.

Eighty-two per cent of failed startups cite cash-flow problems as a contributing factor, according to a 2023 CB Insights post-mortem analysis. The pattern is always the same: by the time the founder realises the numbers are wrong, the damage is already months old. Hiring a fractional CFO early is what separates a controlled fundraise from a fire drill.

Hayat Amin argues that founders almost always ask the question too late. "The right moment to bring in a fractional CFO is the moment you stop understanding your own numbers, and that moment arrived three months before you noticed," Amin says. Most companies cross that line somewhere between £500K and £2M in annual revenue, long before a CFO on the payroll makes economic sense.

Revenue size does not decide it. Five observable breakdowns in your finance function do. If three or more apply, you are already behind.

When nobody owns the financial model, it is time to hire a fractional CFO

You should hire a fractional CFO the moment your financial model has no owner, when your accountant files the taxes, bookkeeps the ledger, and produces statutory reports, but nobody is building the forward-looking model that drives decisions. This is the clearest signal that you have outgrown your current finance setup.

Accountants are trained to look backward. They reconcile what happened. A fractional CFO looks forward: runway projections, scenario modelling, pricing impact analysis.

The distinction matters because every strategic decision (hiring, expansion, pricing) depends on a model no accountant was trained to build. If your board asks "what happens to cash if we delay the next raise by six months?" and nobody in the room can answer in under sixty seconds, you have the gap.

How does a slow month-end close signal you need a fractional CFO?

A month-end close that stretches past five working days signals a finance function running without process design, exactly the gap a fractional CFO fills on day one. Beyond Elevation's operators routinely compress a 15-day close to under 48 hours within the first quarter of engagement.

Your team lacks a close calendar, a reconciliation checklist, and automated accruals. Those are CFO-level process decisions, not bookkeeper tasks.

Hayat Amin's Two-Day Close Playbook starts with one question: which five journal entries consume 80 per cent of your close time? In every engagement, the answer is the same: intercompany, deferred revenue, prepayments, accrued expenses, and payroll. Automate those five, and the close drops from weeks to days. (Read the full playbook.)

Why is fundraise preparation a trigger to hire a fractional CFO?

A fractional CFO is essential when you are approaching a fundraise and nobody on your team can translate the general ledger into an investor-grade financial narrative. Investors reject more decks for sloppy unit economics than for weak product. The numbers are the first filter that decides whether they read on.

Companies with patents are 10.2x more likely to secure early-stage funding, according to a 2024 WIPO study. The same multiplier logic applies to finance readiness: a clean data room, a three-year model, and a unit-economics page that withstands diligence multiply your odds in exactly the same way.

If your accountant is producing the investor deck, stop. Accountants produce compliance documents. A fractional CFO produces a fundraising instrument, a model that tells the investor exactly what their money buys and when it compounds.

What does missing a cash flow forecast tell you about when to hire a fractional CFO?

Any company past £1 million in annual revenue operating without a 13-week rolling cash flow forecast has already missed the moment to hire a fractional CFO. Cash flow is not profit. Profitable companies die from cash timing every quarter.

Hayat Amin reminds founders that cash visibility is the metric that separates companies that choose when to raise from companies that are forced to. "A rolling forecast is a 90-minute build for an experienced CFO and a six-month guessing game for everyone else," Amin says.

A fractional CFO installs this forecast in week one. It covers receipts, disbursements, payroll, tax obligations, and debt service, and it updates automatically from your accounting system. Without it, you are flying blind with real money.

Are you making strategic decisions without unit economics?

If your leadership team debates a new hire, a price change, or a market expansion without referencing customer acquisition cost, lifetime value, or gross margin by product line, the finance function has failed to deliver what the business needs. A fractional CFO builds the unit-economics layer that turns gut-feel decisions into ones you can defend with numbers.

This is where Hayat Amin's Finance Function Readiness Test draws the sharpest line. The test scores a company across seven axes: model ownership, close speed, cash visibility, unit economics, board reporting, fundraise readiness, and compliance posture. Companies that score below four out of seven are spending money they do not have, making decisions they cannot justify, and building plans they cannot defend to an investor.

Beyond Elevation runs this diagnostic in the first week of every fractional CFO engagement. The score determines the 90-day sprint: what gets fixed first, what gets automated, and what gets reported to the board.

What does a fractional CFO deliver in the first 90 days?

A fractional CFO installs a functioning finance operation within the first 90 days. Week one is the diagnostic. Weeks two through four rebuild the close process, install the cash forecast, and map unit economics. Months two and three build the board pack, the investor model, and the reporting cadence.

The difference between a fractional CFO and a consultant is delivery. A consultant hands you a deck. A fractional CFO hands you a finance function that runs after they leave. (What exactly is a fractional CFO?)

At the end of 90 days, the founder should be able to answer every board question from a single dashboard, close the books in under 48 hours, and walk into a fundraise with a model that has already been stress-tested.

How much does a fractional CFO cost compared to hiring one on the payroll?

A fractional CFO costs between £3,000 and £8,000 per month for one to two days per week, roughly a quarter of a full-time CFO salary of £150,000 to £250,000, before equity, benefits, and employer NI. The economics are clear: until your finance function generates enough complexity to fill five days a week, a fractional hire delivers the same output at a fraction of the cost. (Full pricing breakdown for 2026.)

Hayat Amin puts it bluntly: "A CFO on the payroll at a £2M-revenue company spends three days a week justifying their existence. A fractional CFO spends two days a week building the function and zero days justifying anything."

The real question is whether you can afford the decisions you are making without a fractional CFO.

What Should You Do Next?

Score yourself against the five signals above. If three or more apply, your company has outgrown its accountant, and the right move is a fractional CFO, not a bigger accounting team, a controller, or another spreadsheet.

FAQ

When is a company too small for a fractional CFO?

Below £300K in annual revenue, a good bookkeeper and a quarterly accountant review are enough. Between £300K and £1M, a fractional CFO on a monthly retainer of four to eight hours covers the gap. Above £1M, you need at least one dedicated day per week.

What is the difference between a fractional CFO and an accountant working reduced hours?

An accountant records and reports what happened. A fractional CFO builds the financial model, cash forecast, and board reporting that drive what happens next. They are complementary roles, not substitutes. (Full comparison here.)

How long does a typical fractional CFO engagement last?

Most engagements run 12 to 18 months. The first 90 days build the function. Months four through twelve embed it into the company's operating rhythm. After that, the company either brings the role onto the payroll or continues on a reduced retainer.

Can a fractional CFO help with fundraising?

Yes. Fundraise preparation, building the model, the data room, and the unit-economics narrative, is one of the top three reasons companies hire a fractional CFO. A well-prepared CFO-grade data room cuts diligence time in half and reduces the chance of a down-round renegotiation.

Does a fractional CFO replace my accountant?

No. Your accountant stays. A fractional CFO sits above the accountant, setting the chart of accounts, designing the close process, and building the forward-looking models. The accountant feeds the data. The CFO turns it into decisions.
