The Year-End CFO Checklist Most Companies Start Two Months Too Late

Hayat Amin · Updated 2026-10-27

The year-end CFO checklist determines whether Q1 starts clean or buried in restatements. Here are the 15 numbers to lock before December 31, and why October is already late.

The year-end CFO checklist runs in October, not December. Companies that start year-end close in December spend three times longer reconciling the same numbers as companies that start in October, according to a 2025 Deloitte CFO Signals survey. The difference is architecture, not discipline.

Hayat Amin argues that year-end close is not a calendar event, it is a system design problem. "If your finance function cannot produce clean numbers on demand, December just exposes what was broken in March," Amin says. The operators who close in two days do not work harder in Q4. They build the close into the operating rhythm all year.

This year-end CFO checklist covers the 15 numbers that must be locked before January 1. Skip any of them and Q1 starts with a restatement, a delayed audit, or a board meeting that goes sideways.

What should a year-end CFO checklist cover?

A year-end CFO checklist covers three layers: backward-looking reconciliation (did you record what happened?), current-period accuracy (are the closing entries right?), and forward-looking readiness (is the 2027 budget locked?). Most companies only do the first layer. That is why January feels like a second close.

The 15 items fall into five blocks: revenue recognition, balance sheet reconciliation, accruals and provisions, tax and compliance, and forward planning. Each one produces a specific number that either matches reality or does not. There is no "roughly correct" in year-end close.

Beyond Elevation runs a year-end CFO checklist with every fractional CFO engagement starting no later than October 15. The reason: any number that does not reconcile in October can be investigated and fixed before the auditors arrive. A number that does not reconcile in January gets disclosed as an adjustment, and adjustments destroy board confidence.

Which revenue recognition errors surface during year-end close?

Revenue recognition errors are the most common year-end restatement trigger, responsible for 39% of all financial restatements according to Audit Analytics. The three that surface most often: multi-element arrangements recognised in the wrong period, deferred revenue balances that do not match contract schedules, and recognition tied to milestones applied inconsistently across projects.

For any company using ASC 606 or IFRS 15, the year-end CFO checklist requires a contract-by-contract review of performance obligations. If a contract spans the year boundary, the CFO must verify that revenue allocated to each performance obligation matches the standalone selling price. One misallocated contract misstates Q4, and it cascades into Q1 comparatives.

Hayat Amin's rule for year-end revenue review is direct: "Pull every contract above 5% of ARR. Match the performance obligation schedule to the cash received schedule. If they diverge by more than one period, you have a recognition error. Fix it before the auditors find it."

How do you reconcile the balance sheet before year-end?

Balance sheet reconciliation before year-end means proving that every line item, cash, receivables, payables, fixed assets, accrued liabilities, ties to a source document with a date and a counterparty. A balance that exists only in the ledger is an unresolved question, not a confirmed asset.

The line items that fail reconciliation most often are intercompany balances, prepaid expenses that were never amortised, and fixed asset registers that have not been updated since the last audit. In the first 100 days of a fractional CFO engagement, the balance sheet reconciliation almost always reveals at least one stale line item carrying six figures.

Start with cash. If your bank balance does not match your ledger balance on October 31, stop and fix that before touching anything else. Every other reconciliation depends on cash being right.

What accruals and provisions must a CFO lock before December?

Accruals and provisions that must be locked before December include unbilled revenue, accrued payroll and bonuses, warranty provisions, legal contingencies, and any earn-out or deferred consideration from prior acquisitions. Missing even one material accrual misstates operating profit and, depending on materiality, triggers a restatement.

The accrual that trips up growth-stage companies most often is unbilled revenue. If you delivered work in Q4 but have not invoiced the client, that revenue must still appear in the period it was earned. Conversely, if you invoiced in Q4 for work you will deliver in Q1, that is deferred revenue, not recognised revenue.

Hayat Amin reminds founders that accruals are what separates numbers that look right from numbers that hold up to audit. "An auditor does not care what you intend to bill. They care what the contract says, what was delivered, and whether the accrual matches both. If it does not, they adjust it, and the adjustment appears in the management letter your board reads."

Which tax and compliance deadlines hit before year-end?

Tax and compliance deadlines that hit before year-end include corporate tax provision estimates, R&D tax credit documentation, transfer pricing adjustments for multi-entity groups, and VAT or sales tax reconciliations. Missing a filing deadline costs a penalty, and it signals to auditors and investors that the finance function lacks control.

The R&D tax credit is the one most growth-stage companies leave on the table. If your engineering team built anything novel this year, those hours and costs qualify for a credit that directly reduces your tax liability. But the documentation must be assembled before year-end, retroactive claims are weaker and attract more scrutiny. A fractional CFO who has filed R&D credits before knows exactly which project logs, timesheets, and expense records to pull.

What forward-looking numbers should be set before January?

The forward-looking numbers that belong on every year-end CFO checklist are the 2027 operating budget, Q1 cash flow forecast, headcount plan with fully loaded costs, and capital expenditure schedule. These numbers plan 2027, and they set the baseline against which every board meeting and investor update that year will be measured.

A rolling forecast is better than a static annual budget, but even rolling forecasts need an annual anchor. The year-end budget is that anchor. Without it, the Q1 forecast has no base case to roll from.

The budget must be locked before December 15. Any later, and it collides with the close itself, the same people reconciling the books are the people building the budget. Hayat Amin's approach at Beyond Elevation is to separate the two entirely: budget is an October deliverable, close is a December deliverable. They never share a deadline.

Why does the year-end CFO checklist start in October?

The year-end CFO checklist starts in October because every remediation window shrinks by 50% per month after that. An error found in October has eight weeks to fix. The same error found in December has eight days, and costs the company either an audit qualification or an overtime bill that wipes out whatever the CFO saved by starting late.

Hayat Amin's Year-End Lock Sequence is a five-phase framework that moves the heavy work forward. Phase 1 (October) locks the balance sheet reconciliation. Phase 2 (early November) locks revenue recognition. Phase 3 (mid-November) locks accruals and provisions. Phase 4 (December 1-15) locks the forward budget. Phase 5 (December 16-31) is the final close, but by this point, the close is a formality because every number was already verified.

Companies that run an exit-ready finance function don't panic in Q4. They execute a sequence. The year-end CFO checklist starts the close before it's urgent, not after.

FAQ

How long should year-end close take?

A well-run finance function completes year-end close in five to seven business days after December 31. Companies using Hayat Amin's Year-End Lock Sequence routinely close in three days because the heavy reconciliation work is done in October and November, not January.

Can a fractional CFO handle year-end close?

A fractional CFO handles year-end close for dozens of companies every year. The advantage is pattern recognition, a fractional CFO who has closed 50 year-ends knows exactly which line items break and in what order. A controller closing their first year-end doesn't have that reference set.

What happens if you skip year-end reconciliation?

Skipping year-end reconciliation means starting Q1 with unresolved balances. These surface as audit adjustments, restated financials, or, in a fundraise or exit, due diligence findings that reduce your valuation. According to PwC, companies with unresolved reconciliation items at year-end receive materially lower valuations in M&A due diligence.

When should you hire a CFO for year-end close?

Hire a fractional CFO no later than October 1 if your company does not have a finance leader. A CFO engaged in October has time to assess the ledger, build the close plan, and execute it. A CFO engaged in December is doing triage, not planning.
