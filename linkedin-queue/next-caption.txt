Most companies plan to hire one "AI person" for 2027.

The problem is that's three jobs.

You need someone who decides WHAT to automate.

You need someone who builds the automations.

You need someone who audits whether they're actually working — and kills the ones that aren't.

When one person does all three, you get slow rollout, unclear ROI, and hidden cost overruns.

The companies winning split it.

Strategist. Operator. Auditor.

Three distinct roles. Not three headcounts.

All three can run fractionally for less than one mid-market full-time AI hire.
